# CTC — Ceylon Tobacco Company PLC — Research File

> **Sources:** Annual Report 2025 (200 pp) and the interim for the six months to 30 June 2026, both primary, read 4 Oct 2026. **New name: this is the first file, built from scratch. It is not part of the HAYL→DIPD→HAYC or ALUM→SINS→SERV→LIOC sequences.**
> **Position:** none.
> **One-line thesis:** A cash machine that hands out everything it earns (100% payout, Rs 29 Bn a year, no debt) but one whose revenue is *what is left after the State takes 69% of every rupee sold* — so the investment question is never "is the business good" (it is) but "what is the stream worth when its owner-in-practice is the Treasury, and when the market already prices it like a bond."
> **Three things this file establishes:**
> 1. **At Rs 1,803.25 (30-Jun) the stock fails the hurdle by a wide margin**: trailing P/E 11.5×, earnings yield 8.7% against 12.17%. It would take a hurdle of ≤ 8.65% for a zero-growth CTC to be fair at this price. **Floor 960 / second tranche 860 / ceiling 1,280.** The print is 88% above the floor and 41% above the ceiling.
> 2. **Earnings are flat, not growing**: EPS 147.64 → 158.24 → 155.54 (FY23–25); PBT grew +17% in two years but the corporate tax rate rose from 40% to 45% in 2025, and nothing in H1 2026 changes the shape (PAT +2.5%).
> 3. **One new quality flag**: H1 2026 operating cash flow was only **51% of PAT** (FY25: 106%). Most of it is a Rs 5.0 Bn drop in payables, so probably timing — but it is the first reading and it needs a second.

## 1. What the company actually is

Sri Lanka's legal cigarette manufacturer and marketer (Dunhill; the John Player family — Gold Leaf, Gold Pro, Gold; Capstan), an 84.13% subsidiary of British American Tobacco (via BAT International Holdings BV). Listed on the CSE, 187,323,751 shares, unchanged for years. Asset-light: PP&E Rs 6.6 Bn, capex Rs 0.8 Bn in FY25 and Rs 50 Mn in H1 2026, 252 permanent employees. No borrowings (the last BAT loan was repaid in 2025).

**The economics are a levy pass-through, not a normal P&L.** Of Rs 215.7 Bn of FY25 turnover, **Rs 149.1 Bn (69%) is government levies**: excise special provision tax Rs 112.0 Bn, VAT Rs 33.4 Bn, social security levy Rs 3.7 Bn. The company's "revenue" (Rs 66.6 Bn) is what is left. Add Rs 24.7 Bn of income tax and CTC paid **Rs 173.8 Bn to the Treasury in 2025 (~4% of all government tax revenue)** — about 81% of turnover. The shareholders' slice is Rs 29.1 Bn, 13.5% of turnover. Every investment judgement in this file is a judgement about how stable that slice is when two-thirds of the price is set by the Finance Ministry.

**Market structure (management estimates, 2025):** the combustible market is ~8.9 Bn sticks. **Legal cigarettes are only 22%** (~2.0 Bn); beedi 72% (~6.4 Bn, "grown nearly 100% since 2015"); smuggled cigarettes 6% (~0.5 Bn, a decline attributed to enforcement). Volume +1% in 2025; management says it retained ~69% of premium-tier volume (+10% on the prior year) after the 6% excise increase of January 2025.

**Moat (Layer 3a check B): claimed and partly evidenced, not quantified.** Evidence: the John Player family grew 5% in 2025 on price-laddering; Dunhill retention improved; the direct-store-service network grew ~66% (~24,000 partners); operating margin on turnover rose from 23% to 25%. Against that, **no legal-market-share figure is disclosed anywhere in these documents**, so durability is inferred from brands, retention and route-to-market, not from a defended share number. The deeper position is regulatory — a licensed, tax-compliant channel, backed by a Rs 500 Mn excise bank guarantee — and that is *also* the source of the main risk: the same regulator sets the price floor. Verdict: real brand and distribution strength; no checkable moat metric; not a basis for any quality premium.

**Ownership and float.** BAT 84.13%, **Philip Morris Brand SARL 8.32%** (a competitor's affiliate holds a stake; the filings count it as "public"), Employees' Provident Fund 1.43%, the rest widely held. Reported public holding is 15.87% (6,889 holders; Rs 53.6 Bn). Stripping the Philip Morris and EPF blocks, **the genuinely tradable float is ~6.1% of shares (~Rs 20.7 Bn)**. FY25 trading was thin: **2.7 Mn shares (1.4% of the register) in 15,966 trades**, roughly Rs 4 Bn of value over the year. The company complies via Option 1 of Listing Rule 7.13.1 (float-adjusted market cap above Rs 10 Bn). No director holds a share.

## 3. Earnings quality — decomposing the stream

**What recurs (Layer 4 step 2).** There are no fair-value, associate or asset-sale lines: the only non-core item is net finance income (Rs 579 Mn in H1, ~2% of PBT). That is unusually clean. Two small one-offs sit in FY25:
- **Prior-year tax adjustment: Rs 443 Mn charged (Rs 2.37/share)** inside the tax line.
- **Reversal of obsolete-inventory provisions: Rs 563 Mn credited** inside other operating expenses (2024 had a Rs 524 Mn charge) — Rs 1.65/share after 45% tax.
They nearly offset: **FY25 EPS normalised = 156.25** (154.7 if a normal provision charge is assumed rather than zero). Trailing-twelve-month EPS is 157.40 (155.54 − 74.97 + 76.83), flattered by ~Rs 0.64 from lower H1 provisions. **Sustainable EPS adopted: Rs 156.00.**

**H1 2026 bridge (+Rs 1,112 Mn PBT, reconciles exactly):** revenue +1,507; other income +13; raw materials −672; employee benefits −436; depreciation −88; other operating expenses +743; finance income +45. So **the entire revenue gain is spent on higher costs, and the profit growth comes from a Rs 743 Mn fall in "other operating expenses"** (of which only ~Rs 217 Mn is lower provisions; the rest is unexplained in the interim). Costs overall rose 7.4% against revenue +4.8%. That drop will not repeat each half.

**Quarters are volatile; read the half.** Derived from the filing (half-year less Q2): Q1 2026 turnover **+14.1%**, revenue +13.6%, PAT +0.3%; Q2 2026 turnover **−4.9%**, revenue −2.4%, PAT +4.5%. Q1's tax rate was 48.5% against 42.6% a year earlier; Q2's was 43.5% against 46.9%. The swings look like shipment timing and tax-rate noise, not trend. Two Q2 items are unexplained: **employee benefit expense jumped to Rs 1,135 Mn (Q1: ~749 Mn; Q2 2025: 755 Mn)**, and raw materials were +37.5% year on year.

**Tax rate: structural, not temporary.** The 45% rate on sales profits (30% on interest income) applied from 2025; H1 2026's 45.9% confirms it. No sign of reversal. Government take is also stable: levies were 68.8% of turnover in H1 2026 vs 69.1%, so no excise change shows in these numbers. The interim's "Rs 41.8 Bn government revenue" is Q2 levies plus Q2 income tax (35,870 + 5,930), and "+4% for the half" is the six-month figure (84,097 vs 81,126, +3.7%); the narrative mixes quarter and half, so I rely on the statements.

## 4. Cash, balance sheet and dividends

**OCF vs PAT (Layer 3a check A):** FY25 Rs 31.0 Bn vs Rs 29.1 Bn (**1.06×**, FY24 1.06×) — clean. **H1 2026: Rs 7.3 Bn vs Rs 14.4 Bn (0.51×; H1 2025 0.83×).** The drivers: payables fell Rs 5.0 Bn (vs −1.1 Bn a year earlier) and cash tax paid was Rs 14.4 Bn (income tax 12.76 + withholding 1.65) against a Rs 12.2 Bn book charge. One period, not a trend, and the payables line is mostly government levies and accruals whose timing moves around year-end (Dec-25 payables Rs 25.6 Bn, of which levies 13.4, accruals 8.2, related parties 1.9) — so I do not name it as a risk yet. **It becomes one if the full-year FY26 ratio is below ~0.8×.**

**Balance sheet, 30 June 2026 (Rs Mn):** cash and equivalents 25,310 (31,367 at Dec-25); inventories 6,159; receivables 6,323; current liabilities 33,159 (payables 20,629, income tax 11,831, unclaimed dividends 612); equity 10,812; deferred tax 1,070. No borrowings; leases Rs 91 Mn. **The cash is a timing float, not surplus:** after trade payables, income tax and unclaimed dividends the net position is about −Rs 7.8 Bn. This is a business that distributes cash as it receives it, with the Treasury and suppliers funding the gap — fine while the pass-through works, brittle if a large levy payment or tax assessment arrives out of sequence. Current ratio 1.14×.

**Dividends: the payout is the product.** FY25: Rs 110.00 of interim dividends plus Rs 28.00 (Rs 5.2 Bn, declared after year-end) plus a proposed final of Rs 17.54 (Rs 3.3 Bn) = **Rs 155.54 per share, 100% of EPS**. In H1 2026 Rs 14.9 Bn (Rs 79.54/share) went out, which implies a first interim of ~Rs 34.00 (derived by subtraction, not stated in the filing). **A second interim of Rs 41.00 (Rs 7.7 Bn) was recommended for payment on 14 Sep 2026, identical to last year's second interim.** Equity fell from Rs 11.0 Bn to Rs 10.8 Bn (profit + OCI Rs 14.7 Bn, dividends Rs 14.9 Bn), so book value per share is Rs 57.72 and P/B is 31× — book is not an anchor for this stock.

## 6. Governance and filings

Governance runs on BAT group standards: KPMG as auditor, a related-party review committee, nil director shareholdings, a Top Employer certification (sixth year). **The CEO appears to have changed between the two documents.** Fariyha Subhani is the MD & CEO throughout the Annual Report (an executive director since 27 May 2025; the report is dated April 2026); the interim, signed 13 August 2026, is signed by Sarmad Abbasi as Managing Director/CEO, and its director list no longer includes her. Neither filing says why or when (homework). **Filing quality is good but not clean:** the Annual Report's highlights table prints net assets of "110,475" (2025) and "111,367" (2024), a ten-fold misprint (balance sheet: 11,047 and 11,137); net assets per share appears as 60.00/59.00 in one table and 58.97/58.98 in others; dividend per share is 156.34 (declared in the year) in the directors' report and 155.54 (attributable to the year) in the highlights, both correct. I used the statements, not the summaries.

## 7. Valuation (Layer 4)

**Hurdle:** long T-bond (2037A) secondary-market midpoint **12.17%** (CBSL, 25-Sep-2026; provisional, no primary auction since 11-Sep), neutral P/E = 1 ÷ 0.1217 = **8.22×**.

**Sustainable EPS: Rs 156.00** (§3: FY25 normalised 156.25; trailing 157.40; the H1 cost gains and provision benefit are not carried forward). No market-linked items to haircut.

**Floor multiple — every named risk is a listed subtraction from the 8.22× neutral:**

| Haircut | Size | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Excise and corporate-tax policy (risk 1) | −10% | Largest single exposure; one Budget can move EPS more than a year of operations |
| Illicit and beedi substitution (risk 2) | −6% | Legal market is 22% of sticks and competes against much cheaper untaxed products |
| Buyer-base and exit liquidity (risk 3) | −6% | ~6% tradable float; 1.4% turnover; excluded from many mandates |
| Parent and related-party leakage (risk 4) | −3% | ~Rs 2.9 Bn/yr cost-plus flows to the parent group |
| **Total** | **−25%** | Floor multiple 8.22 × 0.75 = **6.16×** |

**Floor = 156.00 × 6.16 = Rs 961 → rounded down to Rs 960.** No quality premium is applied: net cash, a 100% payout record and a 1.06× cash-conversion history are reasons the haircut is *not larger* (AAIC's discount was ~41%, LIOC's ~27%), and the moat is unquantified (§1). **The "wrong-but-still-fine" test:** if EPS falls 25% to ~117, the earnings yield at 960 is still **12.2%, equal to the hurdle** — a full excise-shock-sized hit leaves the floor price paying the bond rate.
**Second tranche = floor × 0.90 = 864 → Rs 860.**
**Ceiling = 156.00 ÷ 0.1217 = Rs 1,282 → rounded down to Rs 1,280** (8.22× — the price at which a zero-growth stream yields the hurdle). **No growth stretch:** EPS has been flat for two years and revenue growth is mostly excise pass-through, which the system treats as narrative, not contracted arithmetic.

**Cross-checks.**
- **Dividend yield (second anchor):** at the floor 16.3% (on DPS ≈ EPS = 156); at the ceiling 12.2%; **at Rs 1,803.25, 8.65%** — above UAL's ~6.7% and AAIC's ~5.5% (July, not re-checked), but still about 3.5 points under the hurdle, and unlike those two the dividend here is genuinely fully covered by cash earnings.
- **What the market is assuming (hurdle sensitivity of a zero-growth ceiling):** 12.17% → 1,282; 11% → 1,418; 10% → 1,560; 9% → 1,733; **8.65% → 1,803.** The stock is priced as if the relevant discount rate were the policy rate (8.75%) or the short end, not the long bond. That is the market's judgement, and the vault's method does not follow it.
- **Earnings-shock table (floor / ceiling / e/y at 1,803):** EPS 117 → 721 / 961 / 6.5%; 140 → 865 / 1,154 / 7.8%; **156 → 961 / 1,282 / 8.65%**; 157.4 → 970 / 1,293 / 8.73%. Even on the most flattering EPS the print is 39% above the ceiling.
- **Market's own memory (cited only after the ceiling was set, per Layer 4a):** the 2025 range was 1,200–1,735 and 2024's 955–1,415, so a ceiling of 1,280 is a level the stock traded at less than a year ago. The Q2 2026 range (1,710–1,849) sits entirely above it.
- **Book value:** P/B 31× — meaningless here, as for any company that pays out everything.

**Price context (the print is stale).** Last traded **Rs 1,803.25 on 30 June 2026** (quarter high 1,849, low 1,710; Rs 1,725 at Dec-25, +4.5% YTD; +23% on Rs 1,465 a year earlier). Market cap Rs 337.8 Bn. **The stock has since gone ex the Rs 41.00 dividend (paid 14 Sep) and the CSE closed at a six-month low on 30-Sep per the vault's market notes; I have no current CTC quote.** The ex-dividend adjustment alone is ~2.3% (Rs 41.00 on Rs 1,803). Trailing P/E at 30-Jun: 11.5×; earnings yield 8.7% (TTM) / 8.65% (adopted).

## 8. Verdict

**WATCHLIST — no position, nothing actionable.** The print is **88% above the floor and 41% above the ceiling**; the margin the hurdle requires has already been spent and then some. The business is genuinely excellent at generating cash; the problem is entirely the price, and the price has risen while earnings stood still.

**What would raise the numbers:** a full year of EPS clearly above ~165 (about +6%) on a stable 45% tax rate (a confirmed sustainable-EPS shift, not a pass-through); a measurable fall in beedi/illicit share with excise held at or below inflation; the long-bond primary auction printing materially below 12.17%; a disclosed legal-market-share series that evidences the moat (would justify a modest premium to neutral, per Layer 3a).
**What would lower them:** an excise increase above inflation or a further corporate-tax rise in the Budget; a cut in the payout ratio; FY26 OCF/PAT below ~0.8×; loss of premium-tier volume retention; the 12.17% hurdle drifting up at a primary auction; any plain-packaging or similar regulatory step.

**Portfolio check (Layer 5, for completeness; no order is near).** CTC shares no primary driver with JXG/JINS (rate-cycle via First Capital), JKH (retail/EV, tourism/oil, LKR loan) or LIOC (fuel margin and tax concessions); its driver is excise policy and illicit substitution, so it would not enter any existing correlated cluster. LIOC alone is already ~59% of invested capital, a single-name breach under the 50% ceiling: new capital to a non-cluster name would reduce that share, not add to it, but none of this is relevant while CTC trades far above its floor. Sizing note for if it ever arrives: ~6% tradable float and ~1.4% turnover mean orders need patient limits and small clips.
