## 1. What DIPD actually is

Public limited company, incorporated 1976, two reportable segments:

| Segment | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Hand Protection** | Industrial, general-purpose, and medical rubber gloves — manufacturing in Sri Lanka, Thailand, Italy (Icoguanti S.p.A), France, Poland |
| **Plantations** | Tea and rubber estate management — Kelani Valley Plantations PLC, Talawakelle Tea Estates PLC, Horana Plantations PLC (held via Hayleys Plantation Services) |

**Ownership:** Hayleys PLC 42.12% direct + Volanka (Pvt) Ltd 8.14% + Haycarb PLC 6.80% — all three Hayleys-linked, giving **effective group control north of 55%** (matches HAYL's own 55.78% figure). Public float 40.99%, 12,949 shareholders, float-adjusted market cap Rs. 12.12 bn as at 31/03/2026 — thin.

**Cross-note for the HAYL file:** DIPD is the Hand Protection segment HAYL's own segment table shows at Rs. 4.13 bn PBT — DIPD's Group PBT this period is Rs. 6.83 bn, meaning Plantations plus non-Hand-Protection items account for the gap. ## 4. The amalgamation — Company vs Group (the one genuine complication)

D P L Premier Gloves Ltd, a wholly-owned subsidiary, was legally amalgamated into the parent Dipped Products PLC effective 1 August 2025 (common-control transaction, pooling-of-interest method).

This is the same shape of distortion HAYL's file flagged for the Maldives disposal at the parent level — a real balance-sheet event that only bites one of the two financial statements. Because DPL Premier Gloves was already consolidated inside the **Group** numbers as a subsidiary before the merger, folding it into the parent legally doesn't change Group economics — it only restates the **Company** (standalone) statements, where revenue jumped 76% (16.5bn vs 9.4bn) purely as a consolidation artefact.

**Practical consequence: value off the Group numbers, not the Company numbers.** Company EPS (Rs. 3.38) looks worse than Group EPS (Rs. 5.88) for a structural reason that has nothing to do with earning power — using it would understate the business by pretending the minority-interest-bearing subsidiaries don't exist. Flagged explicitly because it's the easiest place to make an error on this file.

## 5. Balance sheet and leverage — the risk that actually matters here

| Group, Rs.'000 | FY26 | FY25 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| LT interest-bearing loans | 4,811,958 | 1,638,220 | +193.7% |
| ST loans + bank overdrafts | 8,200,387 | 6,351,569 | +29.1% |
| ST portion of LT loans | 1,887,942 | 1,765,875 | +6.9% |
| **Total interest-bearing debt** | **14,900,287** | **9,755,664** | **+52.7%** |
| Total equity | 43,512,213 | 39,631,220 | +9.8% |
| Total liabilities | 42,499,807 | 34,984,460 | +21.5% |
| PP&E | 35,656,551 | 30,106,785 | +18.4% |

Interest-bearing debt is up **53% in one year**, funding a PP&E build (+18%) and a new non-current financial-assets position (442m → 2.84bn — DIPD's own treasury parking cash in bonds at these yields, which explains the 61% jump in finance income; same "institutions chasing yield" behaviour the macro tracker already has flagged for the broader market).

Finance costs only rose 3%, because most of this debt was drawn late in the year. **The named risk is not today's leverage ratio — it's that FY27 will carry a full year of interest on a debt base that FY26 barely felt.** This is the DIPD-specific parallel to HAYL's own leverage flag (debt/equity 1.42× → 1.70× at the Hayleys Group level) — the subsidiary is re-levering at the same time as the parent.

## 6. Valuation — hurdle method

**Hurdle:** long T-bond (01 July 2037), 12.58% at the 13 July 2026 auction, reconfirmed at the 16 July re-tap. Neutral P/E = 1 ÷ 0.1258 ≈ **7.9×**.

**Sustainable EPS — the weak point in this file, stated plainly:** I only have one interim report, not the multi-year series HAYL's file used (five years) or the annual reports the SUN/JKH files drew on. This is a two-data-point estimate, not a mid-cycle average across a real commodity cycle, and Plantations-heavy earnings can swing wider than two years will show. Treat the number below as provisional pending the annual report.

Decomposing what's available:
- **FY25 reported EPS 6.47** — strip a Rs. 370,271k non-recurring gain on bargain purchase on acquisition (fully non-cash, appears untaxed) and a Rs. 118,750k bad-debt-provision reversal (tax-effected at the 26.4% FY25 rate → Rs. 87,270k after tax). **Adjusted FY25 PAT ≈ 3,417,917k → Adjusted EPS ≈ 5.71.**
- **FY26 reported EPS 5.88** — no comparable one-off identified; other income actually fell 26%, consistent with a cleaner year. Kept as-is.
- **Two-point average ≈ Rs. 5.80 — sustainable EPS.**

**Multiple — cut for named risks:** the debt-lag risk (§5) and the Plantations cyclicality/margin drag (§3) are two separate, live risks, which is one more than HAYL's file used to justify its 6.3× (HAYL: single dominant risk cluster, but a 74-year AAA(lka)-rated diversified parent). DIPD is a subsidiary carrying group-level re-leveraging *and* a shrinking, margin-compressing segment. **Floor multiple: 6.2×.**

**Floor** = 5.80 × 6.2 ≈ Rs. 35.9, rounded down → **Rs. 36.**
**Ceiling** = 5.80 × 7.9 (hurdle-neutral) ≈ Rs. 45.8, rounded down → **Rs. 45–46.**

## 8. Verdict

**WATCHLIST — floor 36, ceiling 45–46, confirmed market price ~56.50.** No position. DIPD trades **~23% above the ceiling and ~57% above the floor** — priced above where this framework will pay for it, the same shape of mispricing already found on its own parent, HAYL (which sits ~50–65% above its ceiling). Two Hayleys-group names in a row running ahead of the hurdle-rate math is a pattern worth carrying into the HAYC file next: either the market is systematically under-pricing the 12.6% hurdle for this group right now, or these two floors need a second look — and those are different hypotheses that the next file should try to distinguish, not just confirm.

The user-supplied chart (DIPD vs ASI, since Oct 2025) shows DIPD underperforming the index by ~11% and in a sustained downtrend since a spring peak — directionally consistent with a stock working its way back down toward, not yet reaching, a ceiling it overshot. Nothing here is a timing call; the floor is the only actionable number, and price is currently ~57% above it.

**What would raise the ceiling:** a real multi-year EPS series showing Rs. 5.80 as trough-not-mid-cycle; FY27 finance costs *not* catching up to the debt (i.e., DIPD termed the debt out cheaply).
**What would lower the floor:** FY27 finance costs jumping as the debt-lag risk predicts; Plantations margin continuing to compress past FY26's 8.4%.
