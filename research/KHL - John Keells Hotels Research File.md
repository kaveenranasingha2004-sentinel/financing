## 1. What KHL actually is

Public limited company, incorporated 1979, listed on the CSE. Operates the **Cinnamon Hotels & Resorts** brand across two reportable geographic segments — Sri Lanka and Maldives. **Ownership: John Keells Holdings PLC holds 80.32%** (1,169,598,478 of 1,456,146,780 shares), unchanged year-on-year. Public float **19.24%** at FYE (down slightly from 19.68%), spread across 10,713 shareholders (up sharply from 8,120 — the FY26 price rally to 27.40 in September 2025 clearly drew retail attention before the stock gave most of it back by year-end). **Directors hold nil shares.**

**A disclosed mitigant worth recording plainly, and not present in most other files in this vault:** the AR's own liquidity-risk note states the Group "is also confident on the support which can be extended by its parent company in managing funding requirements should there be a need" — an explicit, disclosed backstop from JKH. This matters directly for the current-ratio risk flagged in Section 6.

## 3. The one-off, found and quantified — Cinnamon Citadel Kandy divestment

On **31 March 2026** (the last day of the fiscal year), the Group disposed of its 98.39% stake in Kandy Walk Inn Ltd (owner of Cinnamon Citadel Kandy) to Lavendish Leisure (Dedigama Group) for Rs. 2,777mn, as part of a stated **asset-light strategy**. **Gain on disposal at Group level: Rs. 1,139,210k**, booked entirely within Q4 FY25/26's "Other operating income" line (which jumped to Rs. 1,220,618k that quarter, from a typical Rs. 33–43k/quarter run-rate in Q1–Q3).

**A second, smaller item sits below EBIT and isn't captured by the company's own "Recurring" metric:** a Rs. 375,025k net fair-value gain on investment-property remeasurement (vs Rs. 74,597k in FY25) — non-cash, and lumpy year to year, though this type of gain does recur in some form annually rather than being a true one-time event.

**Adjusted (sustainable) PBT, stripping both:**
Reported PBT 2,945,809 − disposal gain 1,139,210 − FV gain 375,025 = **Adjusted PBT ≈ Rs. 1,431,574k**
Applying the year's own effective tax rate (33.8%): **Adjusted PAT ≈ Rs. 947,702k → Adjusted EPS ≈ Rs. 0.65** — roughly **half** the reported Rs. 1.33. FY25/26 was a genuine recovery year, but the headline EPS overstates the recurring earnings power behind it by close to 2×.

**A structural consequence the P&L strip alone doesn't capture:** Cinnamon Citadel Kandy is now **out of the portfolio entirely** going forward. It contributed real operating results for eleven months of FY25/26 (its FY25 net book value was Rs. 2,092,247k, roughly 7% of the Sri Lanka real-estate base) before being sold on the year's last day. FY26/27's Sri Lanka segment starts from a smaller base than FY25/26 — a second, independent reason not to carry FY25/26's Sri Lanka strength forward unadjusted.

## 5. The Q1 relapse — confirmed as (at least partly) seasonal, but not fully explained

The Annual Report's own quarterly table settles the question the earlier DRAFT left open:

| | Q1 (Jun) | Q2 (Sep) | Q3 (Dec) | Q4 (Mar) | Full Year |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 6,273,865 | 6,822,947 | 8,673,303 | 10,518,634 | 32,288,749 |
| EBIT | (181,244) | (13,512) | 1,361,210 | 3,974,385 | 5,140,839 |
| PBT | (861,458) | (675,481) | 1,093,781 | 3,388,967 | 2,945,809 |
| EPS (Rs., standalone quarter) | (0.52) | (0.48) | 0.52 | 1.80 | 1.33 |

**Confirmed: Q1 and Q2 are structurally loss-making quarters, every year** (Apr–Sep is the low/shoulder season; Oct–Mar carries the entire year's profit). A single quarter read in isolation would badly mislead — the driver is tourism seasonality, a structural and repeating feature, not a one-off event.

**What this does and does not resolve about Q1 FY26/27:** Q1 FY26/27's PBT of −1,941,333 was 125% worse than Q1 FY25/26's −861,458. Seasonality explains the *existence* of a Q1 loss; it does **not** explain why *this* Q1's loss was so much larger than *last* year's. That YoY comparison remains a genuine, unresolved flag. **Q2 FY26/27 is the decisive test:** if it mirrors FY25/26's Q2 (a narrowing loss), the relapse was likely noise around a strong trend. If Q2 also deteriorates YoY, the recovery trend itself is in question. **This is the single biggest live risk to the position just opened** — Q2 (quarter to 30 Sep 2026) is now closed, so the interim is due imminently.

## 6. Balance sheet — a genuine, multi-year deleveraging trend (the strongest evidence in this file)

From the Annual Report's ten-year "Decade at a Glance" table:

| | FY23 | FY24 | FY25 | FY26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Debt/equity (excl. lease) | 79.00% | 71.60% | 67.24% | **51.14%** |
| Interest cover | 0.78× | 0.98× | 1.12× | **2.24×** |
| ROE | (1.06%) | (1.38%) | (0.71%) | **5.88%** |
| Pre-tax ROCE | 2.62% | 3.66% | 4.08% | **7.70%** |
| Current ratio | 0.32 | 0.25 | 0.29 | **0.40** |

**Four consecutive years of falling leverage and rising interest cover** — not a one-year story, and the most reliable finding in this file because it doesn't depend on how the FY25/26 one-off is treated. Net debt (excl. lease) fell 17.4% in FY25/26 alone, to Rs. 15,862,049k.

**The standing risk the trend doesn't fix: current ratio below 1.0× for at least four straight years**, improving (0.32 → 0.40) but still meaning current liabilities structurally exceed current assets — a bank-overdraft-dependent short-term funding shape. The one genuine mitigant is Section 1's disclosed parent-support backstop from JKH — real, but a mitigant is not the same as the risk being resolved.

**Debt maturity profile (Group, contractual undiscounted, at 31 March 2026):** Rs. 18,575,123k falls due within 1 year (down from Rs. 22,979,333k) — more than half of that (Rs. 10,587,354k) is bank overdrafts.

**Contingent liabilities are small and shrinking** — Rs. 37.1mn (down from Rs. 69.4mn), tax-assessment-related. A clean line.

### 6a. Layer 3a addendum — cash conversion and moat

**OCF vs PAT:** *not verified in this file.* The recovered text carries reported PAT (1,933,944) and adjusted PAT (~947,702) but not the cash-flow statement's operating cash flow line. Cash-conversion is therefore an **open item** (Homework 5), not a clean pass. Partial indirect evidence only: net debt fell Rs. 3.35bn in FY25/26 while the Kandy sale brought in Rs. 2,777mn of proceeds — i.e., a large part of the deleveraging may be asset-sale-funded rather than operating-cash-funded. That is a hypothesis to test, not a finding.

**Moat claim vs evidence:** *Claim* — the Cinnamon brand and the JKH group give pricing power and a captive customer base. *Evidence for:* Maldives RevPAR +9% with revenue only +5.9% (rate-led, not volume-led); Beach Resorts RevPAR +19%; recurring EBIT +44.9%; disclosed parent funding backstop. *Evidence against:* pre-tax ROCE 7.70% and adjusted ROE ≈ 2.7% are both below the 12.17% hurdle; interest cover only 2.24×; four years of sub-1.0 current ratio; the stock's own mid-year round-trip. **Verdict: a brand that can raise room rates, but returns on capital have not yet shown a moat that earns its cost of capital.** The thesis rests on operating leverage plus deleveraging, not on a proven moat — consistent with the asset-anchor method rather than an earnings-yield one.

## 7. Valuation — asset-anchor method (Layer 4, Section 6: recovery-stage business)

**Earnings-yield is not the primary method here**, for the same reason as JKH: even on the *adjusted* EPS estimate (Rs. 0.65), the earnings-yield value (0.65 ÷ hurdle — ≈ Rs. 5.16 at the 12.6% used in August, ≈ Rs. 5.34 at 12.17%) sits far below both book and the market price — an artefact of a still-low absolute ROE rather than a signal that the stock is expensive. Book value carries the derivation, cross-checked against the recovery trend.

**NAV/share: Rs. 23.95** (31 March 2026, up 10.8% YoY).

**Discount to book — risk-by-risk:**
1. **ROE still well below the hurdle**, even after adjustment (≈2.7% adjusted vs 12.17% required) — book isn't yet earning its keep; it's a claim on the *trajectory*.
2. **Q1 FY26/27 YoY relapse, unresolved.**
3. **Current ratio persistently <1.0× for four straight years.**
4. **Kandy divestment shrinks the go-forward Sri Lanka base.**
5. **Thin float (19.24%).**

**Floor ≈ 0.65× book** = 23.95 × 0.65 ≈ **Rs. 15.55, rounded → Rs. 15.50.**
**Second tranche** = Floor × 0.90 ≈ **Rs. 14.00.**
**Ceiling ≈ 0.85× book** = 23.95 × 0.85 ≈ **Rs. 20.36, rounded → Rs. 20.00–20.50** — held below full book because KHL's adjusted ROE sits below JKH's own at the point JKH's ceiling was set, and the Q1 relapse is an open question JKH's file didn't carry.

**Hurdle re-check, 09 Oct 2026:** see Section 0 — required ROE at the floor ≈ 7.9%; no change to the three levels absent a trigger event.

## 8. Market price and verdict

31 March 2026 close: Rs. 16.20 (down 20% YoY), after a year ranging from a high of Rs. 27.40 (26-Sep-2025) to a low of Rs. 16.00 (31-Mar-2026) — a striking round-trip: the stock rallied hardest mid-year, then sold off into year-end even as Q3/Q4 delivered the strongest profits. Plausibly the market discounting the Q4 gain as non-recurring, plausibly a broader tourism de-rating, or both. Open, not resolved.

Price path since: 18.10 at 30-Jun-2026 (P/B 0.756×, quarterly range 16.00–18.70) → **15.00 on 09-Oct-2026** (P/B 0.626×). That is a ~17% fall from the 30-Jun print and a new print below the 16.00 level that held as the FY low. The file cannot explain the fall from primary sources — no filing since 30 June has been read. Candidate explanations to test, not asserted: market anticipation of a weak Q2 (consistent with the Q1 relapse), the tourism/Hormuz regime named in the concentration tracker, or flow in a 19%-float stock.

**Verdict: HOLD (first tranche, 100 shares @ ≈15.54).** The price is below the floor and above the second tranche; no trigger has fired; the floor stands. The honest framing: this is a staged-entry position in a recovery-stage asset play where the only hard evidence for the thesis (four years of deleveraging) is from filings that pre-date the price fall, and the one filing that post-dates it (Q1 FY26/27) was negative YoY. Position size is small (~6.4% of invested capital), which is the right size for that evidence.

**Actions by price:**
- **Above 15.50:** hold; no buys.
- **14.00 or lower:** second-tranche buy level (subject to the concentration check — the JKH+KHL cluster would still be well below 50%).
- **Below 14.00 without a Q2 explanation:** do not average down mechanically — re-read the trigger list first.
- **20.00–20.50:** ceiling; trim/exit zone.

**What would raise the ceiling:** Q2 FY26/27 confirming a narrowing loss rather than deepening; current ratio continuing past 0.40×; further deleveraging, ideally operating-cash-funded.
**What would lower the floor:** Q2 FY26/27 also deteriorating YoY; any drawdown on the JKH funding backstop; reversal of the debt/equity improvement; evidence that the FY26 deleveraging was mostly asset-sale-funded.
