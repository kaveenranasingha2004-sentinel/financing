# KHL — John Keells Hotels PLC — Research File

> **Sources:** Integrated Report 2025/26 (352pp, primary, year ended 31 March 2026) and Interim Condensed Financial Statements for Q1 FY26/27 (three months ended 30 June 2026, primary), both read 02 Aug 2026.
> **Position:** none. **This version replaces an earlier DRAFT** built from the Q1 FY26/27 interim alone, with no earnings history and no annual report — that file could only anchor on book value with a wide, low-confidence discount. The Annual Report closes the two biggest gaps that draft explicitly flagged as unresolved: (1) whether Apr–Jun is a genuine seasonal trough — **confirmed, yes**, via the AR's own quarterly table; (2) a real earnings series to work from — **now available**, ten years of it. The floor and ceiling below are revised upward from the earlier draft (12.50 / 17.00–17.50 → 15.50 / 20.00–20.50) on the strength of that evidence, not because the underlying business changed between sessions.

## 1. What KHL actually is

Public limited company, incorporated 1979, listed on the CSE. Operates the **Cinnamon Hotels & Resorts** brand across two reportable geographic segments — Sri Lanka and Maldives. **Ownership: John Keells Holdings PLC holds 80.32%** (1,169,598,478 of 1,456,146,780 shares), unchanged year-on-year. Public float **19.24%** at FYE (down slightly from 19.68%), spread across 10,713 shareholders (up sharply from 8,120 — the FY26 price rally to 27.40 in September 2025 clearly drew retail attention before the stock gave most of it back by year-end). **Directors hold nil shares.**

**A disclosed mitigant worth recording plainly, and not present in most other files in this vault:** the AR's own liquidity-risk note states the Group "is also confident on the support which can be extended by its parent company in managing funding requirements should there be a need" — an explicit, disclosed backstop from JKH. This matters directly for the current-ratio risk flagged in Section 5.

## 3. The one-off, found and quantified — Cinnamon Citadel Kandy divestment

On **31 March 2026** (the last day of the fiscal year), the Group disposed of its 98.39% stake in Kandy Walk Inn Ltd (owner of Cinnamon Citadel Kandy) to Lavendish Leisure (Dedigama Group) for Rs. 2,777mn, as part of a stated **asset-light strategy**. **Gain on disposal at Group level: Rs. 1,139,210k**, booked entirely within Q4 FY25/26's "Other operating income" line (which jumped to Rs. 1,220,618k that quarter, from a typical Rs. 33–43k/quarter run-rate in Q1–Q3).

**A second, smaller item sits below EBIT and isn't captured by the company's own "Recurring" metric:** a Rs. 375,025k net fair-value gain on investment-property remeasurement (vs Rs. 74,597k in FY25) — non-cash, and lumpy year to year, though this type of gain does recur in some form annually rather than being a true one-time event.

**Adjusted (sustainable) PBT, stripping both:**
Reported PBT 2,945,809 − disposal gain 1,139,210 − FV gain 375,025 = **Adjusted PBT ≈ Rs. 1,431,574k**
Applying the year's own effective tax rate (33.8%): **Adjusted PAT ≈ Rs. 947,702k → Adjusted EPS ≈ Rs. 0.65** — roughly **half** the reported Rs. 1.33. FY25/26 was a genuine recovery year, but the headline EPS overstates the recurring earnings power behind it by close to 2×.

**A structural consequence the P&L strip alone doesn't capture:** Cinnamon Citadel Kandy is now **out of the portfolio entirely** going forward. It contributed real operating results for eleven months of FY25/26 (its FY25 net book value was Rs. 2,092,247k, roughly 7% of the Sri Lanka real-estate base) before being sold on the year's last day. FY26/27's Sri Lanka segment starts from a smaller base than FY25/26 — this is a second, independent reason not to simply carry FY25/26's Sri Lanka strength forward unadjusted, separate from and additional to stripping the one-off gain itself.

## 4. Segment picture — both geographies genuinely improved, one much more cleanly than the other

| Segment | FY25/26 Revenue | FY24/25 Revenue | Rev Δ | FY25/26 PBT | FY24/25 PBT | PBT Δ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sri Lanka | 13,067,257 | 11,163,836 | +17.1% | 2,647,161 | 472,238 | +460% (incl. one-off) |
| Maldives | 19,221,492 | 18,154,391 | +5.9% | 298,648 | (170,231) | swing to profit |

**Maldives is the cleaner story:** revenue +5.9%, RevPAR +9%, PBT swinging from a Rs. 170mn loss to a Rs. 299mn profit — **no material one-off identified in this segment's own numbers.** This is a genuine operating turnaround in the segment that carries almost all of the Group's lease liabilities (Rs. 16,208,208k of the Group's Rs. 16,727,308k — 97%), i.e., the harder, more capital-intensive half of the business is the one improving on its own merits.

**Sri Lanka's headline strength is real but partly borrowed from the one-off:** stripping the Rs. 1,139,210k disposal gain from Sri Lanka's segment PBT gives an adjusted ≈ Rs. 1,507,951k — still a genuine +219% improvement on FY25's Rs. 472,238k (RevPAR +4.2%, Beach Resorts +19% RevPAR as travellers shifted coastal following Cyclone Ditwah's impact on central Sri Lanka), but a meaningfully smaller number than the Rs. 2,647,161k headline suggests, and one that will lose the Kandy hotel's ongoing contribution from FY26/27 onward.

## 5. The Maldives-Q1 relapse — now confirmed as (at least partly) seasonal, but not fully explained

The Annual Report's own quarterly table settles the question this file's earlier DRAFT explicitly left open:

| | Q1 (Jun) | Q2 (Sep) | Q3 (Dec) | Q4 (Mar) | Full Year |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 6,273,865 | 6,822,947 | 8,673,303 | 10,518,634 | 32,288,749 |
| EBIT | (181,244) | (13,512) | 1,361,210 | 3,974,385 | 5,140,839 |
| PBT | (861,458) | (675,481) | 1,093,781 | 3,388,967 | 2,945,809 |
| EPS (Rs., standalone quarter) | (0.52) | (0.48) | 0.52 | 1.80 | 1.33 |

**Confirmed: Q1 and Q2 are structurally loss-making quarters, every year, by design of the business's seasonality** (Apr–Sep is the low/shoulder season for Sri Lanka–Maldives hospitality; Oct–Mar carries the entire year's profit, consistent with the Northern Hemisphere winter travel season). This is the same category of finding as ALUM's Q4-loaded fire-insurance pattern — a single quarter read in isolation would badly mislead — except here the driver is tourism seasonality, a structural and repeating feature, not a one-off event.

**What this does and does not resolve about Q1 FY26/27:** the earlier draft flagged that Q1 FY26/27's loss (PBT −1,941,333) was 125% worse than Q1 FY25/26's (−861,458). The seasonality confirmation means the *existence* of a Q1 loss is normal — it does **not** explain why *this* Q1's loss was so much larger than *last* year's Q1, in a year when the full-year trend (Q2 FY25/26 onward) was strongly positive. That YoY comparison remains a genuine, unresolved flag. **Q2 FY26/27 is now the decisive test:** if it mirrors FY25/26's Q2 (a narrowing loss, on the way to Q3/Q4 profitability), the relapse was likely noise around a strong trend. If Q2 FY26/27 also deteriorates YoY, the recovery trend itself is in question.

## 6. Balance sheet — a genuine, multi-year deleveraging trend (the strongest evidence in this file)

From the Annual Report's own ten-year "Decade at a Glance" table:

| | FY23 | FY24 | FY25 | FY26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Debt/equity (excl. lease) | 79.00% | 71.60% | 67.24% | **51.14%** |
| Interest cover | 0.78× | 0.98× | 1.12× | **2.24×** |
| ROE | (1.06%) | (1.38%) | (0.71%) | **5.88%** |
| Pre-tax ROCE | 2.62% | 3.66% | 4.08% | **7.70%** |
| Current ratio | 0.32 | 0.25 | 0.29 | **0.40** |

**Four consecutive years of falling leverage and rising interest cover** — this is not a one-year story, and it is the single most reliable finding in this file, because it doesn't depend on how the FY25/26 one-off is treated. Net debt (excl. lease) fell 17.4% in FY25/26 alone, to Rs. 15,862,049k. **This trend is real, monotonic, and the best evidence available that the business is genuinely, structurally recovering — not just posting one good year on the back of an asset sale.**

**The standing risk that the trend doesn't fix: current ratio has stayed below 1.0× for at least four straight years**, improving (0.32 → 0.40) but still meaning current liabilities structurally exceed current assets — the same bank-overdraft-dependent short-term funding shape flagged in the earlier draft, now confirmed as a multi-year pattern rather than a single snapshot. The one genuine mitigant is Section 1's disclosed parent-support backstop from JKH — real, but a mitigant is not the same as the risk being resolved.

**Debt maturity profile (Group, contractual undiscounted, at 31 March 2026):** Rs. 18,575,123k falls due within 1 year (down from Rs. 22,979,333k the year before) — more than half of that (Rs. 10,587,354k) is bank overdrafts. The improving trend is real; the overdraft-heavy composition of it is not yet resolved.

**Contingent liabilities are genuinely small and shrinking** — Rs. 37.1mn (down from Rs. 69.4mn), tax-assessment-related. This is a clean line, unlike ALUM's live Rs. 85.1mn Court of Appeal dispute — worth stating plainly since it removes one category of risk this file would otherwise need to price in.

## 7. Valuation — asset-anchor method (Layer 4, Section 6: recovery-stage business)

**Earnings-yield is still not the primary method here**, for the same reason as JKH: even on the *adjusted* EPS estimate (Rs. 0.65), the resulting earnings yield (0.65 ÷ hurdle 12.6% ≈ Rs. 5.16 fair value) sits far below both book value and the market price — an artefact of a still-low absolute ROE (adjusted ROE ≈ 2.7%, reported 5.88%) rather than a signal that the stock is expensive. Per the Layer 4 method's own guidance for depressed/recovery businesses, book value carries the derivation, cross-checked against the recovery trend rather than a static earnings multiple — the same logic already applied to JKH itself.

**NAV/share: Rs. 23.95** (31 March 2026, up 10.8% YoY).

**Discount to book — risk-by-risk:**
1. **ROE still well below the hurdle**, even after adjustment (≈2.7% adjusted vs 12.6% required) — book value isn't yet earning its keep on a pure return basis; it's a claim on the *trajectory*, not the current run-rate.
2. **Q1 FY26/27 YoY relapse, unresolved** — the seasonality question is settled, the YoY deterioration is not.
3. **Current ratio persistently <1.0× for four straight years** — a standing structural liquidity risk, partially but not fully offset by disclosed parent support.
4. **Kandy divestment shrinks the go-forward Sri Lanka base** — FY25/26's segment strength won't fully repeat even before adjusting for the one-off gain itself.
5. **Thin float (19.24%)** — same order of magnitude as every other majority-controlled subsidiary in this vault.

Five named risks — fewer and less severe in aggregate than the six used in the earlier DRAFT, reflecting genuinely better information (a confirmed multi-year deleveraging trend, a settled seasonality question, small and shrinking contingent liabilities, a disclosed parent backstop) rather than a change in the underlying business.

**Floor ≈ 0.65× book** = 23.95 × 0.65 ≈ **Rs. 15.55, rounded → Rs. 15.50.**
**Second tranche** = Floor × 0.90 ≈ **Rs. 14.00.**
**Ceiling ≈ 0.85× book** = 23.95 × 0.85 ≈ **Rs. 20.36, rounded → Rs. 20.00–20.50** — held below full book (unlike JKH's ~1.0× ceiling) because KHL's adjusted ROE (≈2.7%) sits below JKH's own reported ROE (3.4%) at the point JKH's ceiling was set, and because the Q1 relapse is a genuinely open question JKH's file didn't carry.

## 8. Market price and verdict

31 March 2026 close: Rs. 16.20 (down 20% YoY from Rs. 20.20), after a year that ranged from a high of Rs. 27.40 (26-Sep-2025) to a low of Rs. 16.00 (31-Mar-2026) — **a striking round-trip: the stock rallied hardest mid-year, then sold off into the year-end even as Q3/Q4 delivered the strongest actual profits of the year.** This isn't fully explained by anything in this file — plausibly the market discounting the Q4 gain as non-recurring in real time (which would be a *correctly skeptical* market read, consistent with this file's own adjustment), plausibly a broader macro/tourism de-rating, or both. Flagged as open, not resolved.

Last known price (Q1 FY26/27 interim, quarter ended 30 June 2026): **Rs. 18.10**, within a quarterly range of 16.00–18.70.

At 18.10: P/B = 18.10 ÷ 23.95 = **0.756×** — **between this file's floor (15.50) and ceiling (20.00–20.50).**

**Verdict: WATCHLIST. No position.** No actionable signal — price sits inside the band, same positioning as JKH itself. This is a materially more confident WATCHLIST than the earlier DRAFT, built on a real multi-year deleveraging trend and a settled seasonality question, but two real open items keep it from tightening further: the Q1 FY26/27 YoY relapse (resolves with Q2), and the market's own late-FY26 sell-off, which this file cannot yet explain from primary sources alone.

**What would raise the ceiling:** Q2 FY26/27 confirming FY25/26's pattern (narrowing loss, on track for H2 profitability) rather than deepening; current ratio continuing its four-year improvement past 0.40×; further deleveraging.
**What would lower the floor:** Q2 FY26/27 also deteriorating YoY (calling the recovery trend itself into question); any drawdown on the disclosed JKH funding backstop (a sign the mitigant is being tested, not just held in reserve); reversal of the debt/equity improvement.
