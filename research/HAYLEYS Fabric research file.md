# MGT — Hayleys Fabric PLC — Research File

> **Sources:** Annual Report 2025/26 (year ended 31 March 2026, primary) and Interim Report for Q1 FY2026/27 (period ended 30 June 2026, primary), both read 06 Aug 2026.
> **Position:** none. **Queue context:** 8th of the Hayleys-group sequence, inserted before the banking-sector turn as a same-sector continuation under the corrected one-sector-per-cycle rule. Flagged into the queue on a single signal — P/B 0.9×, the first sub-book print anywhere in this sequence — against a background where every other Hayleys name traded 23–200%+ *above* its earnings-yield ceiling. **That signal does not survive full derivation intact**, and the divergence is left visible below rather than smoothed over: MGT's book-value discount and its earnings-yield discount tell two different stories, and reconciling them is most of what this file is about.

---

## 1. What MGT actually is

Public limited company, incorporated 1993 (formerly Hayleys MGT Knitting Mills, renamed 2015), listed on the CSE Main Board. **Single reportable operating segment** — the Group states directly, under SLFRS 8, that its operations are integrated with "no distinguishable components that meet the criteria for separate segment reporting." Core business: manufacture and export of knitted fabric (single jersey, interlock, pique, rib, fleece, jacquard) for the apparel industry, sold via direct exports (23% of FY26 revenue), indirect exports (76%), and a small local-sales leg (1%).

**Ownership, cross-checked:** Hayleys PLC (No. 3 Share Investment Account) 58.96% direct + Hayleys Advantis 2.42% + Hayleys Agriculture Holdings 1.67% = **63.05%** — matches HAYL's own Note 39 effective stake (63.04%) almost to the decimal, the cleanest ownership cross-check of any file in this sequence. Public holding 36.80%, 8,541 shareholders — third-widest float after DIPD (41%) and HAYC (31%).

**The structural fact that makes this file different from every prior one in the sequence: functional currency is USD, not LKR.** The company earns and reports primarily in dollars (an export business), with LKR figures presented only as a translation for CSE compliance. This matters directly for how "sustainable EPS" gets built — see §6.

## 3. Segment picture — none to give

Single operating segment, confirmed directly in the Notes (18.2.1). No segment-reconciliation exercise is possible or needed here, unlike DIPD/HAYC — the whole company *is* the Textiles exposure HAYL's consolidated table shows. ## 4. Earnings quality — genuinely clean, no one-offs to strip, which makes the volatility real

Checked Note 19 (Other Operating Income, USD 716k, ~1% of PAT — solar power income, creditors write-back, sundry income, all small and recurring) and Note 20 (Other Expenses, effectively nil). **No disposal gain, no insurance settlement, no bargain-purchase gain identified anywhere in either primary source.** Unlike ALUM (fire settlement) and DIPD (bargain-purchase gain + bad-debt reversal), MGT's reported EPS needs no adjustment for contamination — which means the earnings volatility documented below is a genuine operating story, not an accounting one.

**The 10-year PAT series is the most volatile in this sequence, by a wide margin:**

| USD'000 | 2026 | 2025 | 2024 | 2023 | 2022 | 2021 | 2020 | 2019 | 2018 | 2017 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PAT | 2,077 | 7,036 | 7,865 | 1,133 | 12,777 | 3,852 | 1,271 | 1,058 | (680) | 490 |

A ~24× swing between the 2018 loss year and the 2022 peak, inside a single decade — wider than ALUM's fire-contaminated two years, wider than DIPD's or HAYC's two-point reads. 2021–22 line up with the post-COVID apparel-demand supercycle (revenue jumped from ~77M to ~150M in a single year); 2023's near-trough (1.13M) lines up with Sri Lanka's own currency-crisis year. **This looks like two distinct regimes stitched together, not noise around a stable mean.**

**A named, unresolved structural risk found in the tax note:** effective tax rate 31.8% (FY25) → **38.6% (FY26)**, against a 30% statutory rate both years. The reconciliation (Note 23.1.1) isolates the cause precisely: **"Losses not eligible for offset" adds 26.8 percentage points** to the Group ETR. Because Sri Lankan tax law taxes each Group entity separately, a loss-making subsidiary's losses can't shelter the profitable entity's tax bill — the loss shows up as *higher* consolidated tax rather than lower consolidated profit. Consistent with the interim's even-worse 59% Q1 FY26/27 ETR — whatever's loss-making at the subsidiary level hasn't resolved. **Which subsidiary, and whether the loss is growing or shrinking, is unanswered by either document — the single most important open number in this file.**

**Finance-cost note, checked and partly deflated:** the "collapsed finance expense" flagged from the interim is confirmed as partly an FX-translation reversal, not a pure leverage improvement — FY25's finance cost included a USD 1,057k unrealised FX translation *loss*; FY26's equivalent line is a small USD 35k *gain*. Real interest cost did fall too (term-loan repayments outpacing new borrowing), but the swing is smaller in substance than the headline suggests. Same caution DIPD's and HAYC's files applied to comparable lines.

## 5. The QoQ recovery claim — checked against the Annual Report's own quarterly table, and it holds, with a caveat

| FY25/26 (Group, USD'000) | Q1 | Q2 | Q3 | Q4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 34,462 | 31,140 | 27,045 | 31,168 |
| PBT | 1,955 | 726 | 112 | 590 |
| PAT | 1,295 | 450 | 24 | 308 |

Q3 FY25/26 was the actual trough (PAT margin ~0%). Q4 recovered to 308k, and Q1 FY26/27 (already in hand) posted 506k — **+64% versus Q4, in the same neighbourhood as the queue's original +71% flag.** So the recovery-off-trough claim is real, not a stale artifact. But it's a bounce off a bad quarter inside a bad year, not a return to form: Q1 FY26/27's 506k is still 61% below Q1 FY25/26's 1,295k, and management's own written outlook (Chairman's statement, dated 15 May 2026 — before Q1 FY26/27's results existed) is explicitly cautious: Middle East disruption, elevated logistics costs, continued pressure on consumption sentiment. **Nobody at the company was calling a trough at the point this Annual Report was signed.** FY26/27 Q2 is the real test of whether the bounce continues.

## 6. Balance sheet and leverage

| Metric | FY26 | FY25 |
|---|---|---|
| Interest Cover | **2.1×** | 4.3× |
| Current Ratio | 1.03 | 1.05 |
| Quick Asset Ratio | 0.54 | 0.51 |
| Gearing [Debt/(Equity+Debt)] | 47% | 51% |
| Total Debt (USD'000) | 34,971 | 39,388 |

**Interest cover fell from 4.3× to 2.1× in one year — now the weakest in the entire Hayleys-group sequence, below ALUM's previously-worst 2.20×.** This is a genuine one-year deterioration even though gearing itself improved slightly (debt paydown, equity growth from retained earnings). The two readings — improving gearing, worsening coverage — mean the debt load shrank a little while the earnings covering it shrank a lot faster. Current ratio at 1.03 is thin, in the same range as ALUM (1.08) and SINS (1.2×), not a standalone red flag but no cushion either.

**Company's own disclosed risk register** names two items directly: "heavy reliance on a few large buyers for revenue growth," and FX exposure from "LKR denominated borrowings and local expenses" against USD revenue — a two-directional risk (natural hedge on the revenue side, live exposure on the cost/debt side), not a clean tailwind despite the company being a dollar earner.

**Contingent liabilities: Nil, both years** (Note 27.2) — one genuinely clean line, the same category SINS's fourteen-item tax register and JKH's Customs dispute are not.

## 7. Valuation — hurdle method, with the currency wrinkle named explicitly

**Hurdle:** long T-bond ≈12.6% → neutral P/E ≈ **7.9×**.

**The method question, resolved per Kaveen's instruction to use the lower estimate:** because MGT's functional currency is USD, sustainable EPS is built from the USD earnings series, then converted to LKR at the *current* spot rate — the economically correct treatment for a dollar-earning business valued by a rupee-based investor today (the same logic the macro notes already apply to exporter margins under LKR depreciation).

Two windows were tested and diverge sharply — flagged, not hidden:
- **Method A (adopted) — 9-year average, excluding FY2022 as the least-repeatable year** (post-COVID apparel demand supercycle, compounded by Sri Lanka's own 2022 currency-crisis distortions — the same category of year HAYL's file flagged and excluded logic for its own 2022–23 print): PAT sum USD 24,102k ÷ 9 years = **USD 2,678k/yr** → EPS USD 0.00645.
- **Method B (rejected as base case) — HAYL's own 5-year precedent (2022–2026), including the peak:** USD 6,178k/yr → EPS USD 0.01487. Rejected here because, unlike HAYL's window (which passed its own consistency check — the 5-yr average landed close to FY26's actual print), MGT's FY26 actual (LKR EPS 1.54) sits *dramatically below* this window's implied output. A mid-cycle estimate that the current year contradicts by this much isn't confirmed the way HAYL's was.

**Sustainable EPS (Method A) at current spot (335.55 LKR/USD, 06-Aug-2026): 0.00645 × 335.55 ≈ Rs. 2.16.**

**This remains genuinely provisional** — a 9-year average across two regime changes is not the same confidence level as HAYL's 5-year mid-cycle window that self-confirmed. The gap between Method A (2.16) and Method B (4.99) is the real open question of this file, not a footnote to it.

**Floor multiple — five named risks, more severe in aggregate than any prior Hayleys-group name:**
1. **Widest earnings volatility in the sequence** — 24× trough-to-peak over 10 years, confirmed clean of accounting contamination (§4), meaning it's a real operating characteristic, not a strippable artifact.
2. **Interest cover 2.1×** — now the weakest in the queue, below ALUM's prior-worst 2.20×.
3. **Structural tax inefficiency** — an unresolved, unnamed subsidiary loss adding ~9 points to consolidated ETR.
4. **Three consecutive years of revenue decline** (−26% cumulative FY23→FY26) — a structural demand trend, not a single bad quarter.
5. **Buyer concentration**, company's own disclosed risk.

Offsetting, modestly: contingent liabilities nil; gearing improving even as coverage worsens; QoQ bounce off the Q3 FY26 trough is real, not fabricated (§5).

**Floor multiple: 4.2×** — the lowest in the sequence, below ALUM's 5.0× and SINS's net 4.8×, reflecting the combination of worst-in-queue interest cover and widest-in-queue earnings volatility.

**Floor** = 2.16 × 4.2 ≈ 9.07, rounded down → **Rs. 9.00**
**Second tranche** = Floor × 0.90 → **Rs. 8.10**
**Ceiling** = 2.16 × 7.9 (hurdle-neutral, no growth stretch — revenue has declined three years running, the opposite of mechanical growth) ≈ 17.06, rounded down → **Rs. 17.00**

## 8. Market price and verdict

**Current price, confirmed via TradingView (06-Aug-2026, live): Rs. 30.10** (−2.90% on the day pulled). Cross-checked against two same-vintage primary-source prints — the Annual Report's own FY-end close (Rs. 30.50, 31-Mar-2026) and the interim's LTP (Rs. 31.40, 30-Jun-2026) — all three cluster within a tight band, the cleanest price convergence of any file in this sequence (no DIPD/HAYC-style aggregator spread).

At Rs. 30.10: **~77% above the ceiling, ~234% above the floor.** Trailing P/E ≈ 19.5×, earnings yield on the *reported* FY26 EPS ≈ 5.1% — well under half the hurdle before any risk adjustment; on the sustainable (Method A) EPS the gap is even wider.

**The honest reconciliation this file exists to make:** the P/B signal that put MGT in the queue (0.9×) does not survive contact with the earnings-yield method. NAV/share moved from 29.92 (31-Mar) to 32.41 (30-Jun); at the current 30.10 price, P/B is **~0.93–1.01×** depending on which NAV snapshot is used — essentially *at* book, not confidently below it, and certainly not the "first genuinely cheap Hayleys name" framing the queue note carried in. **MGT is not an exception to the standing Hayleys-group premium pattern (HAYL, DIPD, HAYC, ALUM, SINS all >20% over ceiling) — it's the same pattern again, just visible on a different valuation axis than the one that flagged it.** That's a meaningful finding on its own: the group-wide premium hypothesis gets *stronger*, not weaker, from this file — even a name that screened cheap on book turns out to be priced well above where earnings power would pay for it.

**Verdict: WATCHLIST. No position.**

**What would raise the ceiling:** FY26/27 Q2 confirming the Q1 bounce is a real trend, not a one-quarter blip off a one-quarter trough; identification of the loss-making subsidiary and evidence its losses are shrinking (directly lowers the ETR risk); a resolution of the Method A/B gap in Method B's favour via a longer, cleaner data series.
**What would lower the floor:** a second consecutive year of revenue decline; interest cover falling further from 2.1×; the QoQ bounce reversing in Q2 FY26/27.
