# HAYC — Haycarb PLC — Research File

> **Source:** Interim Report FY ended 31/03/2026 (primary, the only document available this session), read 20 Jul 2026.
> **Position:** none. **Queue context:** 3rd of the HAYL → DIPD → HAYC → LIOC → KHL sequence, and — like DIPD — literally inside HAYL's own numbers: HAYL's file lists Haycarb at a 67.73% effective stake, and its segment table shows "Purification Products" FY26 PBT of Rs. 6,337,251k — a near-exact match to this report's own Group "Results from operating activities" of Rs. 6,337,250k. That's a genuine cross-file consistency check, not a coincidence, and it confirms HAYC *is* the Purification Products segment in HAYL's consolidated numbers, essentially in full.

## 1. What HAYC actually is

Public limited company, incorporated 1973, two reportable segments:

| Segment | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Activated Carbon** | Coconut-shell-based activated carbon for water/air treatment, gold recovery, food & beverage, energy storage, cigarette filters, pharmaceutical, catalyst support — ~16% global market share per public sources. Manufacturing and sales entities across Thailand, England, Australia, Indonesia, USA, Singapore, Philippines, India, Germany |
| **Environmental Engineering** | Water and wastewater treatment solutions |

**Ownership:** Hayleys PLC (via "No. 3 Share Investment Account") holds **67.73%** directly — confirmed identically in both this report's shareholder page and HAYL's own file. Employees' Provident Fund 4.45%. Public holding 30.98%, 8,257 shareholders, float-adjusted market cap Rs. 10.08 bn — thin, similar order of magnitude to DIPD's float.

**The most globally diversified name in the queue so far** — more listed foreign subsidiaries/associates (11 countries) than DIPD's five. That cuts two ways: genuine geographic diversification of end-demand, but also more currency-translation surface area than either HAYL or DIPD carries.

## 4. The amalgamation — Company vs Group (same shape as DIPD, smaller scale)

Ultracarb (Pvt) Ltd, a wholly-owned subsidiary, was amalgamated into the parent Haycarb PLC effective 1 October 2025 (common-control transaction, pooling-of-interest method) — structurally identical to DIPD's DPL Premier Gloves merger and disclosed with the same note format.

Impact disclosed directly: revenue contribution (Oct–Mar) Rs. 2,349,800k, profit after tax (Oct–Mar) Rs. 346,501k. Because Ultracarb was already inside the **Group** consolidation before the merger, this restates the **Company** (standalone) figures only — Company revenue jumped 46% (33.7bn vs 23.1bn) partly on this consolidation artefact, while Group revenue's 55% jump is the real, comparable number. **Same rule as DIPD: value off Group, not Company.** Company EPS (8.75) understates the business for the same structural reason DIPD's Company EPS did.

## 5. Balance sheet and cash flow — the risk that actually matters here, and it's sharper than DIPD's

| Group, Rs.'000 | FY26 | FY25 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Inventories | 23,192,905 | 15,156,266 | **+53%** |
| Trade and other receivables | 14,053,061 | 7,076,724 | **+99%** |
| Total current assets | 42,887,318 | 28,143,263 | +52% |
| Current interest-bearing loans/borrowings | 14,687,663 | 4,551,863 | **+223%** |
| LT interest-bearing loans | 2,204,413 | 1,360,386 | +62% |
| **Total interest-bearing debt** | **16,892,076** | **5,912,249** | **+186%** |
| Total liabilities | 30,521,953 | 16,220,805 | +88% |
| Total equity | 33,345,940 | 29,676,651 | +12% |

**Net cash flow from operating activities: Rs. (6,573,608)k — deeply negative**, versus an already-negative Rs. (354,107)k in FY25. Driven almost entirely by working capital: receivables consumed Rs. 7.83bn of cash, inventories consumed Rs. 8.07bn, partially offset by payables (+2.77bn). Investing activities absorbed a further Rs. 3.96bn (mostly Rs. 4.15bn of PP&E purchases). Net result: **cash and cash equivalents (net of overdrafts) swung from +Rs. 158,920k at FY25-end to Rs. (10,654,498)k at FY26-end — a ~Rs. 10.8bn negative swing in one year**, with bank overdrafts and short-term loans alone at Rs. 13.5bn.

**This is a materially sharper version of the risk flagged in both HAYL (operating cash flow reversal) and DIPD (debt-lag risk) files — not a repeat of the same finding, a worse one.** HAYL's Group operating cash flow went from +17.99bn to −10.50bn on a Rs. 43.5bn equity base; HAYC's went from −0.35bn to −6.57bn on a Rs. 33.3bn equity base — smaller absolute number, but the debt used to plug the gap (interest-bearing debt +186%, short-term alone +223%) is proportionally the most aggressive re-leveraging seen in this queue so far, DIPD included (+53%). **The business grew inventory and receivables faster than it grew cash, and financed the gap almost entirely with short-term borrowing.** Whether that's a deliberate capacity/inventory build ahead of demand or a working-capital control problem isn't answerable from this document — it's the single most important open question for the next file update.

## 6. Valuation — hurdle method

**Hurdle:** long T-bond (01 July 2037), 12.58% at the 13 July 2026 auction. Neutral P/E = 1 ÷ 0.1258 ≈ **7.9×**.

**Sustainable EPS:** unlike DIPD, this one's clean on two points rather than needing adjustment — **12.18 (FY26) and 12.10 (FY25) are within 1% of each other**, and no material one-off items are identifiable in the P&L (the only disposal/impairment lines are immaterial — tens of millions against billions of profit). Same caveat as every file in this queue so far: this is still a two-data-point read, not a real multi-year mid-cycle average; the annual report homework item stands. **Sustainable EPS ≈ Rs. 12.15.**

**Multiple — cut for named risks:** the operating-cash-flow/leverage risk in §5 is more severe in degree than DIPD's debt-lag risk (which itself justified a cut to 6.2×), and it's compounded by the 8-point gross margin compression in §2 — two risks, one of which (cash conversion) is more acute than anything flagged on HAYL or DIPD. **Floor multiple: 5.8×.**

**Floor** = 12.15 × 5.8 ≈ Rs. 70.5, rounded down → **Rs. 70.**
**Ceiling** = 12.15 × 7.9 (hurdle-neutral) ≈ Rs. 96.0, rounded down → **Rs. 95–96.**

## 8. Verdict

**WATCHLIST — floor 70, ceiling 95–96, confirmed market price Rs. 175.00.** No position. HAYC trades **~83% above the ceiling and ~150% above the floor** — the largest premium-to-fair-value found anywhere in the Hayleys-group sequence so far (HAYL ~50–65% over ceiling, DIPD ~23% over, HAYC ~83% over). This is now the **third consecutive Hayleys-group name priced well above its hurdle-rate ceiling**, and by a widening margin each time — a pattern strong enough that it's no longer just "worth carrying into the next file," it's approaching a standing hypothesis: **the market is currently paying a structural, and apparently growing, premium for Hayleys-group industrial names specifically**, not mispricing them one at a time by coincidence. LIOC next (unrelated sector, different mechanism) is a genuinely useful test of whether that premium is Hayleys-specific or just this market's current temperature for industrials generally.

**What would raise the ceiling:** FY27 Q1 operating cash flow showing the inventory/receivables build converting back to cash (the same test HAYL's file is waiting on, one layer down in the same group); confirmation the margin compression in §2 is input-cost timing (recoverable) rather than structural pricing pressure.
**What would lower the floor:** a second consecutive quarter of negative operating cash flow funded by more short-term debt; gross margin compressing further past 19.9%.
