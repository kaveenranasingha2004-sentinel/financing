## 1. What SERV actually is

Public limited company, incorporated 4 May 1969, listed on the CSE. **Operates a single hotel property** — The Kingsbury, Colombo — unlike every other file in this vault, which has covered either a diversified industrial business or a multi-property portfolio (KHL's Sri Lanka + Maldives estate). No segment diversification by geography or property; revenue splits by **activity** instead: Rooms, Food & Beverage (10 restaurants), Banquets & Events, and Others.

**Revenue mix, FY25/26 (Rs. 5,562,090k total):**

| Activity | Revenue (Rs.'000) | % of total |
|---|---|---|
| Food & Beverage (restaurants) | 2,680,000 | 48.2% |
| Rooms | 1,410,000 | 25.4% |
| Banquets & events | 1,160,000 | 20.9% |
| Other | ~312,000 | 5.6% |

**F&B is the largest single revenue line, not rooms** — a genuinely different structure from KHL's resort-driven model, and one reason the two companies shouldn't be read as comparable businesses just because both sit under a hospitality umbrella.

**Ownership:** Hayleys PLC 36.08% direct + Carbotels (Pvt) Ltd 23.40% = **59.48% Hayleys-linked** (reasonably close to HAYL's own claimed "57.57% effective stake" via Note 39's NCI-based method — small delta, not worth over-interpreting). Employees' Provident Fund 4.45%. Perera-family individual holdings total ~7.2% (three named individuals), plus three "Renuka"-named entities (Renuka Hotels PLC, Renuka City Hotels PLC, Renuka Consultants & Services) holding a further ~2.7% combined — very likely the same family cluster given the shared naming convention, though this file doesn't have primary confirmation of that link. **Nominal public holding 39.17%, 8,137 shareholders** — but if the Renuka-named entities are family-linked, genuine free float is probably meaningfully below the headline 39%.

**Directors' shareholding:** nil for nine of ten directors; one director (S.C. Ganegoda) holds 125,000 shares — a clean line overall.

## 3. The tax story — why PAT is the wrong number to trust here

The Annual Report's own tax reconciliation note (10.2) shows the Company had **zero current tax liability in both years** — taxable income after utilising carried-forward tax losses came to nil in FY25/26 *and* FY24/25. The entire reported tax line in both years is a **deferred tax movement**, not cash tax:

| | FY25/26 | FY24/25 |
|---|---|---|
| Current tax (expense)/reversal | – | – |
| Deferred tax (charge)/release | (178,119) | 11,566 |
| **Income tax (expense)/reversal** | **(178,119)** | **11,566** |

The MD&A explains the FY25/26 charge as **"the recognition of a deferred tax charge"** — while **the prior year's benefit came from "recognition of additional tax losses that had not been accounted for in earlier periods."** In plain terms: FY24/25's PAT of Rs. 551mn was flattered by a one-off accounting recognition of previously-unrecognised tax losses (a non-recurring benefit). FY25/26's PAT of Rs. 365mn reflects those losses now being **utilised down** against current profits — a real, ongoing process, but not comparable in kind to the prior year's one-off.

**This is a genuinely different earnings-quality problem than anything else in this vault so far** — not an insurance settlement (ALUM), not a disposal gain (KHL), not an effective-tax-rate shift (DIPD) — a **loss-carryforward shield that is winding down**. The company traces back to COVID-era losses (FY2019/20 through FY2022/23 combined losses of roughly Rs. 2.14bn per the ten-year table) that sheltered profits from cash tax through the recovery. FY25/26's effective P&L tax rate (178,119 ÷ 542,911 = **32.8%**) is already close to the 30% statutory rate — a signal the loss shield is nearing exhaustion, though this file cannot precisely quantify the remaining runway from the disclosure available.

**Sustainable EPS derivation:** given PBT has been genuinely stable across two post-recovery years (539,587 → 542,911k), the more defensible anchor is PBT, normalised at something close to the statutory 30% rate rather than either year's actual (artificially benefited or artificially depressed) tax charge:
541,249k (2-yr avg PBT) × 0.70 ≈ **Rs. 378,874k normalised PAT → EPS ≈ Rs. 0.78** — close to FY25/26's own reported 0.75, since that year's actual effective rate was already near-statutory. **FY24/25's 1.14 should not be used as a base figure — it's the one-off year, not FY25/26.**

**Sustainable EPS: ≈ Rs. 0.76** (midpoint, FY26 actual to normalised estimate).

## 4. Where the flat-PBT story came from — cost growth is the real risk, not one-off

Revenue grew 4.4%, but the story underneath is entirely about costs, not demand:

- **Occupancy improved to 75% from 67%** — a genuine, positive operating metric, and the reason revenue and gross profit grew despite a genuinely tougher cost environment.
- **Administrative expenses +13.0% YoY**, explicitly attributed to **"higher staff costs following salary revisions."**
- **Marketing expenses +29.6% YoY**, explicitly attributed to **"aggressive promotional and brand visibility initiatives... amid intensifying competition within the hospitality sector, including the recent entry of new market participants."**

**This is not a one-off cost spike — it's a stated, ongoing competitive response**, and it's the single most important named risk in this file: revenue growth of 4% is being earned at a cost base growing 13–30% in its two largest discretionary lines. Gross profit margin actually *expanded* (49.8%→49.0%... actually check: 2,769,631/5,562,090=49.8% vs 2,555,244/5,327,046=48.0% — a genuine 1.8pt gross margin improvement), meaning the pressure is happening below the gross-profit line, in SG&A — a cost-control question, not a pricing-power question.

## 5. Balance sheet — a genuine multi-year deleveraging trend, alongside a standing liquidity risk

From the Annual Report's own ten-year Key Indicators table:

| | FY22/23 | FY23/24 | FY24/25 | FY25/26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gearing (%) | 84.11 | 59.34 | 46.26 | **32.22** |
| Current ratio | 0.30 | 0.61 | 0.78 | **0.61** |
| Net assets/share (Rs.) | 0.88 | 2.79 | 3.96 | **5.56** |

**Four consecutive years of falling gearing** (84%→32%) — a real, structural deleveraging story, the same category of finding as KHL's own multi-year debt/equity improvement, and worth noting as a pattern showing up across more than one Hayleys-linked hospitality name now.

**The current ratio doesn't share that clean trend** — it improved through FY24/25 (0.78) then **fell back to 0.61 in FY25/26**, and the Q1 FY26/27 interim shows it easing further to **~0.57** (current assets 968,888k ÷ current liabilities 1,702,882k). Current liabilities structurally exceed current assets across the entire observed period — the same shape of standing liquidity risk flagged on KHL, without KHL's disclosed explicit parent-funding backstop; nothing in this file's sources states an equivalent commitment from Hayleys.

**Contingent liabilities:** confirmed none material, both in the Annual Report and the Q1 interim ("no material contingent liabilities or contingent assets"). A clean line — no ALUM-style live legal dispute here.

**The cost-growth pattern flagged in §4 is not a one-off FY25/26 event — it's continuing into the new fiscal year at a very similar rate** (admin +12% vs +13% FY26; marketing +25% vs +30% FY26). Gross profit was essentially flat (+0.4%) against a 1.6% revenue increase, and the entire operating-profit decline (−39.5%) traces to admin and marketing costs growing far faster than the top line. **This is the single most important open question the next interim needs to answer: is this cost growth reaching a plateau, or does it keep compounding as the "intensifying competition" the AR describes continues?**

**Finance income partially masked the operating deterioration at the PBT level** — a 7.3x jump (4,127k→34,298k) driven by FX gains on the Company's USD-denominated cash/receivables (the same LKR-depreciation-driven mechanism boosting finance income across several files in this vault this year). This is a real gain but not an operating one — worth separating from the underlying hospitality business's own trajectory, which on this quarter's evidence is worse than PBT alone suggests.

**No quarterly seasonality table exists in the Annual Report** (unlike KHL's, which had one) — this file cannot yet confirm whether Apr–Jun is a structurally weak quarter for a Colombo city hotel the way it demonstrably is for KHL's beach-resort seasonality. A city hotel's demand is more plausibly driven by business travel and MICE (meetings/conferences) than Northern Hemisphere leisure seasons — but this is a hypothesis, not a confirmed fact from primary sources. **Flagged as open, not resolved.**

## 7. Valuation — hurdle method

**Hurdle:** long T-bond ≈12.6% → neutral P/E ≈ **7.9×**.

**Sustainable EPS: ≈ Rs. 0.76** (§3) — anchored to two years of genuinely stable PBT (~540–543mn), normalised at close to the statutory tax rate rather than either year's distorted actual tax charge. **ROE check supports earnings-yield as the primary method here** (unlike JKH/KHL, which needed an asset-anchor because ROE sat below the hurdle): FY25/26 ROE on average equity ≈ 15.7% reported, ≈16.3% on the normalised PAT estimate — comfortably above the 12.6% hurdle, consistent with a business that has real earning power, not a depressed recovery play.

**Floor multiple — five named risks:**
1. **Cost growth (admin +12-13%, marketing +25-30%) outpacing revenue (+2-4%) across two consecutive periods** — an ongoing competitive-response cost, not a one-off, per the company's own stated reasoning.
2. **Current ratio persistently below 1.0×** (0.61 FYE, ~0.57 Q1) — a standing liquidity risk with no disclosed parent-support backstop (unlike KHL).
3. **Deferred-tax/loss-carryforward normalisation uncertainty** — the shield appears close to exhausted (FY26 effective rate already near-statutory), but the exact remaining runway isn't disclosed with enough precision to be confident the tax line won't move again.
4. **Single-property concentration** — no geographic or property diversification, unlike every other hospitality name in this vault.
5. **Ownership concentration** — 59.5% Hayleys-linked directly, plus a family cluster (Perera individuals + likely-related Renuka entities) that may mean genuine free float is well below the nominal 39.17% public holding.

**Floor multiple: 5.5×** — between ALUM's 5.0× (more severe, fire-contaminated earnings) and DIPD/HAYC's 5.8–6.2× (single dominant risk each); SERV's risk count (five) sits closer to ALUM's, but no single risk here is as acute as ALUM's 76.6%-short-term-debt or live legal dispute.

**Floor** = 0.76 × 5.5 ≈ Rs. 4.18, rounded down → **Rs. 4.10.**
**Second tranche** = Floor × 0.90 ≈ **Rs. 3.70.**
**Ceiling** = 0.76 × 7.9 (hurdle-neutral) ≈ Rs. 6.00, rounded down → **Rs. 6.00.**

## 8. Market price and verdict — the Hayleys-group premium extends across sectors

Last traded price, per the Q1 FY26/27 interim's own investor-information page: **Rs. 15.00** (30 June 2026), quarterly range 14.00–17.00. The Annual Report's own decade table shows FYE (31-Mar-2026) price of Rs. 15.10, P/E 20.17× — both sources converge closely.

**At 15.00: trailing P/E ≈ 20× on reported FY26 EPS, earnings yield ≈ 5.0% — well under half the 12.6% hurdle**, before any risk adjustment. **~150% above the ceiling and ~266% above the floor.**

**This is the finding worth sitting with:** every industrial Hayleys-group name researched so far (HAYL ~50–65% over ceiling, DIPD ~23%, HAYC ~83%, ALUM ~200%, SINS ~50%) has traded well above its hurdle-rate ceiling. **SERV — a single-property hospitality business, not an industrial one — shows the same pattern, at a similar order of magnitude to the group's higher end.** This is meaningful ahead of LIOC, which was deliberately queued last specifically to test whether the premium is sector-specific or Hayleys-specific: a hospitality name already showing the same premium as the industrial names is evidence pointing toward **"Hayleys-linked ownership itself," not "Hayleys' industrial businesses specifically,"** as the more likely common thread — though LIOC (a genuinely unrelated business, not just a different sub-sector) remains the cleaner test and shouldn't be skipped on the strength of this alone.

**Verdict: WATCHLIST. No position.**

**What would raise the ceiling:** Q2 FY26/27 confirming the admin/marketing cost growth is plateauing rather than continuing to outpace revenue; clearer disclosure on the remaining tax-loss-carryforward balance, removing the deferred-tax estimation uncertainty; a first full post-recovery cycle (through at least one soft year) confirming ~540mn PBT as genuinely sustainable rather than a two-year plateau.
**What would lower the floor:** a third consecutive period of cost growth outpacing revenue; current ratio deteriorating further past Q1's ~0.57; any confirmation that the "Renuka" shareholders are family-linked, tightening the real free float further.
