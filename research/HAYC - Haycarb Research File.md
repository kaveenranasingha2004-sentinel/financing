# HAYC — Haycarb PLC — Research File

> **Sources:** Interim Report FY ended 31/03/2026 (primary, read 20 Jul 2026); **Interim Report for the Three Months Ended 30 June 2026 (primary, read 13 Aug 2026)**.
> **Position:** none. **Queue context unchanged:** 3rd of the HAYL → DIPD → HAYC → LIOC → KHL sequence.
> **This version folds in the Q1 FY26/27 interim and, as of 17-Sep-2026, the hurdle-rate re-derivation.** The quarter looks spectacular on the headline (EPS +113% YoY) but roughly half of the pretax improvement above the operating line was consumed by a sharp jump in interest expense and FX losses on the debt book — the exact cost-of-leverage risk this file named in July, now visible in the P&L rather than just the balance sheet. Read alongside a genuine (if partial) improvement in operating cash flow, this is a mixed quarter, not a green light. **Separately, the long T-bond secondary-market yield moved 12.6%→11.96% (CBSL, 11-Sep-2026), pushing the neutral P/E from 7.9× to 8.36×** — re-derived below (§6) the same way LIOC's was: holding the same proportional risk-haircut the original 5.8× floor multiple represented, rather than mechanically scaling the old dollar figures. | **Environmental Engineering** | Water and wastewater treatment solutions |

**Ownership, reconfirmed via this interim's own Top 20 register (30-Jun-2026):** Hayleys PLC ("No. 3 Share Investment Account") 67.73% — unchanged, exact match to the original file. Employees' Provident Fund 4.45%. **Public holding 30.91%** (from 30.98% previously — essentially flat), 8,671 shareholders (up from 8,257). No change of substance to the ownership picture.

**A methodology note carried over from this interim's own disclosure:** the Group adopted **SLFRS 18** (replacing LKAS 1) from 1 April 2025 — a presentation-only standard that reclassifies items within the statement of profit or loss (moving finance income/finance cost into new line items such as "Foreign exchange differences" and "Other individually immaterial income from investments") without changing recognition or measurement. The comparative Q1 FY25/26 figures used throughout this update have been retrospectively reclassified onto the same basis, per the interim's own Note 3 reconciliation — so the YoY comparisons below are genuinely like-for-like, not distorted by a presentation change.

**Read this precisely — it's the central finding of this update.** Gross margin didn't continue compressing (§3 of the original file flagged an 8-point FY26 collapse, 27.9%→19.9%); it held essentially flat at ~19.5-19.8% quarter to quarter, which reads as the margin finding a new level rather than either recovering toward FY25's 27.9% or deteriorating further. **A genuine positive**, though one quarter of stability isn't proof the compression is fully behind the business.

**The full decomposition of the pretax growth is the real story:**

| Contributor to PBT growth (Rs.'000) | Amount |
|---|---|
| Gross profit growth | +1,244,141 |
| Admin expense growth (cost) | −453,359 |
| Operating FX gains (translation of foreign subsidiaries) | +379,757 |
| Other operating items (net) | −459 |
| **= Operating profit growth** | **+1,170,850** |
| Equity-investee profit, FX-on-cash, other investment income (net) | +57,882 |
| **= Operating result before financing, growth** | **+1,228,732** |
| **Interest expense (worse)** | **−219,533** |
| **FX loss on interest-bearing debt (worse)** | **−345,402** |
| **= PBT growth** | **+663,797** |

**Nearly half of the operating-level improvement (Rs. 564,935k of Rs. 1,228,732k, ~46%) was consumed by financing costs before it reached PBT.** This is exactly the shape of finding DIPD's own Q1 FY26/27 update made about its own debt-lag risk — a named leverage risk showing up in the P&L, not resolving. For HAYC it's compounded by an FX dimension: the operating-level FX line was a **tailwind** (+379,757, foreign-subsidiary translation gains as the rupee moved), while the debt-level FX line was a **headwind** (−345,402, loss on foreign-currency-denominated borrowings) — almost exactly offsetting each other. **HAYC's global footprint cuts both ways on the same currency move, in opposite directions, in the same quarter.**

## 4. Balance sheet and cash flow — the named risk, now with a genuinely mixed quarter of evidence

**Total interest-bearing debt continued growing, not stabilising:**

| Group, Rs.'000 | 30/06/2026 | 31/03/2026 | 30/06/2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| LT interest-bearing loans | 2,057,852 | 2,204,413 | 1,235,832 |
| ST loans + overdrafts (current) | 17,085,503 | 14,687,663 | 7,168,167 |
| **Total interest-bearing debt** | **19,143,355** | **16,892,076** | **8,403,999** |
| Total equity | 34,950,252 | 33,345,940 | 30,918,040 |
| **Debt/Equity** | **54.8%** | **50.7%** | **27.2%** |

**Debt grew a further 13.3% in a single quarter, and 128% YoY** — the re-leveraging flagged as HAYC's central named risk in July has not paused. Debt/equity has effectively doubled in a year (27.2% → 54.8%).

**Operating cash flow turned positive, a genuine and welcome reversal:** net cash from operating activities was **+Rs. 646,186k this quarter, versus a small negative (−Rs. 16,637k) in the same quarter last year** — a real improvement on the deeply negative FY26 full-year picture (−Rs. 6,573,608k). This is the first quarter in this file's history where the operating cash line reads positive.

**But the overall cash position kept deteriorating.** Net cash and cash equivalents (including overdrafts) moved from −Rs. 10,611,698k (31-Mar-2026) to **−Rs. 13,035,713k (30-Jun-2026)** — a further Rs. 2.42bn negative swing in the quarter, and Rs. 11.05bn worse than the same point a year ago (−Rs. 1,990,065k). Capex kept growing (PP&E purchases Rs. 1,899,436k this quarter, +37% YoY), and financing outflows (interest paid +159% YoY, loan repayments up, dividends up) outran the improved-but-still-modest operating inflow. **Read together: the business generated cash from operations for the first time in this file's record, but is still spending faster than it earns, funded by continued short-term borrowing.**

## 6. Valuation — hurdle method, re-derived under the new hurdle rate

**The hurdle moved.** Per the macro dashboard's 11-Sep-2026 update, the long T-bond (2037A) secondary-market yield now reads **11.96%**, down from 12.6% (last confirmed at the 16-Jul-2026 primary auction). This is a CBSL secondary-market quote, **not yet confirmed by a fresh primary auction** — treat this re-derivation as current-best-estimate, per the same discipline applied to LIOC's re-derivation, and re-check when the next auction result lands.

**New neutral P/E = 1 ÷ 0.1196 ≈ 8.36×** (was 7.9×).

**Sustainable EPS — held at Rs. 12.15** (the original two-point average of FY25 12.10 / FY26 12.18). Q1 FY27's strong print (annualised ≈14.80, TTM 14.14) is **not** treated as grounds to revise this: nearly half the quarter's operating-level gain was FX-translation-driven, a line this file has no basis yet to call recurring at this magnitude, and one quarter still isn't the annual-report multi-year series this file has flagged as missing since July.

**Floor multiple — re-derived, not mechanically scaled, same method as LIOC's re-derivation.** The original 5.8× floor multiple represented a **73.1% retention of the old 7.9× neutral** (a ~27% aggregate haircut for the operating-cash-flow/leverage risk, the sharpest version of that risk found anywhere in the Hayleys-group queue). **That risk didn't resolve this quarter — see §4, genuinely mixed evidence** — so the same 73.1% retention is held and reapplied to the new 8.36× neutral, giving a like-for-like floor multiple of **6.11×**.

**Floor** = 12.15 × 6.11 ≈ Rs. 74.2, rounded down → **Rs. 74.00.**
**Second tranche** = Floor × 0.90 ≈ **Rs. 66.50** — this file had not previously carried an explicit second-tranche line (unlike DIPD, ALUM, SINS, SERV, and KHL in the same sequence); added here per the standing system procedure (Layer 4, step 5) for consistency across the vault.
**Ceiling** = 12.15 × 8.36 (new hurdle-neutral) ≈ Rs. 101.6, rounded down → **Rs. 101–102.**

## 7. Verdict

**At Rs. 175.00 against the re-derived band: ~136% above the new floor (74.00), ~72% above the new ceiling (101–102)** — narrower than the old read (~150% / ~83%), purely because the risk-free comparator got cheaper, the same mechanical effect logged on LIOC. HAYC remains priced far above where this framework will pay for it, and unlike LIOC, this move doesn't bring the price meaningfully closer to being actionable — HAYC stays the widest-premium name in the Hayleys-group sequence by a large margin even under the friendlier hurdle.

**Verdict unchanged: WATCHLIST — no position.** This quarter is a genuine two-sided result, handled the way this file's method is built to handle it: the operating business (Activated Carbon specifically) grew strongly and margin stabilised — credited. The cost of the debt this file has flagged since inception showed up in the P&L for the first time, consuming nearly half the operating-level gain — also credited, in the other direction. Neither outweighed the other enough to move the risk-adjusted multiple; only the hurdle-rate input moved the dollar figures.

**What would raise the ceiling:** a second consecutive quarter of positive operating cash flow, ideally alongside debt growth actually slowing rather than continuing at a double-digit quarterly pace; confirmation the ~19.5-19.8% gross margin is a genuine new floor rather than a stabilisation point on the way to further compression.
**What would lower the floor:** finance costs and FX-on-debt losses continuing to grow at anything like this quarter's pace into Q2; a reversal back to negative operating cash flow; debt/equity continuing past 54.8% without a corresponding earnings base to support it.
