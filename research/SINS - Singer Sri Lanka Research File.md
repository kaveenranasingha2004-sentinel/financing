# SINS — Singer (Sri Lanka) PLC — Research File

> **Sources:** Interim Financial Statements for the twelve months ended 31 March 2026 (primary, read 30 Jul 2026), Annual Report 2025/26 (400pp, primary, read 30 Jul 2026), and Interim Financial Statements for the three months ended 30 June 2026 (primary, read 10 Aug 2026) — all directly from the company. Current price cross-checked via TradingView (30-Jul-2026 live quote) and the Q1 FY26/27 interim's own investor-information page, converging.
> **Position:** none. **Queue context:** 2nd of the ALUM → SINS → KHL → LIOC sequence. This file was built in three passes — an initial draft off the interim statements alone, a revision on the annual report, and now a quarterly update on the first FY26/27 interim. Every pass is left visible rather than smoothed over: the interim-only draft couldn't tell me the actual interest cover ratio and produced an overly harsh first-pass floor; the annual report corrected that with better evidence and surfaced a genuine disclosure-inconsistency red flag; this update tests the file's own named trigger against one real quarter of new data. All three passes are logged in the Thesis Log below.

## 1. What SINS actually is — and why it doesn't fit the Hayleys-industrial template

Six segments (now shown as seven lines in Q1 FY26/27's own segmental note, "Automobile and Other" newly broken out): Consumer Electronics, **Financial Services**, Furniture, Home Appliances, Digital Products, Sewing Machines, Automobile and Other. The structural fact that matters most: SINS carries a **"Direct Interest Cost"** line inside cost of sales, above gross profit — the funding cost of an installment-credit book. SINS is not a retailer with a financial-services segment bolted on; it's a **retailer-plus-captive-lender**, and the captive-lender leg is a top-two profit contributor (Financial Services PBT Rs. 5.92bn FY26, ~40% of Group operating profit; Rs. 1.93bn in Q1 FY26/27 alone, the single largest segment PBT line that quarter — see §9). Every risk assessment below has to be read through that lens — this is the most rate-sensitive business model in the Hayleys sequence so far, in both directions.

**Ownership:** per the Q1 FY26/27 interim's own Top 20 Shareholders note, Hayleys PLC holds **67.13%** direct (783,801,111 shares) + Hayleys Advantis 9.54% + Volanka 2.98% + Hayleys Aventura 2.71% + Carbotels 2.49% + Hayleys Agriculture 2.43% = **87.28% Hayleys-linked** — matching HAYL's own stated 86.45% effective stake almost exactly, a clean cross-check between the two files, reconfirmed this quarter. **Public holding: 10.94%, 8,717 shareholders** (up from 7,673 at the annual report) — still the thinnest float of any Hayleys-group name in this sequence. Float-adjusted market cap Rs. 9.70bn per the Q1 interim's own disclosure.

**Credit ratings (external validation, from the AR):** Singer (Sri Lanka) PLC Group — **AA-(lka), Fitch, stable outlook** — second only to HAYL's AAA(lka) across the whole sequence. Singer Finance (Lanka) PLC, the lending subsidiary — **BBB+(lka), Fitch, stable**, with Tier 1 Capital Ratio 10.92% (vs 8.5% CBSL minimum) and Total Capital Ratio 15.43% (vs 12.5% minimum), both improved YoY via a rights issue.

No fire settlement, no bargain-purchase gain, no disposal gain identifiable anywhere in the notes — unlike ALUM and DIPD, **no one-off contaminating this number** was found. Growth looks organic: every segment up, Financial Services and Home Appliances doing the heavy lifting.

Both sides small relative to Rs. 11.44bn PBT and roughly netting — not large enough to strip from sustainable EPS, but see §5 for a disclosure-quality flag tied to this same event.

## 4. The cash flow and balance sheet — the sharpest risk in the file (FY26 full year)

**Net cash from operating activities: Rs. (41,547,859)k**, versus an already-negative Rs. (13,866,914)k the prior year — driven by Increase in Debtors falling Due within one Year (37.1bn), Inventories (11.2bn), and Debtors falling Due after one Year (11.8bn). This is the **worst operating cash flow reversal of any Hayleys-group name researched so far** — wider than HAYC's -6.57bn or HAYL's -10.50bn — funded almost entirely by new debt: net proceeds from interest-bearing loans and borrowings were Rs. 33.04bn.

| Metric (Group, AR Performance Highlights) | FY26 | FY25 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Total Debt | Rs. 82,330mn | Rs. 49,279mn | +67% |
| Net Debt | Rs. 74,731mn | Rs. 40,875mn | +83% |
| Gearing Ratio | 77.7% | 74.1% | worse |
| Net Debt/Equity | **3.4×** | 2.6× | worse — highest absolute leverage in the sequence |
| **Interest Cover** | **4.2×** | 2.7× | **+55% — best in the sequence** |
| Current Ratio | 1.2× | 1.2× | flat |
| Quick Asset Ratio | 0.9× | 0.8× | improved |

**The honest read, held in tension deliberately:** simultaneously the most levered balance sheet in the Hayleys queue *and* the best-covered. Both true. **Q1 FY26/27 gives the first real test of both sides of this tension — see §9.**

**Credit quality — a live, named, unresolved risk:** Impairment loss on trade and other receivables nearly doubled in FY26, Rs. 1,031,192k vs Rs. 543,500k (**+90% YoY**). The Annual Report's own risk register separately names *"increased exposure to non-performing loans (NPLs) and capital adequacy pressures"* under CBSL requirements as a live risk. The **auditors flagged ECL allowance as a Key Audit Matter**. **No NPL ratio is disclosed anywhere in either document** — still the single most important open question, and it remains unanswered in the Q1 FY26/27 interim too (see §9, Open homework).

## 5. A disclosure-quality flag — the same event, two contradictory materiality claims

The Annual Report's Financial Capital review states Cyclone Ditwah losses of Rs.345mn were included with resultant losses to inventory. The formal climate-risk (TCFD-style) disclosure section, covering the identical event, states the damage resulted in *"no measurable effect"* on the Group's financials. Individually immaterial either way — but the two sections of the same document can't both be right about materiality. Flagged as a standing disclosure-quality item, a reason to read future risk disclosures from this company with a slightly higher bar for verification.

## 6. Contingencies — the longest tax-dispute register in this sequence

Fourteen separate items spanning Singer (Sri Lanka) PLC proper, Singer Digital Media, Regnis Appliances, Regnis (Lanka), Singer Industries (Ceylon), and Singer Finance — VAT, income tax, deemed VAT, surcharge tax, default notices, spanning assessment years back to 1993, confirmed unchanged in substance by the Q1 FY26/27 interim's own contingencies note (items a–o). Largest individual items: a deemed-VAT dispute (~Rs. 1.19bn determined liability, under further appeal, no provision made) and a VAT/income-tax assessment pair totaling over Rs. 1.5bn combined with penalties (2017–2022 assessment years). **One item resolved favourably since the AR:** item (o), a Rs. 43mn VAT-on-Financial-Services assessment, was annulled by the Tax Appeal Commission on 24 December 2024 — small, but the first contingency in this register to actually close out rather than escalate. **One item newly disclosed:** item (i), Singer Finance surcharge tax — Department of Inland Revenue's January 2026 determination revised the liability down to a net outstanding Rs. 247.2mn (tax) + Rs. 65.9mn (interest) + Rs. 49.4mn (penalty) after prior part-payments; appealed further to the Tax Appeals Commission 11 Feb 2026, hearing not yet commenced. None of this changes the floor multiple — the register's sheer breadth and multi-entity legacy-liability complexity was already priced in as risk #6 below, and nothing here is large enough on its own to move that assessment.

## 7. Valuation — hurdle method

**Hurdle:** long T-bond ≈12.6% → neutral P/E ≈ **7.9×**.

**Sustainable EPS:** Rs. 6.29 (§2, FY26), taken at face value — no identifiable one-off to strip. **The honest limitation, unchanged by this update:** this is only two full-year data points (3.43 → 6.29) with no multi-year series to confirm FY26 is a plateau rather than a peak. **Q1 FY26/27 (§9) argues, if anything, against plateau — annualized Q1 EPS runs 17% above FY26's full year — but one quarter is not a basis for revising the sustainable-EPS estimate itself; per the standing method, only a confirmed second full year would do that.**

**Floor multiple — six named risks, weighed against two genuine positives found only in the annual report:**

Risks:
1. Only two data points behind an 83% EPS jump.
2. Worst operating-cash-flow reversal in the Hayleys queue (-41.5bn), funded almost entirely by new debt.
3. Highest absolute leverage in the sequence — gearing 77.7%, net debt/equity 3.4×, both worsening.
4. Impairment/bad-debt provisioning +90% YoY, flagged by auditors as a Key Audit Matter, with no NPL ratio disclosed to size the underlying risk.
5. Total funding cost +38% YoY (FY26) — the core rate-sensitivity risk of the lending-book model. **Q1 FY26/27 shows this risk accelerating, not stabilizing (+68% YoY that quarter — see §9). This is a live confirmation of the named risk, not a new one, and does not by itself change the multiple, per the same discipline JKH's file applies to confirmed-but-not-yet-re-priced risks — but it is the single most important data point this update contributes.**
6. Thinnest public float in the sequence (10.94%) plus the longest tax-contingency register plus a documented internal disclosure inconsistency.

Offsetting positives:
- Interest cover 4.2×, improving — the best debt-servicing coverage of any Hayleys-group name researched.
- AA-(lka) Group Fitch rating, second only to HAYL's AAA(lka); Singer Finance separately BBB+(lka) with compliant, improving regulatory capital ratios.
- Cleanest earnings quality of any file so far — no identifiable one-off; smallest NCI leakage (4.1%, reconfirmed at 4.2% in Q1 FY26/27).
- **New this update:** impairment provisioning growth decelerated to +56% YoY in Q1 FY26/27 from FY26's full-year +90% — the first quarter that doesn't confirm risk #4's trajectory, worth tracking but not yet enough to relax the multiple (§9).

**Net calibration: 4.8×** — unchanged this update. The case for tightening (decelerating impairment growth, flat QoQ leverage) and the case for cutting further (accelerating funding cost, still-negative operating cash flow) point in opposite directions on a single quarter's evidence; per this file's own standing rule, numbers change on trigger events, not on price or on a single ambiguous data point. Q2 FY26/27 is the next real test.

**Floor** = 6.29 × 4.8 ≈ 30.2, rounded down → **Rs. 30.00**
**Second tranche** = 30.00 × 0.90 → **Rs. 27.00**
**Ceiling** = 6.29 × 7.9 (hurdle-neutral, no growth stretch — the growth here is leveraged-book expansion, not the mechanical/contracted kind this method reserves a stretch for) ≈ 49.7, rounded down → **Rs. 49–50**

## 9. Q1 FY26/27 Update (quarter ended 30 June 2026, reviewed 10-Aug-2026)

**The file's own standing next-trigger — "does impairment provisioning keep outpacing book growth, or was FY26 Q4 a one-off" — gets its first real answer this quarter, and the two named risks move in opposite directions.**

**1. Headline growth still accelerating, not plateauing.**

| | Q1 FY26/27 | Q1 FY25/26 | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Group Revenue | 38,722,928 | 27,552,849 | +41% |
| Operating Profit | 4,800,426 | 3,010,960 | +59% |
| Profit Before Tax | 3,474,325 | 2,334,376 | +49% |
| Profit for the Period | 2,241,072 | 1,553,261 | +44% |
| **EPS (Rs.)** | **1.84** | 1.28 | +44% |

Annualised (1.84 × 4 ≈ 7.36), this quarter's run-rate sits **~17% above FY26's full-year EPS of 6.29** — the second consecutive period showing genuine growth rather than a post-peak plateau, strengthening (not yet confirming) the case that 6.29 understates rather than overstates sustainable earnings power. NCI took Rs. 93,462k of Rs. 2,241,072k — **4.2% minority leakage**, matching FY26's 4.1% almost exactly.

**2. Impairment vs. receivables growth — the first data point that doesn't confirm the standing risk.**
Impairment loss on trade and other receivables: Rs. 187,255k vs Rs. 120,344k a year earlier (**+56% YoY**) — a real deceleration from FY26's full-year +90%. Meanwhile the book being provisioned against grew faster: Group trade and other receivables +66% YoY (56,682,826k → 94,230,838k). **Provisioning growing slower than the receivables book it covers is the first quarter in this sequence that argues against the credit-quality risk worsening — one data point, not a reversal, but the first one pointing the other way.**

**3. Funding cost — the sharpest acceleration in the file's history.**
Direct Interest Cost: Rs. 2,373,301k vs Rs. 1,340,533k (**+77% YoY**). Finance Cost: Rs. 1,207,220k vs Rs. 786,019k (**+54% YoY**). **Combined funding cost: Rs. 3,580,521k vs Rs. 2,126,552k = +68% YoY** — materially faster than FY26's already-elevated full-year +38%. This is the most consequential single number in this update: the rate-sensitivity risk this file has carried since its first draft is not stabilising, it is compounding faster than the year that originally justified the floor-multiple cut.

**4. Operating cash flow — improved, but still negative.**
Net cash used in operating activities: Rs. (4,099,138)k vs Rs. (5,175,874)k in Q1 FY25/26 — a smaller shortfall year-on-year, and a much smaller one in absolute terms than FY26's full-year Rs. (41.5)bn reversal (as expected, since Q1 is one quarter of twelve). Genuinely encouraging directionally; nowhere near enough data to call the full-year cash-conversion problem resolved.

**5. Leverage — flat quarter-on-quarter, a sharp deceleration from FY26's borrowing pace.**
Total interest-bearing debt (current + non-current): Rs. 84,220,979k (30-Jun-2026) vs Rs. 82,286,238k (31-Mar-2026) — **+2.3% in the quarter**, a marked slowdown from FY26's full-year +67%. Net Assets per Share rose to Rs. 18.41 (Group) from Rs. 17.07 at FY26-end. Worth flagging as a positive data point but treating with the same caution as the impairment deceleration above — Q1 is seasonally the smaller working-capital-build quarter for a retailer, so one quarter of restraint doesn't yet confirm a structural slowdown in borrowing.

**6. New item — a property acquisition, small and not thesis-moving.**
Per the interim's post-balance-sheet-date note: Singer (Sri Lanka) PLC executed a sale-and-purchase agreement with ACME Printing and Packaging PLC (28-Apr-2026) to acquire premises in Piliyandala for Rs. 630mn "for the purpose of expanding the local manufacturing business," completed 24-Jul-2026. Small relative to the balance sheet (~0.8% of FY26 revenue); a real capital commitment worth noting for context, not a valuation input.

**7. Dividend.** First interim dividend of Rs. 0.50/share (Rs. 583.8mn) declared 30-Jun-2026, paid 22-Jul-2026 — continuity with FY26's dividend pattern, not a new signal either direction.

**At 76.00: trailing P/E ≈ 12.08×, earnings yield ≈ 8.3%** — still below the 12.6% hurdle before any risk adjustment, essentially unchanged from the 30-Jul read (P/E 11.85×, e/y 8.44%). **~52% above the ceiling, ~153% above the floor** — the premium has widened marginally, not narrowed, and remains squarely mid-pack in the Hayleys-group premium sequence (DIPD ~23%, SINS ~50–52%, HAYL ~50–65%, HAYC ~83%, ALUM ~200%, MGT ~77%).

**Verdict: no falsifier fired. WATCHLIST confirmed, no position.** The file's own named trigger produced a genuinely mixed answer rather than a clean confirmation or reversal — impairment growth decelerated (good), funding cost accelerated sharply (bad), leverage growth paused (good, tentatively), operating cash flow improved but stayed negative (neutral-to-good). Per the standing method, a single ambiguous quarter does not move the floor multiple in either direction; Q2 FY26/27 is the next real test, and it should be read specifically against the funding-cost trajectory established here, since that is now the single fastest-moving risk in the file.

**What would raise the ceiling:** a second consecutive quarter of decelerating impairment growth relative to receivables growth; funding-cost growth rate falling back toward FY26's 38% pace rather than compounding further; a disclosed NPL ratio showing the credit book is not deteriorating faster than provisioning assumes.
**What would lower the floor:** funding cost growth continuing to accelerate past this quarter's 68%; a reversal back to FY26-pace borrowing growth in Q2; impairment growth re-accelerating past receivables growth.

## Open homework

1. **Singer Finance's actual NPL ratio** — still nowhere in either primary source, including this quarter's interim. The single most important unresolved number in this file, unchanged after three passes.
2. A real multi-year EPS series (5+ years) — Q1 FY26/27 strengthens rather than resolves the plateau-vs-peak question.
3. Whether the Rs. 345mn Cyclone Ditwah loss / "no measurable effect" contradiction gets reconciled or repeats in next year's disclosure.
4. **Funding-cost trajectory — newly the most urgent open item.** Q2 FY26/27 needs to be read specifically for whether +68% YoY was a one-quarter spike (e.g., timing of new borrowings drawn late in FY26 not yet lapping) or a genuine acceleration in the cost of the lending-book model.
5. Reconcile SINS's Financial Services segment PBT against HAYL's Consumer & Retail segment once both are current for the same period — a cross-file consistency check in the same spirit as the DIPD/HAYC reconciliations already done.
6. "Automobile and Other" newly broken out as its own segmental line in the Q1 FY26/27 interim (Rs. 4.72bn revenue, Rs. 291–293mn PBT) — not previously tracked separately in this file; worth watching whether this becomes a recurring disclosure or reverts to being folded into "Other."
