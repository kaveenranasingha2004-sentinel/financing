# SINS — Singer (Sri Lanka) PLC — Research File

> **Sources:** Interim Financial Statements for the twelve months ended 31 March 2026 (primary, read 30 Jul 2026) and Annual Report 2025/26 (400pp, primary, read 30 Jul 2026), both directly from the company. Current price cross-checked via TradingView (30-Jul-2026 live quote), converging with both the AR's own FY-end close and the interim's LTP.
> **Position:** none. **Queue context:** 2nd of the ALUM → SINS → KHL → LIOC sequence. This file was built in two passes — an initial draft off the interim statements alone, then revised on the annual report. The revision is left visible rather than smoothed over: the interim alone couldn't tell me the actual interest cover ratio, and inferring leverage risk without it led to an overly harsh first-pass floor multiple. The annual report corrected that with better evidence, and also surfaced a genuine disclosure-inconsistency red flag the interim never could have shown. Both passes are logged in the Thesis Log below.

## 1. What SINS actually is — and why it doesn't fit the Hayleys-industrial template

Six segments: Consumer Electronics, **Financial Services**, Furniture, Home Appliances, Digital Products, Sewing Machines. The structural fact that matters most: SINS carries a **"Direct Interest Cost"** line (Rs. 6,567,935k, FY26) inside cost of sales, above gross profit — the funding cost of an installment-credit book. SINS is not a retailer with a financial-services segment bolted on; it's a **retailer-plus-captive-lender**, and the captive-lender leg is now the largest single profit contributor (Financial Services PBT Rs. 5,915,637k FY26, ~40% of Group operating profit, ahead of Home Appliances at Rs. 5,040,455k). Every risk assessment below has to be read through that lens — this is the most rate-sensitive business model in the Hayleys sequence so far, in both directions.

**Ownership:** Hayleys PLC 67.13% direct + Hayleys Advantis 9.54% + Volanka 2.98% + Hayleys Aventura 2.71% + Carbotels 2.49% + Hayleys Agriculture 2.43% = **87.28% Hayleys-linked** — close to HAYL's own stated 86.45% effective stake, a clean cross-check between the two files. **Public holding: 10.94%, 7,673 shareholders** — the thinnest float of any Hayleys-group name in this sequence (DIPD 41%, HAYC 31%, ALUM ~20%), sitting on the CSE's minimum compliant float threshold.

**Credit ratings (external validation, from the AR):** Singer (Sri Lanka) PLC Group — **AA-(lka), Fitch, stable outlook** — second only to HAYL's AAA(lka) across the whole sequence. Singer Finance (Lanka) PLC, the lending subsidiary — **BBB+(lka), Fitch, stable**, with Tier 1 Capital Ratio 10.92% (vs 8.5% CBSL minimum) and Total Capital Ratio 15.43% (vs 12.5% minimum), both improved YoY via a rights issue.

| | FY26 | FY25 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|

| Gross Profit | 40,892,147 | 27,995,731 | +46% |
| Operating Profit | 14,892,977 | 8,539,531 | **+74%** |
| Finance Cost | (3,564,445) | (3,175,197) | +12% |
| Profit Before Tax | 11,440,791 | 6,221,709 | +84% |
| Profit for the Period | 7,658,132 | 4,178,261 | +83% |
| **Basic EPS (Rs.)** | **6.29** | 3.43 | +83% |

No fire settlement, no bargain-purchase gain, no disposal gain identifiable anywhere in the notes — unlike ALUM and DIPD, **no one-off contaminating this number** was found. Growth looks organic: every segment up, Financial Services and Home Appliances doing the heavy lifting.

NCI took Rs. 316,133k of Rs. 7,658,132k — **4.1% minority leakage**, by far the smallest in the sequence (DIPD 24%, HAYL 37%). Owners' profit is genuinely almost all of it.

**Total funding cost is the number to watch, and it's rising faster than the headline suggests:** Direct Interest Cost (+57%) plus Finance Cost (+12%) = total funding cost up **38% YoY** to Rs. 10.13bn. That's the cost of running a leveraged lending book into a rising-rate world — the same mechanism the insurance-sector files track, but here it's a cost line, not an income line. The lending spread is absorbing it for now (Financial Services PBT margin ~27%), but this is the single biggest thing that can go wrong here.

**One disclosed one-off worth flagging but not adjusting for:** Cyclone Ditwah caused a **Rs. 345mn loss** embedded in Selling & Administrative Expenses (per the Financial Capital review — 30+ shops damaged) and generated offsetting insurance-claim income inside "Miscellaneous Income" (which rose 62% YoY, Rs. 413mn → Rs. 670mn, partly Ditwah-related). Both sides are small relative to Rs. 11.44bn PBT (Rs. 345mn ≈ 3%) and roughly netting — not large enough to strip from sustainable EPS, but see §5 for a disclosure-quality flag tied to this same event.

## 4. The cash flow and balance sheet — the sharpest risk in the file, now with the ratio that contextualizes it

**Net cash from operating activities: Rs. (41,547,859)k**, versus an already-negative Rs. (13,866,914)k last year — driven by Increase in Debtors falling Due within one Year (37,122,389), Inventories (11,163,351), and Debtors falling Due after one Year (11,811,605). This is the **worst operating cash flow reversal of any Hayleys-group name researched so far** — wider than HAYC's -6.57bn or HAYL's -10.50bn — funded almost entirely by new debt: net proceeds from interest-bearing loans and borrowings were Rs. 33.04bn this year.

| Metric (Group, AR Performance Highlights) | FY26 | FY25 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Total Debt | Rs. 82,330mn | Rs. 49,279mn | +67% |
| Net Debt | Rs. 74,731mn | Rs. 40,875mn | +83% |
| Gearing Ratio | 77.7% | 74.1% | worse |
| Net Debt/Equity | **3.4×** | 2.6× | worse — highest absolute leverage in the sequence |
| **Interest Cover** | **4.2×** | 2.7× | **+55% — best in the sequence** |
| Current Ratio | 1.2× | 1.2× | flat |
| Quick Asset Ratio | 0.9× | 0.8× | improved |

**The honest read, held in tension deliberately:** this is simultaneously the most levered balance sheet in the Hayleys queue (77.7% gearing, 3.4× net debt/equity) *and* the best-covered (4.2× interest cover, improving). Both are true. For a retailer-lender, growing the receivables book by borrowing against it is the business model, not automatically a red flag the way it would be for an industrial company sitting on unsold inventory — but it means the entire structure depends on continued debt-market access at manageable rates, and the coverage ratio, however good today, is exactly what would deteriorate first if funding costs kept outrunning book growth.

**Credit quality — a live, named, unresolved risk:** Impairment loss on trade and other receivables nearly doubled, Rs. 1,031,192k vs Rs. 543,500k (**+90% YoY**). The Annual Report's own risk register separately names *"increased exposure to non-performing loans (NPLs) and capital adequacy pressures"* under CBSL requirements as a live risk. The **auditors flagged ECL allowance as a Key Audit Matter** — confirming this is genuinely judgment-heavy and material (allowances: trade receivables Rs. 1,096m, hire purchase Rs. 301m, lease receivables Rs. 673m, loans Rs. 982m, other Rs. 1,615m). **No NPL ratio is disclosed anywhere in either document** — this is the single most important open question the file is carrying forward, not answerable from what's available.

## 5. A disclosure-quality flag — the same event, two contradictory materiality claims

The Annual Report's Financial Capital review states: *"Losses from Cyclone Ditwah amounting to Rs.345 Mn is included from this as over 30 shops were damaged with resultant losses to inventory as well."* The formal climate-risk (TCFD-style) disclosure section, covering the identical event, states: *"32 retail outlets... sustained minor damage... resulting in no measurable effect on the Group's Statement of Profit & Loss or Financial Position or Cash Flow Statement."*

Individually immaterial either way against Rs. 11.44bn PBT — but the two sections of the same document can't both be right about materiality. Flagged as a standing disclosure-quality item, the same category as the DIPD/HAYC price-data-quality lessons: not a valuation input on its own, but a reason to read future risk disclosures from this company with a slightly higher bar for verification.

## 6. Contingencies — the longest tax-dispute register in this sequence

Fourteen separate items spanning Singer (Sri Lanka) PLC proper, Singer Digital Media, Regnis Appliances, Regnis (Lanka), Singer Industries (Ceylon), and Singer Finance — VAT, income tax, deemed VAT, surcharge tax, default notices, spanning assessment years back to 1993. Largest individual items: a deemed-VAT dispute (~Rs. 1.19bn determined liability, under further appeal, no provision made) and a VAT/income-tax assessment pair totaling Rs. 662mn + Rs. 847mn penalty and Rs. 596mn + Rs. 780mn penalty (2017–2022 assessment years). None are JKH-tax-contingency scale relative to equity, but the sheer number and span of amalgamated-entity legacy disputes — six different corporate identities' historical liabilities are now Singer's problem — is a genuine complexity risk unlike anything in DIPD or HAYC's files.

## 7. Valuation — hurdle method

**Hurdle:** long T-bond ≈12.6% → neutral P/E ≈ **7.9×**.

**Sustainable EPS:** Rs. 6.29 (§2), taken at face value — no identifiable one-off to strip. **The honest limitation:** this is only two data points (3.43 → 6.29, an 83% jump) with no multi-year series available to confirm FY26 is a plateau rather than a peak. The caveat travels with the number, not a substitute for it.

**Floor multiple — six named risks, weighed against two genuine positives found only in the annual report:**

Risks:
1. Only two data points behind an 83% EPS jump.
2. Worst operating-cash-flow reversal in the Hayleys queue (-41.5bn), funded almost entirely by new debt.
3. Highest absolute leverage in the sequence — gearing 77.7%, net debt/equity 3.4×, both worsening.
4. Impairment/bad-debt provisioning +90% YoY, flagged by auditors as a Key Audit Matter, with no NPL ratio disclosed to size the underlying risk.
5. Total funding cost +38% YoY — the core rate-sensitivity risk of the lending-book model, live now with the long bond at 12.6%.
6. Thinnest public float in the sequence (10.94%) plus the longest tax-contingency register plus a documented internal disclosure inconsistency.

Offsetting positives (not present, or not evidenced, in prior files in this sequence):
- Interest cover 4.2×, improving — the best debt-servicing coverage of any Hayleys-group name researched.
- AA-(lka) Group Fitch rating, second only to HAYL's AAA(lka); Singer Finance separately BBB+(lka) with compliant, improving regulatory capital ratios.
- Cleanest earnings quality of any file so far — no identifiable one-off; smallest NCI leakage (4.1%).

**Net calibration: 4.8×** — a deliberate upward revision from this file's own first-pass draft of 4.5×, made openly: the interest-cover and credit-rating evidence only existed in the annual report, not the interim, and inferring the leverage risk without it had overweighted the danger relative to what the company's own coverage ratios show. The cash-flow and credit-quality risks remain real and are not discounted away by this revision.

**Floor** = 6.29 × 4.8 ≈ 30.2, rounded down → **Rs. 30.00**
**Second tranche** = 30.00 × 0.90 → **Rs. 27.00**
**Ceiling** = 6.29 × 7.9 (hurdle-neutral, no growth stretch — the growth here is leveraged-book expansion, not the mechanical/contracted kind this method reserves a stretch for) ≈ 49.7, rounded down → **Rs. 49–50**

## 8. Market price and verdict

**Current price, confirmed via TradingView (30-Jul-2026, live): Rs. 74.50.** Cross-checked against two independent same-vintage prints — the Annual Report's own FY-end close (Rs. 77.00, 31-Mar-2026) and the interim statement's LTP (Rs. 76.20) — all three converge in a tight band, giving genuine confidence in the number rather than a single-source read.

At 74.50: trailing P/E ≈ 11.85×, earnings yield ≈ 8.44% — below the 12.6% hurdle before any risk adjustment. **~50% above the ceiling, ~148% above the floor.**

**Verdict: WATCHLIST. No position.** SINS lands almost exactly mid-pack in the Hayleys-group premium pattern first identified in HAYL's file and confirmed at every name since (DIPD ~23% over ceiling, SINS ~50%, HAYL ~50–65%, HAYC ~83%, ALUM ~200%). Worth sitting with plainly: **SINS has the best-evidenced earnings quality, the best interest cover, and the second-best credit rating of any name in this sequence — and it still trades roughly 50% above where this framework would pay for it.** That strengthens the standing hypothesis rather than weakening it: this doesn't look like the market mispricing distressed or opaque names one at a time — it looks like a structural premium being paid for the Hayleys-group name itself, independent of individual company quality. LIOC, still queued last and deliberately unrelated to Hayleys, remains the real test of whether that premium is group-specific or just this market's current temperature for industrials generally.

**What would raise the ceiling:** a real multi-year EPS series showing 6.29 as a genuine new operating plateau rather than a single strong year; disclosed NPL ratio showing the credit book is not deteriorating faster than provisioning already assumes; operating cash flow converting back toward positive as receivables growth normalizes.
**What would lower the floor:** a second consecutive year of impairment growth outpacing book growth; interest cover reversing back toward FY25's 2.7× as funding costs continue to outrun profit growth; any material Court/Tax Appeals Commission ruling against the company on the larger contingencies in §6.

3. Whether the Rs. 345mn Cyclone Ditwah loss / "no measurable effect" contradiction gets reconciled or repeats in next year's disclosure.
