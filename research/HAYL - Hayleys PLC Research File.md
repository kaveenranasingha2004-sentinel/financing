## 1. What Hayleys actually is

A 1952-listed holding company sitting atop **14 industry segments plus a residual "Others"**, run federated — "each sector which falls under the purview of a different GMC member is considered a separate segment," Chairman as chief operating decision maker. 202 affiliates group-wide.

Two entities matter for reading these numbers correctly, and they diverge sharply this year:
- **Consolidated (Group)** — all subsidiaries combined. This is what the market prices.
- **Company (standalone parent)** — the holdco alone, which issues the listed debentures and pays the dividend from its own cash. Its P&L looks nothing like the Group's this year (Section 4).

**The holdco structure is genuinely closer to JXG than to JKH or SUN.** Hayleys owns controlling stakes in eight separately-listed CSE companies through the sector structure:

A true SOTP — summing each listed sub's market cap at Hayleys' stake, plus a fair multiple on the unlisted sectors, minus holdco net debt — would be the more defensible method long-run, the same way JXG's SOTP beat a blended multiple. **I tried to build it today and stopped**, not because it's a future file's job, but because the web sources I pulled for DIPD's price alone gave four different numbers in the same session — Rs. 46.70, 55.50, 56.50, 59.90, spanning weeks with no reliable timestamp. Using that to construct a formal SOTP would trade one error (deferring the valuation) for another (false precision on bad data). **This is a data-quality problem, not a scheduling one** — it gets fixed by a broker-terminal price check, not by waiting for the DIPD file to exist. The earnings-based floor/ceiling below doesn't wait on it.

## 2. The headline numbers (12 months to 31.03.2026, consolidated)

| Metric | FY26 | FY25 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | Rs. 585.02 bn | Rs. 490.56 bn | +19% |
| EBITDA | Rs. 64.82 bn | Rs. 60.26 bn | +8% |
| Operating profit (EBIT) | Rs. 49.37 bn | Rs. 48.40 bn | +2% |
| Profit before tax (continuing) | Rs. 38.04 bn | Rs. 36.71 bn | +4% |
| Profit attributable to owners | Rs. 14.05 bn | Rs. 13.45 bn | +4% |
| Basic EPS (group, all operations) | Rs. 18.71 | Rs. 17.91 | +4% |
| Basic EPS (continuing operations only) | Rs. 20.34 | Rs. 19.69 | +3% |
| ROE | 12.41% | 14.44% | −2.0 pts |
| ROCE | 10.73% | 13.89% | −3.2 pts |
| Interest cover (Group) | 2.88× | 3.33× | −0.45 |

**Revenue outgrew profit at every line below gross margin.** Operating profit +2% against revenue +19% is the single most important number in the P&L — this year's growth was funded (direct interest cost +57%, admin expenses +19%), not free. ROE and ROCE both compressed, and Group interest cover eased from 3.33× to 2.88× — still comfortable for an AAA(lka)-rated (Fitch) issuer, but moving the wrong direction while leverage grew.

Non-controlling interest took Rs. 8.17bn of the Rs. 22.22bn group profit — 37%. Any valuation off consolidated profit that doesn't net this out overpays for cash flow that isn't the parent's.

**Consumer & Retail is now the single largest profit contributor**, up 70% on 44% revenue growth. The portfolio-review pages confirm this is operational, not translational: "record performance," market-share gains, new global brand additions (Starlink, Hikvision, HP), driven by a genuine recovery in consumer demand — this is Singer Sri Lanka territory. This is a real, sourced finding, not a guess.

**Half the segments shrank.** Textiles (−58%), Industry Inputs (−92%), Power & Energy (−74%), Leisure (−43%), Plantations (−22%) — a genuinely broad deceleration under the "record revenue" headline. Group-level +4% PBT growth is really: Consumer & Retail funding softness everywhere else.

**"Others" collapsed 84% (7.99bn → 1.30bn) — now resolved, not left as an open unknown.** This segment isn't an operating sector; per the portfolio-review pages, "Others" comprises **Hayleys Business Solutions International (a small BPO/shared-services business, Rs. 1.6bn revenue) plus Hayleys PLC's own centralised corporate results.** Two things sit inside that corporate layer: (1) FY25 carried a one-off Rs. 370mn gain on bargain purchase that didn't repeat, and (2) the segment's "Results from operating activities" line moves with corporate-level items (treasury income, service fee income/costs, VAT on financial services) that are naturally lumpy year to year at this scale relative to the segment's small revenue base. This is a holdco-bookkeeping segment, not a hidden operating problem — it's 0.3% of group revenue and doesn't change the group verdict either way.

## 4. The parent-company (standalone) picture — the one genuine one-off

| Metric | FY26 (Company) | FY25 (Company) |
|---|---|---|
| Profit for the period | **(1,435,851)** — a loss | 3,991,560 |
| Other expenses | (6,892,408) | — |

The swing is one disclosed item: **a Rs. 6.9bn impairment at the standalone parent**, tied to the committed disposal of Luxury Resort (Pvt) Ltd's Maldives hotel operations — asset purchase agreement signed 16 May 2026 with Bohemia Luxury Resort (Pvt) Ltd for USD 17.25mn, split Rs. 2.9bn impairment of the parent's investment and Rs. 2.5bn of receivables due from the subsidiary. At consolidated level this shows up as a far smaller Rs. 1.23bn discontinued-operations loss, because it's Luxury Resort's own assets being written down there, not the parent's intercompany claim.

**Treated as closed, with one caveat.** The transaction is signed, not yet completed — management expects completion "within the first few months" of the reporting date. If it closes at the agreed price, this is a clean, one-time exit from a loss-making Leisure asset (Leisure sector PBT nearly halved this year, partly because of it) and shouldn't recur. That's the working assumption behind treating FY26's group EPS as broadly clean of this item. If the sale falls through or reprices, that assumption breaks — named explicitly as a floor-risk in Section 6.

The Annual Report's own consolidated Performance Highlights & Ratios table (not the quarterly report's company-level table) gives the real group figures directly, resolving the earlier draft's open question:

| Ratio | FY26 (Group) | FY25 (Group) |
|---|---|---|
| Debt/Equity | **169.89%** (1.70×) | 141.86% (1.42×) |
| Gearing | 62.95% | 58.65% |
| Interest cover | 2.88× | 3.33× |
| Total debt | Rs. 289.66 bn | Rs. 204.41 bn |

**The 3.49× debt/equity in the quarterly investor-info page is the standalone Company ratio, not the Group's** — inflated by the same Maldives impairment that hit standalone equity. Confirmed directly from the Annual Report's own consolidated table, not inferred. Group leverage is 1.70×, up from 1.42× — a real increase, funded partly by the Rights Issue and partly by new debentures, still within an AAA(lka)-rated (Fitch) profile but moving in one direction for two straight years.

**Operating cash flow went sharply negative: Rs. (10.50)bn vs Rs. +17.99bn last year** — receivables +66.4bn, inventories +26.5bn, working capital consuming cash faster than 19% revenue growth alone would explain. This is the file's single biggest unresolved quality-of-earnings flag, and it's the reason the floor below doesn't take FY26's raw EPS at face value.

## 6. Valuation — hurdle method, completed

**Hurdle:** current long T-bond yield ≈ 12.6% → neutral P/E ≈ 7.9×, the multiple at which the stock merely ties the risk-free bond.

**Sustainable EPS:** the ten-year EPS series (3.71 → 0.35 trough → 24.34 crisis-devaluation peak → 18.71 today) shows this business's reported earnings carry a real macro-cyclical (FX/import-substitution) component — 2022–2023 were currency-crisis windfall years, not repeatable operating performance. Using the last five years (2022–2026: 24.34, 21.80, 9.19, 17.91, 18.71) as the mid-cycle window — spanning the peak, the mean-reversion trough, and the current plateau — gives a sustainable base of **~18.4**, close enough to FY26's own 18.71 to confirm the current print isn't another spike.

**Multiple:** cut below the 7.9× neutral for the named risks in Section 5–7 (operating cash flow reversal, leverage rising two years running, 14-segment complexity, unresolved Maldives sale) — but not cut as hard as an insurer-specific single-line risk (JINS), because this is an AAA(lka)-rated, 74-year, genuinely-diversified blue-chip with confirmed operational (not FX) growth in its largest segment. **6.3×.**

**Floor** = 18.4 × 6.3 = 115.9, rounded down → **Rs. 115.**
**Ceiling** = 18.4 × 7.9 (hurdle-neutral) = 145.4, rounded down → **Rs. 140–145.**

**Where the market is:** last traded **215.00**, Q4 high **260.00** — above the ceiling. Q4 EPS grew 4.6% year-on-year (8.19 vs 7.83); the Q4 close price grew 56.9% (137.00 → 215.00) over the same quarter. That's a multiple re-rating, not an earnings re-rating — the same disconnect the system already flagged in JINS and UAL.

## 7. Named risks (the floor's discount, made auditable)

1. **Operating cash flow reversal** — first negative year in the ten-year table; receivables/inventory growth outpacing revenue growth.
2. **Leverage rising two years running** — Group debt/equity 1.42× → 1.70×, gearing 58.65% → 62.95%, interest cover easing 3.33× → 2.88×.
3. **14-segment complexity** — half the segments shrank this year under a headline "record revenue" story; a single multiple compresses real dispersion.
4. **Maldives disposal not yet completed** — signed, not closed; the "clean one-off" reading depends on it completing at the agreed price.
5. **Macro** — the 12.6% hurdle is itself a moving target.
6. **Governance concentration** — K.D.D. Perera holds 51.01% directly plus indirect holdings; 37.09% public float. Not a valuation input, but a position-sizing consideration.

**What would raise the ceiling:** Q1 FY27 operating cash flow converting FY26's profit to cash (working-capital unwind), or a broker-verified SOTP coming in above the earnings-yield ceiling — plausible for a holdco this diversified, the same way JXG's SOTP beat a flat multiple.

**What would lower the floor:** the Maldives sale falling through or repricing, or a third consecutive year of leverage outrunning earnings.
