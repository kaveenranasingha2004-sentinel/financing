## 1. What Hayleys actually is

A 1952-listed holding company atop **14 industry segments plus a residual "Others"**, run federated — "each sector which falls under the purview of a different GMC member is considered a separate segment," Chairman as chief operating decision maker. 202 affiliates group-wide. (Segment names changed in this interim: "Projects and Engineering" is now **Energy, Mobility and Projects**; FY26 figures carry over unchanged.)

Two entities matter for reading these numbers, and they diverge sharply:
- **Consolidated (Group)** — what the market prices.
- **Company (standalone parent)** — issues the listed debentures and pays the dividend from its own cash. **The interim's investor-information page still prints the *Company's* debt/equity (3.28×), not the Group's (1.72×)** — the same trap flagged in July (§5).

**Share base and ownership (changed):** **795,000,000 shares** at 30 June 2026 (750,000,000 at 31 March), after the 3-for-50 rights issue — Rs 9.0bn raised, implied Rs 200 per new share, priced close to market (the disclosed bonus-element factor is only 1.001382). K.D.D. Perera 51.01% (405.55M shares; 405.57M with indirect holdings), Trustees of the D.S. Jayasundera Trust 10.94%, HNB/Phantom Investments 5.40%. **Public holding 37.73%** (from 37.09%), 17,322 shareholders, float-adjusted market cap Rs 71.3bn. ## 6. Valuation — hurdle method, re-derived (hurdle re-anchor + rights-issue re-basing)

**The hurdle moved.** Long T-bond (2037A) secondary-market yield **11.96%** (CBSL, 11-Sep-2026), down from 12.6% (last primary auction, 16 Jul). New neutral P/E = 1 ÷ 0.1196 ≈ **8.36×** (was 7.9×). **Not yet confirmed by a primary auction, and the tape since has been mixed** — T-bill yields rose at two consecutive auctions, while Fitch's 22-Sep upgrade of the sovereign to B− points toward a lower long-end premium over time. Treat as provisional; the reversion case is in the sensitivity below.

**Sustainable EPS — re-based to the current share count.** July's ~18.4 is the 2022–2026 mid-cycle mean (24.34, 21.80, 9.19, 17.91, 18.71 → 18.39), each on the ~750M base — i.e. ≈ Rs 13.8bn of owners' earnings. **On 795M shares that is Rs 17.36.**
- *Proceeds credit — none taken.* Rs 9.0bn of cash arrived in Q1, but the quarter shows Rs 17.9bn of capex and short-term borrowings up Rs 22.5bn; the money was absorbed, not visibly used to retire debt. If it were retiring debt, the carry would be worth roughly +Rs 0.85 a share (Rs 9.0bn × 10.75% × 70% ÷ 795M).
- *Maldives non-recurrence — none taken.* FY26's discontinued loss was Rs 1.23bn (−Rs 1.54 a share on 795M), gone from here. Not credited because the four earlier years in the mid-cycle window can't be cleanly restated ex-Maldives from the documents on hand.
- **Both are documented upside, not adopted** (sensitivity below). Per the standing rule, one quarter and an undisclosed use of proceeds don't move a sustainable-EPS estimate.

**Floor multiple — same proportional haircut, reapplied.** July's 6.3× was 79.4% of the old neutral. Named-risk status this quarter (§7): one resolved (Maldives), two confirmed live (cash conversion; leverage). Resolved and confirmed-live offset — **retention held at 79.4%** → 8.36× × 0.794 = **6.64×**.

| Step | Sustainable EPS | Floor multiple | Floor | Ceiling |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| July file (12.6% hurdle, 750M shares)† | 18.4 | 6.30× | 115.9 | 145.4 |
| Hurdle re-anchor only | 18.4 | 6.64× | 122.1 | 153.8 |
| **Hurdle re-anchor + rights re-basing (adopted)** | **17.36** | **6.64×** | **115.2** | **145.1** |
| Upside case — adds Maldives non-recurrence and proceeds carry (not adopted) | 19.76 | 6.64× | 131.1 | 165.2 |
| Reversion case — hurdle back to 12.6%, re-based EPS | 17.36 | 6.30× | 109.4 | 137.8 |

†July used the rounded 7.9× neutral; here the unrounded 7.94×/8.36× pair is used (0.4% effect, immaterial).

**Floor** = 17.36 × 6.64 ≈ 115.2, rounded down → **Rs. 115.00.**
**Second tranche** = Floor × 0.90 ≈ **Rs. 103.50** — newly stated; this file never carried one (unlike DIPD, ALUM, SINS, SERV, KHL), added per the standing Layer 4 procedure.
**Ceiling** = 17.36 × 8.36 ≈ 145.1, rounded down → **Rs. 144–145.**

The two mechanical effects offset to within 0.6%, so the *numbers* barely move (July: 115 / 140–145) even though both the hurdle and the share base changed underneath them.

## 8. Verdict

**Price: Rs. 238.00** (30-Jun-2026, this interim's own investor-information page; quarterly range 214.00–244.00; market cap Rs 189.0bn; NAV per share Rs 148.27, P/B ≈ 1.61×). Up 10.7% from the Rs 215.00 March close the July file used; no later primary-sourced quote is on file.

**At 238.00: ~64% above the ceiling (144–145), ~107% above the floor (115.00).** July's read was ~51% above the ceiling. The band barely moved; the price did.

**Earnings yield, three ways, against the 11.96% hurdle:**
- On the adopted sustainable EPS (17.36): **7.3%**.
- On trailing-twelve-month owners' profit (Rs 16.11bn ÷ 795M = 20.27): P/E 11.7×, **8.5%**.
- On the most flattering basis, TTM *excluding* discontinued operations (Rs 17.27bn ÷ 795M = 21.73): P/E 11.0×, **9.1%**.
- The company's own 13.04× is Q1 EPS × 4 (18.24) — a run-rate, not a trailing figure.

**Below the hurdle on every basis before any risk adjustment, and above the ceiling under every case in §6 — including the upside case (ceiling 165, price ~44% above it).** The conclusion isn't sensitive to the judgments made.

What it shows: **the parent's price already embeds the subsidiaries' prices — the same three names this vault finds 29%–72% above their own ceilings account for two-thirds of HAYL's market cap.** That is the group-premium hypothesis (LIOC's control-case result in the ALUM→SINS→SERV→LIOC sequence) showing up at the top of the chain rather than being a separate finding.

**Verdict unchanged: WATCHLIST — no position.** Floor 115.00, second tranche 103.50, ceiling 144–145 stand as a complete derivation; the full SOTP remains an upgrade to this number (§Open homework), not a precondition for having one.
