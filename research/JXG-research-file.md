# JXG — Janashakthi PLC — Research File

> **Sources (read 13 Jul 2026):** IPO Prospectus annexures — Independent Valuation Report (Annexure 7), Accountant's Report & Five-Year Summary (KPMG, Annexure 1), Interim FS 31 Dec 2025 (Annexure 4), Embedded Value & Appraisal Value Report for Janashakthi Insurance PLC (Actsure Lab, 30 Sep 2025). **This version folds in the Q1 FY26/27 interim (three months to 30 June 2026, read 04 Oct 2026) — the first full quarter as a listed company, and genuinely eventful.**
> **Position:** 800 shares @ ~9.04 effective, unchanged — the IPO was a primary issuance of 500M new shares to the public, not a rights issue, so existing holders (including this position) weren't diluted in share count, only in ownership percentage.
> **Headline of this update, in order of consequence:** (1) **a new, unpriced acquisition** — JXG agreed to buy Continental Insurance Lanka (a general insurer) from Melstacorp for Rs 5.2 Bn, signed 13-Aug-2026, a subsequent event not yet in these financials; (2) **the IPO's Rs 5.0 Bn landed, and roughly Rs 2.7 Bn of it went straight to paying down standalone holdco debt** — confirmed from the balance sheet, not assumed, which mechanically raises the live-SOTP ceiling; (3) **the quarter itself was a loss** (−Rs 780.8 Mn Group, −Rs 617.1 Mn Company), driven by First Capital Holdings' trading book, the same rate-cycle mechanism the JINS file independently confirmed this update; (4) **a first-ever segment disclosure** lets the SOTP legs be checked against the company's own numbers rather than only against external market caps.

## 1. What the company actually is

JXG is a **pure holding company**. The valuation report says it plainly: it "derives value from its subsidiaries" and has "no other material sources of income or business operations." Three legs, now confirmed by this interim's own Note 4 (Investments in Subsidiaries, equity-method basis):

| Leg | Listed? | Look-through stake (SOTP basis) | Accounting-effective stake (Note 4, confirmed) | What it earns from |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Janashakthi Insurance PLC (JINS.N0000) | Yes | 74.23% | **77.58%** | Life insurance |
| First Capital Holdings PLC (CFVF.N0000) | Yes | 58.02% | **76.57%** | Investment banking / government securities trading |
| Janashakthi Finance PLC (BFN.N0000) | Yes, thin float (~6.5%) | 93.44% | **93.44%** | NBFI — leasing, gold loans, deposits |

Both stake definitions were already documented in the original file (the valuer's cross-holding-adjusted "look-through" figures, used for the live-SOTP market-cap formula to avoid double-counting, and the "accounting effective" figures used for consolidation) — **this interim's Note 4 simply confirms them with current share counts, no discrepancy.**

**Provenance note, newly disclosed:** the pre-IPO 1.8 Bn share count traces to a 2.84-for-1 subdivision effective 31-Dec-2025 (633,551,428 → 1,800,000,000 shares), not previously detailed. The 500M-share IPO on 29-Apr-2026 then took the count to **2,300,000,000**, confirmed.

**The holdco's own balance sheet improved materially this quarter, and this is now measured, not estimated.** Standalone (Company) assets excluding the subsidiary investments themselves: Rs 9.22 Bn; standalone liabilities: Rs 16.00 Bn — **net standalone position −Rs 6.77 Bn**, a genuine improvement from the ~−13.2 Bn figure the original file estimated from Dec-25 prospectus data. The gap is explained directly: Company-level debentures fell 36.2% (Rs 2.03 Bn → Rs 1.29 Bn) and interest-bearing loans fell 13.0% (Rs 15.35 Bn → Rs 13.35 Bn) in a single quarter — **combined standalone debt down 15.7%** — funded by the Rs 5.0 Bn IPO proceeds landing at Company level (confirmed in the cash flow statement: Rs 2.73 Bn went to debt repayment, a further Rs 1.70 Bn into financial assets). **This closes the original file's "use of 5 Bn proceeds" homework item with a real number, not a guess.**

**A second piece of good news embedded in the same data:** the two debenture tranches that were due to mature Mar/Apr-2026 (the original file's flagged refinancing-risk homework, "debentures at 15–16% maturing Mar–Apr 2026/27 — check rollover terms") **have matured and been fully repaid, not rolled over** — both are now absent from Note 6's Jun-26 column. The next refinancing test on the remaining two tranches is now about a year out (Mar-27 and Apr-27).

## 3a. Segmental reporting — a genuinely new disclosure, with one real caveat

This is the **first quarter JXG has disclosed segment results**, and it lets the SOTP legs be checked against the company's own figures rather than only external market caps:

| Segment | Revenue (Q1 FY26/27) | Profit after tax | Segment assets | Segment liabilities |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Life Insurance (≈JINS) | 2,475,340 | **+234,064** | 41,033,790 | 24,278,937 |
| NBFI (≈Janashakthi Finance) | 1,832,133 | **+90,414** | 39,939,202 | 35,841,911 |
| Investment Banking (≈First Capital Holdings) | 2,102,945 | **−689,291** | 90,960,668 | 76,067,715 |
| Other (holdco/corporate) | 78,217 | **−366,496** | 31,317,850 | 16,047,008 |
| Eliminations | (88,457) | (49,458) | (31,840,366) | (2,157,920) |
| **Total** | **6,400,178** | **(780,766)** | **171,411,144** | **150,077,651** |

**Two of four segments were profitable, two lost money.** Investment Banking (First Capital) is by far the largest segment by assets (Rs 91.0 Bn, more than half the Group's total) and posted the biggest loss — directly consistent with the same rate-cycle mechanism the JINS file independently confirmed this update (its own 25% FCH associate stake went from +660 Mn to −289 Mn across H1, worsening in Q2). Back-solving FCH's own total quarterly loss two ways gives a consistent order of magnitude (≈−900 Mn via JXG's 76.57% stake, ≈−578 Mn/quarter average via JINS's 25% stake over H1) — not an exact tie-out across two different ownership routes and periods, but the same story from two independent angles: **First Capital's trading book is the dominant driver of weakness across both JXG and JINS right now.**

**The caveat, stated plainly rather than smoothed over:** a footnote confirms *"Janashakthi Insurance PLC's 31 March 2026 financial information was used for Group consolidation as at 30 June 2026 due to its differing financial year-end (31 December)."* JINS reports on a calendar year (its own "Quarter II 2026" = Jan–Jun); JXG reports to 31 March. **The Life Insurance segment figure above reflects JINS's position as at 31-Mar-2026, a full quarter lagged from JXG's own 30-Jun-2026 reporting date** — it is not directly reconcilable to JINS's own externally-reported Q2 2026 (Apr–Jun) results, and attempts to force a match (e.g., 77.58% × JINS's own Q1 2026 PAT) don't land cleanly, likely because other consolidation adjustments sit inside the segment figure too. **Read the Life Insurance segment number as directionally informative, not as JINS's own quarter restated.**

**"Other" segment lost Rs 366.5 Mn** — likely holdco corporate overhead, and plausibly including one-off listing/IPO costs in a company's first quarter as a public entity, though this isn't disclosed explicitly. Flagged as homework, not assumed.

## 5. Earnings quality — the macro linkage, now confirmed from a second angle this update

First Capital's investment-banking segment remains the largest single profit (or loss) swing factor. This quarter's Group P&L shows it directly: **"Change in fair value of financial assets – FVTPL" swung from +1,256,464 (Q1 FY25/26) to −972,870 (Q1 FY26/27), a −2,229,334 deterioration** — almost certainly FCH's trading book marking down as yields rose, the same mechanism named in the original file ("yields falling → FCH trading gains... yields flat/rising → holdco earnings compress"). **"Securities sold under repurchase agreements" (FCH's repo-funded trading book) grew a further 19.7% in the quarter** (Rs 44.07 Bn → Rs 52.77 Bn) — the book got bigger while it was losing money, which is worth watching rather than alarm, since repo funding of a trading book is the normal business model for a primary dealer, but it does mean the next leg of the rate move (either direction) has a larger base to act on.

**Translation into the five-layer system, unchanged in substance, confirmed in magnitude this quarter:** JXG is a rates-cycle instrument wearing an insurance costume. This update supplies the clearest confirmation yet — the Investment Banking segment's −689 Mn loss, the FVTPL swing, and JINS's own independently-confirmed FCH-associate deterioration are three separate readings of the same underlying event.

**Finance income at Group level jumped from Rs 12.0 Mn to Rs 764.1 Mn — flagged, not explained.** This is large enough to be a real driver of the quarter's result (it's what kept the net loss from being considerably worse) but this document doesn't break out the cause. Plausibly related to the IPO cash sitting in interest-bearing instruments before deployment, or a consolidation/classification effect — **genuine homework, not assumed to be one or the other.**

**Company-level net finance position improved** (−413,979 vs −476,832, a Rs 62.9 Mn narrowing) despite the debt paydown being recent — a small, early, consistent-direction confirmation that the deleveraging is starting to help, with the fuller effect presumably showing in Q2 once a full quarter of lower debt is reflected.

**Two items flagged after JINS's own Q2 2026 read, now checked against this interim:**
- **JINS's zero-dividend finding is confirmed at the JXG end too.** The Company's own investing-activities cash flow shows no dividend-received line this quarter (only finance income of 60,526) — consistent with JINS paying nothing up in H1 2026. Tested against JXG's own holdco cushion: this quarter's standalone debt paydown (§1) was funded by IPO proceeds, not by subsidiary dividends, so the pause didn't bite this quarter specifically — **but it removes a cushion JXG has relied on before** (9M FY26 standalone PBT was loss-making before subsidiary dividends, per the original file), and matters more once the IPO cash is spent down, which the CILL acquisition (§5a) will accelerate.
- **The three-way rate exposure is now visible in the numbers, not just the structure.** A rate-up move hits JXG directly (its own FVTPL swing, −2,229,334 this quarter), via JINS's 25% FCH stake (−289 Mn across H1, per JINS's file), and via JXG's own 76.57% FCH stake (Investment Banking segment, −689,291 this quarter alone) — three separate P&L lines, one underlying driver. Worth remembering as a single risk, not three independent ones, when sizing any future addition to this position.

## 5a. New: the Continental Insurance Lanka acquisition — a fourth leg, unpriced

**Disclosed as a subsequent event (Note 11), not reflected anywhere in these financials.** On 13-Aug-2026, JXG signed a Share Purchase Agreement with **Melstacorp PLC** to acquire **100% of Continental Insurance Lanka Limited** — a licensed general (non-life) insurer, genuinely new territory for a group whose only insurance leg to date (JINS) is pure life — for **Rs 5.2 Bn total consideration**, subject to IRCSL approval. The structure is staged: an initial **81%** controlling stake, then **9%** in year one and **10%** in year two.

**What this means for the file, stated plainly:**
- **Not in the SOTP above** — deliberately. No completion, no IRCSL approval yet, no disclosed financing terms. Adding a speculative value here would be the same mistake the original file corrected the IPO valuer for (confident numbers ahead of confirmed facts).
- **Size:** Rs 5.2 Bn is ~18.7% of JXG's current market cap (Rs 27.83 Bn) — meaningful, not transformational on its own, but a real capital commitment for a holdco that just spent a chunk of its IPO proceeds paying down debt.
- **Strategic read:** diversifies JXG's insurance exposure from pure-life into composite coverage, via the seller being a large, credible conglomerate (Melstacorp) rather than a distressed disposal — a mildly positive signal on deal quality, though price-paid reasonableness hasn't been independently checked here.
- **Financing unknown.** Given the quarter's own debt paydown and still-modest standalone cash position, how the Rs 5.2 Bn (staged) gets funded — fresh debt, further equity, internal cash, or subsidiary dividends — is the single most consequential open question this acquisition raises, because it could partially or fully reverse this update's own debt-paydown finding.

**This is now the file's top homework item**, ahead of the stale CFVF/BFN prices.
