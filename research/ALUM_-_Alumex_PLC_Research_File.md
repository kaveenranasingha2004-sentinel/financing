# ALUM — Alumex PLC — Research File

> **Sources:** Annual Report 2025/26 (year ended 31 March 2026, primary) and Interim Report for Q1 FY2026/27 (period ended 30 June 2026, primary), both read 25 Jul 2026.
> **Position:** none. **Correction embedded in this file's own history:** an earlier screening pass (24-Jul-2026), based only on a third-party earnings summary card, flagged ALUM as the one Hayleys-group name that might *not* be overpriced — its trailing-twelve-month P/E (9.7x) looked far closer to the 12.6% hurdle than HAYL, DIPD, or HAYC. That read was wrong. The primary sources below show the TTM EPS behind that screen was inflated by a fire-insurance settlement. This file replaces that conclusion with the corrected one: ALUM carries the **widest** premium to fair value of any Hayleys-group name researched so far. The reversal is left in, not smoothed over, because the mechanism that caused the original error — trusting a blended TTM figure without checking what's inside it — is worth remembering for every future file in this queue.

## 1. What ALUM actually is

Public limited company, incorporated 2007, listed on the CSE since 31 March 2014. Single reportable segment — the Notes confirm the company does not meet the criteria for multiple operating segments; "all operations are treated as one segment." Core business: manufacture and sale of aluminium extrusions and profiles (construction, industrial, architectural applications), with a growing recycled-aluminium input stream (47% of aluminium input is now recycled, per the ESG highlights).

**Ownership:** Hayleys PLC holds **52.59%** directly (314,826,064 shares) — the fifth Hayleys-group listed subsidiary in this research sequence, alongside DIPD, HAYC, HAYL itself, and (still to come) KHL. Top-20 shareholders hold **79.51%** of the register; real public float is thin, roughly 20%. 8,457 shareholders as at 30 June 2026.

**Geography:** local market is 65% of sales (LKR 11.0bn, +17.3% YoY, "consistent with the country's economic growth"); export sales LKR 5.94bn (+19.6%), driven mainly by Europe (+56.4% to LKR 2.41bn) and Australia (+47.4% to LKR 1.79bn), while America (−27.8%) and Asia ex-Sri Lanka (−30.0%) both declined. A genuinely mixed export picture, not a uniform growth story.

## 3. Quarterly pattern — why a single TTM figure cannot be trusted here

The Annual Report's own Quarterly Analysis table (FY25/26 Company):

| | Q1 (Apr–Jun 25) | Q2 (Jul–Sep 25) | Q3 (Oct–Dec 25) | Q4 (Jan–Mar 26) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue (Rs.'000) | 3,717,753 | 3,919,246 | 3,659,744 | 5,639,743 |
| PBT (Rs.'000) | 30,510 | 67,544 | (66,034) | 1,006,087 |
| EPS (Rs.) | 0.04 | 0.08 | (0.08) | **1.40** |

**Q4 alone produced 97% of the full year's EPS.** The prior fiscal year (FY24/25) was more extreme still: Q1 was a **−1.45 EPS loss quarter**, Q3 was a **+2.14 EPS quarter** — inside the same twelve months, the same business. This is not organic seasonal variation; it is a fire-insurance event moving through the P&L on its own timetable (§4), and it means any TTM EPS blended across these quarters (the "1.66" figure used in the earlier screen) inherits that contamination.

## 4. Earnings quality — the fire, and what it did to two fiscal years

A fire broke out at the company's **Ekala plant**. The Annual Report discloses the P&L consequences directly:

- **FY24/25 ("previous year" in the FY26 report):** other income of **Rs. 1,498.1mn**, of which **Rs. 1,447.0mn was an insurance claim** relating to the fire. Other expenses included a **Rs. 947.3mn fire-damage loss**, also fire-related. **Net one-off pretax gain embedded in FY25 PBT: ≈ Rs. 499.7mn** — against a reported PBT of Rs. 1,140.7mn, roughly 44% of that year's entire pretax profit.
- **FY25/26 (the year just reported):** a smaller echo — **Rs. 211.0mn** business-interruption insurance income against **Rs. 27.4mn** residual fire-related expense. **Net one-off pretax gain: ≈ Rs. 183.6mn** — against reported PBT of Rs. 1,038.1mn, about 18%.

**Stripping both, at each year's own effective tax rate** (FY25: 24.0%; FY26: 16.9%, the latter explicitly attributed by the AR to tax-exempt recycling-plant income and a deferred-tax reversal — a structural, recurring feature, not part of the fire contamination, so left in place):

| Year | Reported PBT | One-off (net) | Adjusted PBT | Adjusted PAT | Adjusted EPS |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| FY24 (yr to Mar-24) | 232.0 | none identified | 232.0 | 186.1 | **0.31** |
| FY25 (yr to Mar-25) | 1,140.7 | +499.7 | 641.0 | 487.2 | **0.81** |
| FY26 (yr to Mar-26) | 1,038.1 | +183.6 | 854.5 | 710.1 | **1.19** |

**Sustainable EPS (3-year average): ≈ Rs. 0.77.**

**Cross-check:** the Q1 FY26/27 interim (the freshest primary data available) shows other income of just Rs. 9.6mn — a clean quarter, no comparable one-off. Annualising its EPS (0.25 × 4) gives **≈ Rs. 1.00** — in the same neighbourhood as the 0.77 estimate above, and both are far below the blended 1.66 the original screen relied on. Two independent methods landing close together is the best confirmation available with only three years of data on file; this remains a **thin series** (same caveat every file in this queue has carried) and should be revisited once FY27's full year is clean of fire-related noise.

**FY24's 0.31 is treated as a genuine trough year, not adjusted further** — no one-off identified, and it predates the fire (which hit FY24/25). Interest cover that year was just 1.27x, consistent with a real, unaided weak year rather than an accounting artefact.

## 5. Balance sheet and leverage — the weakest interest cover in the Hayleys-group queue so far

| Metric | FY26 | FY25 | FY24 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gearing | 61.76% | 64.94% | 62.11% |
| Debt/Equity | 161.52% | 185.25% | 163.95% |
| Interest cover | **2.20×** | 2.52× | 1.27× |
| Current ratio | 1.08 | 1.06 | 1.09 |
| Quick ratio | 0.64 | 0.51 | 0.38 |

Gearing has improved marginally (equity base grew 18% YoY, per the highlights table) but **interest cover at 2.20× is the thinnest of any file researched in this sequence** — below HAYL (2.88×), DIPD, and HAYC. The company's own Financial Capital review is explicit about the composition risk: **76.6% of total debt is short-term**, and short-term borrowings alone fund **59.0% of the entire balance sheet** (down from 62.2% in FY25, but still "indicating continued reliance on short-term financing," in the report's own words). Management states an intention to restructure toward fixed-rate long-term facilities — a real, named mitigant, but not yet executed as at this filing.

**A live, unresolved legal item:** a Rs. 85.1mn tax assessment relating to Alco Industries (Pvt) Ltd, amalgamated into Alumex, was appealed to the Tax Appeal Commission and rejected; it has since been escalated to the **Court of Appeal, with a hearing scheduled for 31 July 2026** — six days after this file was written. Small relative to equity (~2.2%), but genuinely open.

## 6. Valuation — hurdle method

**Hurdle:** long T-bond ≈12.6% → neutral P/E ≈ **7.9×**.

**Sustainable EPS:** Rs. 0.77 (§4), cross-checked against the current quarter's clean annualised run-rate (~1.00).

**Floor multiple — cut for five named risks**, more than DIPD (one) or HAYC (one) required, and with the weakest interest-cover figure of any name in this queue:
1. Interest cover 2.20× — thinnest in the Hayleys-group sequence.
2. 76.6% of debt short-term, funding 59% of the balance sheet — a named, unresolved refinancing-composition risk.
3. Earnings quality genuinely difficult to pin down — three years of data, materially fire-contaminated across two of them.
4. Ownership concentration — Hayleys 52.59% direct, ~20% real float.
5. Live Court of Appeal tax dispute (Rs. 85.1mn).

**Floor multiple: 5.0×** — below HAYC's 5.8×.

**Floor** = 0.77 × 5.0 ≈ Rs. 3.85, rounded down → **Rs. 3.80**
**Second tranche** = Floor × 0.90 → **Rs. 3.40**
**Ceiling** = 0.77 × 7.9 (hurdle-neutral) ≈ Rs. 6.08, rounded down → **Rs. 6.00–6.10**

## 7. Market price and verdict

Last traded price per the Q1 FY26/27 interim's own Investor Information: **Rs. 17.90** (30 June 2026), having ranged 16.00–19.90 over the quarter. Market cap ≈ Rs. 10.7bn.

**At 17.90: ~200% above the ceiling and ~370% above the floor.** This is the **widest premium to fair value of any Hayleys-group name researched in this sequence** — wider than HAYL (~50–65% over ceiling), DIPD (~23%), and HAYC (~83%). The pattern first flagged in HAYL's file — that the market may be paying a structural, group-wide premium for Hayleys-linked industrial names — is not breaking on this name; if anything it strengthens the hypothesis, and raises the stakes for what LIOC (the deliberately unrelated-sector control case) shows next.

**Verdict: WATCHLIST. No position.**

**What would raise the ceiling:** a clean FY26/27 full year confirming the ~1.00–1.19 adjusted run-rate as the new normal rather than a post-fire recovery blip; completion of the stated short-term-to-long-term debt restructuring; continued benefit from the recycling-plant tax exemption (structural, not one-off) holding the effective tax rate near 17%.
**What would lower the floor:** a second consecutive year of heavy reliance on short-term debt without the promised restructuring; an adverse Court of Appeal ruling on the Alco Industries assessment; interest cover falling further from 2.20×.
