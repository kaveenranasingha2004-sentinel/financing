> **One-line thesis:** The larger, older, smoother life insurer — twice AAIC's float, cleaner governance, fatter dividend — but slower growth, lower ROE, and a price that currently *fails* the earnings-yield hurdle that AAIC passes.

## 1. What the company actually is
Life insurer, listed since 1988, ~589.3 Mn shares. **JKH owns exactly 90.0%; public float 10.0%** — the bare CSE minimum (3,286 public holders, up from 2,957 at Dec-25; Option 1 compliance, float-adjusted market cap Rs 4.4 Bn). Governance apparatus is full John Keells: KPMG audit, Willis Towers Watson actuary (same firm as AAIC), dedicated RPT Review Committee, SLFRS-17 Steering Committee. Divested its 22% Fairfirst (general insurance) stake in 2025 — now a pure life play. **New CEO:** Senath Jayatilake, appointed to the board and as CEO with effect from 23 Feb 2026 (nil shareholding).

**The circularity worth smiling at, still growing:** UAL's funds hold Rs 829.9 Mn of JKH shares at 30-Jun (761 Mn cited in July), after **net purchases of Rs 321.8 Mn in H1**; JKH owns 90% of UAL; I own JKH. The group invests in itself, and I invest in the loop.

## 2. The four decisive questions

### Q1 — Portfolio yield & structure → **thesis applies, with less torque than AAIC; mix unchanged**
- AUM **Rs 112.2 Bn** at 30-Jun (+3.6% since Dec-25). Classification: HTM 47.1 Bn (42.0%), L&R 16.3 Bn (14.5%), AFS 36.0 Bn (32.1%), FVTPL 12.8 Bn (11.4%). **Amortised-cost sleeves are 56.5% of the book vs 56.8% at Dec-25 — the mix is essentially static**, unlike JINS's visible de-risking. Underneath, HTM (−2.3%) is rotating into L&R (+22.9%); since the Q1 snapshot FVTPL grew most (+2.1 Bn).
- The government-securities share (87% at Dec-25) isn't disclosed in this interim; the older figure stands.
- Net investment income **+15.9%** (Q2 +19.0%), tracking premium growth (+15%). Nothing here looks like a yield collapse; nothing shows a reinvestment-rate tailwind yet either. (The July file's 18% → 17% → 16% fund-return trend uses the Annual Report's broader definition and can't be compared with this line.)
- **Consequence:** rolling maturities into today's 12%+ is still an improvement on 2025's reinvestment rates but likely below the maturing crisis-era book yield. The rising-rate tailwind is real but weaker than AAIC's.

### Q2 — Float growth → **solid, slightly decelerating**
- GWP: 18,867 → 21,647 → 25,750 Mn (FY25, +19%); **H1'26 13.39 Bn (+15.4%)**; Q1 +16.4%, **Q2 +14.4%** (vs AAIC H1 +32%).
- Conventional life fund 93.44 → **102.80 Bn (+10.0% in six months)**; FY25 grew +19.7%, so the ~20% annualised pace holds. Total insurance contract liabilities Rs 103.1 Bn. The freighter keeps moving.

### Q3 — Persistency → **still weaker disclosure**
- No persistency data of any kind in this interim; the Annual Report's "double-digit LIMRA 13-month persistency growth" (~10–12%) remains the only datapoint, no Y1/Y2/Y3 ladder like AAIC's 86/66/54. Unchanged transparency minus.

### Q4 — Capital → **undisclosed this half — and the equity evidence leans negative**
- Last confirmed CAR: 291 → 264 → **215%** (FY25) vs 120% minimum. **No CAR appears anywhere in this interim** (the same omission as AAIC's and JINS's H1 filings).
- **Total equity fell Rs 4.28 Bn (−18.3%) in six months, from 23.37 to 19.09 Bn** (bridge in §3). Part of that is the planned dividend; part is a rate-driven AFS mark. I can't tell from this document how much of either counts toward RBC available capital, so **this is directional evidence, not a CAR estimate.** It is now the single most important missing number in the file.

**The trend turned positive in Q2**, but read it with care: Q2's recovery coincides with the fair-value swing from a Rs 876 Mn loss in Q1 to a Rs 1.33 Bn gain, and most of that passes through the policyholder fund. This document can't say how much reached shareholders. **Same macro, two accounting doors** (as in July): the FVTPL line bounced while the AFS bond marks kept falling — the life fund's share of AFS marks lost Rs 549 Mn in Q1 and a further **Rs 1,136 Mn in Q2**, consistent with the vault's firming-rate read.

**Operating efficiency genuinely improved.** As a share of net written premium: claims paid 32.4% (33.3%), acquisition costs 18.2% (20.8%), other opex 23.6% (24.0%). Total claims and expenses 77.5% of NWP vs 81.5% — **four points better**, because costs grew 9% against premium growth of 15%. This is the part of the business the market-linked noise isn't touching.

**Equity bridge, Dec-25 to Jun-26 (−Rs 4,282 Mn, ties exactly):** profit +504; AFS marks on the company's own book −173; the life fund's share of AFS marks −1,686; final dividend for 2025 −2,946; share-based payments +18. Reported **NAV/share fell from 39.66 to 32.40 (−18.3%).** Note the split in the filing's own segment table: **shareholder-fund equity Rs 15.75 Bn (26.73/share)** vs **life-fund reserves Rs 3.34 Bn (5.67/share)**, the latter down 34% on the AFS mark. Life-fund reserves sit inside reported equity, but their route to shareholders runs through the annual surplus transfer; read headline P/B (2.30×) with that in mind (2.79× on shareholder-fund equity alone).

**What H1 cannot tell us: the Q4 transfer.** FY25 PAT of 3,370 Mn rested on a Rs 3,400 Mn surplus transfer (2,800 → 3,000 → 3,400) booked at the year-end valuation; H1 carries none of it. Rough FY26 arithmetic (mine, not a forecast): FY25's shareholder-fund result ex-associate was ~Rs 1.17 Bn; H1'26 is running −20%, which implies ~Rs 1.0 Bn for FY26. Add the transfer, tax at 30%: a **flat** Rs 3.4 Bn gives EPS ≈ 5.21; **+10%** ≈ 5.61; **+13%** (last year's growth) ≈ 5.74. Every Rs 340 Mn of transfer is worth ~Rs 0.40 per share. The result is decided in February–March, not by anything in this interim.

**Layer 3a checks.** *Cash vs profit:* operating cash flow Rs 8.05 Bn (+18%) against PAT Rs 0.50 Bn — for a life insurer that gap is the float (the life-fund increase is inside operating cash), so it is structural, not a quality signal. The informative cash line is the Rs 2.95 Bn dividend paid out of last year's transfer. *Moat:* a statements-only interim has no management review to score; nothing added.

## 4. Governance & related-party — the clean twin, mostly confirmed
- Parent is JKH: no stressed-holdco extraction risk. **Final dividend for 2025 of Rs 5.00/share (Rs 2,946 Mn) paid again — DPS flat for a second year; Rs 2,651.8 Mn of it flowed to JKH, identical to last year.** *(Cross-note for the JKH file: UAL is a reliable ~2.65 Bn/yr cash spring for the leveraged parent — confirmed again in H1.)*
- **The dividend's cover is thinning.** Payout ≈ 79% of FY24 EPS (~6.35, approximate from the reported −10%), 87% of FY25's 5.72, **~92% of the adopted 5.43 and ~96% of trailing 5.22.** With equity down 18% and CAR undisclosed, the next declaration (Feb–Mar 2027) is the pivotal event in this file.
- **Contingency moved (favourably, not finally).** Previously "no change disclosed." Now: the Court of Appeal ruled for UAL on Y/A 2010/11, 2011/12 and 2015/16, allowing it to deduct all management expenses against life-business investment income; **the Inland Revenue Department has appealed to the Supreme Court.** No amount is disclosed here. It is the same type of issue AAIC's file tracks (item 6, COA in favour on expense matters, CGIR to the Supreme Court), so a Supreme Court reversal would be sector-wide, not UAL-specific.
- Related-party lines remain boring in the best way: shared services fees, small group debentures, nil director holdings.
- **SoAT discrepancy worth verifying.** UAL's note says the SoAT relief covers "the first two quarters of 2026" (AAIC's wording is similar); **JINS's note says the first, second and third quarters.** If UAL's reading is right, its **Q3 2026 interim (Nov) is the first SLFRS 17/9 print**, one quarter earlier than this file has assumed. I can't verify the SoAT text from these documents; carried to homework.
- Transition note unchanged and still the best in the sector: GMM + VFA, fair-value transition approach, insurance finance income/expense split between P&L and OCI, "substantially ready for go-live," validation ongoing and no quantitative disclosure yet.

## 5. Valuation (Layer 4) — re-derived (EPS correction + new hurdle)

**Hurdle:** long T-bond (2037A) secondary-market midpoint **12.17%** (CBSL, 25-Sep-2026; up from 11.96% two weeks earlier), neutral P/E **8.22×** (was 7.94× at 12.6%). Provisional until the next primary auction.

**Sustainable EPS: 5.72 → 5.43.** Step 2 asks whether each line would recur. Fairfirst income fails that test, and H1 2026 now shows it at zero. I remove the H1'25 figure alone, Rs 170.8 Mn (0.29 per share, already net of tax), as a *minimum*: the Annual Report's full-year figure should be a little larger, and this document doesn't contain it. Cross-checks bracket 5.43 sensibly: the **trailing-twelve-month EPS is 5.22** (5.72 − 1.36 + 0.86, and already ex-associate); the flat-transfer scenario gives 5.21; transfer growth of 10–13% gives 5.6–5.7. Market-linked items I leave in at their H1 run-rate; the downside from those is carried as a risk, not subtracted again.

**Floor multiple — same proportional premium as July, reapplied.** July's floor (55 on 5.72 = 9.62×) sat deliberately *above* neutral, a 21% premium argued on three named grounds: earnings understated by conservatism, a real dividend, clean governance. **H1 weakens the first two** (the "understatement" is smaller once Fairfirst is out; dividend cover is thinner) and doesn't touch the third; I hold the premium rather than raise or cut it, because a single half-year isn't a trigger. Reapplied to 8.22×: **9.96×.**

| Basis | Sustainable EPS | Floor | Second tranche (×0.90) | Ceiling |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| July file (12.6% hurdle) | 5.72 | 55 | 50 | 78–80 |
| Flat-transfer / TTM case | 5.21–5.22 | 51.9 | 46.7 | 73.5–75.4 |
| **Adopted (12.17% hurdle)** | **5.43** | **54.00** | **48.50** | **76.50–78.50** |
| July EPS on new hurdle | 5.72 | 56.9 | 51.2 | 80.8–82.8 |

**The ceiling's basis is a hybrid — flagging it plainly.** The pure-method ceiling (sustainable EPS ÷ hurdle) would be 5.43 × 8.22 = **Rs 44.6**; the pure floor sits below that. July deliberately set 78–80 above it to respect the observed trading zone (Q1 high 83.20; this quarter's high 82.00). I've scaled it the same way as the other files so the method stays consistent, but the premium is real: **the adopted floor is 21% above hurdle-neutral value, and the ceiling is ~1.7× the pure-method ceiling.** Whether to keep paying that premium for JKH-quality governance is a judgment call that belongs to me, not to the formula (homework).

**At 74.50 (30-Jun last traded; range 69.00–82.00, vs 70.00 at the Q1 snapshot):**
- Market cap Rs 43.9 Bn; NAV/share 32.40, **P/B 2.30×** (2.05× in July).
- **Trailing P/E 14.3× on 5.22; earnings yield 7.0%** (7.3% on the adopted 5.43; July's read was 8.2% at 70). Against 12.17%, the gap is ~5 points and **wider than in July**: price rose 6.4% while the earnings base fell ~5%.
- Dividend yield **6.7%** on a flat Rs 5.00, ~96% of trailing earnings.
- **~38% above the floor (54.00), 2.6–5.1% below the ceiling (76.50–78.50).** Under July's band it was 35% above the floor and 4.5–6.9% under the ceiling.

**Verdict: WATCHLIST — no position, nothing actionable.** The stock isn't near the floor, so no entry and no portfolio-cluster check is triggered. If it ever neared the floor, UAL would join JXG/JINS in the rate-cycle-insurer cluster *and* double the JKH look-through, so the cap would apply twice.

**What would raise the numbers:** the Q4 transfer growing at last year's +13% *and* shareholder-fund income stabilising; a CAR disclosure showing real headroom after the H1 equity drop; the SLFRS 17 restatement showing a large CSM.
**What would lower them:** a flat or lower surplus transfer (EPS ≈ 5.2 or below, ceiling toward 73–75); a dividend cut; CAR drifting toward the 120% floor; the 12.17% hurdle drifting up at a primary auction; an adverse Supreme Court outcome on expense deductibility.
