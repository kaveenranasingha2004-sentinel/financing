# UAL — Union Assurance PLC — Research File

> **Sources:** Annual Report 2025 (400 pp), Q1 2026 interim (31 Mar 2026), read 15 Jul 2026.
> **Position:** none directly — but **look-through exposure exists via JKH (90.0% parent)**.
> **One-line thesis:** The larger, older, smoother life insurer — twice AAIC's float, cleaner governance, fatter dividend — but slower growth, lower ROE, and a price that currently *fails* the earnings-yield hurdle that AAIC passes.

---

## 1. What the company actually is
Life insurer, listed since 1988, ~589.3 Mn shares. **JKH owns exactly 90.0%; public float 10.0%** — the bare CSE minimum. Governance apparatus is full John Keells: KPMG audit, Willis Towers Watson actuary (same firm as AAIC), dedicated RPT Review Committee, SLFRS-17 Steering Committee. Divested its 22% Fairfirst (general insurance) stake in 2025 — now a pure life play.

**The circularity worth smiling at:** UAL's shareholder fund holds Rs. 761 Mn of JKH shares; JKH owns 90% of UAL; I own JKH. The group invests in itself, and I invest in the loop.

## 2. The four decisive questions

### Q1 — Portfolio yield & structure → **thesis applies, with less torque than AAIC**
- AUM Rs. 108 Bn (1.8× AAIC), **87% government securities** (min 30). Classification: HTM 46.9 Bn + L&R 15.5 Bn (amortised cost, MTM-immune) vs AFS 34.9 Bn (OCI) + **FVTPL 10.7 Bn (P&L-exposed — see §4)**.
- Investment yield: 18% (2023) → 17% (2024) → **16% (2025)** — declining, but from a *higher* base than AAIC (14.9%). UAL locked long during the 2022–23 crisis-yield era; the AR states plainly that lower reinvestment yields reduced fund return in 2025.
- **Consequence:** rolling maturities into today's 12.6% is an *improvement on 2025's reinvestment rates* but still likely below the maturing crisis-era book yield. The rising-rate tailwind is real but weaker than AAIC's — AAIC's yield already reset lower and its faster-growing float compounds new money at new rates harder.

### Q2 — Float growth → **solid, half AAIC's pace**
- GWP: 18,867 → 21,647 → **25,750 Mn (+19%)**; Q1'26 +16% (vs AAIC +30%).
- Life fund 92.8 Bn, **+20% in 2025**; grew another 3.35 Bn in Q1'26. The engine is healthy — it's a freighter next to AAIC's speedboat.

### Q3 — Persistency → **weaker disclosure, adequate signal**
- UAL reports only "double-digit LIMRA 13-month persistency growth" (~10–12%) — no Y1/Y2/Y3 ladder like AAIC's 86/66/54. Improving direction, less granularity. Mark as a small transparency minus.

### Q4 — Capital → **compliant, declining — and LOWER than AAIC's**
- CAR: 291 → 264 → **215%** vs 120% minimum. The "conservative twin" assumption was half-wrong: UAL runs *thinner* regulatory capital than AAIC (245%). Both declining. Determination ratio surplus 15.3 Bn.

## 3. Profitability & earnings character
- FY2025: NPAT 3,370 Mn (−10%), EPS 5.72, **ROE 14%** (vs AAIC 39%). Annual surplus transfer 3,400 Mn (13% growth, smooth: 2,800 → 3,000 → 3,400).
- Claims ratio 33% of NWP, expense ratios flat at 23% — stable, unspectacular, well-run.
- **Q1'26 PAT −48% (266 Mn) — mostly accounting geography, not operations:**
  1. **No Q1 surplus transfer** — UAL transfers annually at the Q4 actuarial valuation (AAIC transfers quarterly: its 720 Mn Q1 transfer vs UAL's zero makes Q1 EPS comparison between the two meaningless).
  2. **Fair value losses 876 Mn through P&L** — the 10.7 Bn FVTPL book marks rising yields straight into the income statement (AAIC routes bond MTM through OCI). Same macro event, different accounting door.
  3. 2025 base included Fairfirst associate income (85 Mn) + realised gains (283 Mn) — both gone post-divestiture.
  - Meanwhile the life fund grew 3.35 Bn in the quarter. Underlying engine: fine.

## 4. Governance & related-party — the clean twin, confirmed
- Parent is JKH: no stressed-holdco extraction risk; dividend of 2,946 Mn (Rs. 5.00/share) paid steadily — **2,652 Mn of it flows to JKH annually**. *(Cross-note for the JKH file: UAL is a reliable ~2.65 Bn/yr cash spring for the leveraged parent — one of the quiet supports of JKH's finance-cost leg.)*
- Related-party lines are boring in the best way: shared services fees, small group debentures. Directors hold nil shares.
- Same SoAT deferral as AAIC (SLFRS 4 for Q1–Q2 2026), but **richer transition disclosure**: GMM + VFA models, fair-value transition approach, election to split insurance finance income between P&L and OCI to damp rate volatility, "substantially ready for go-live." UAL's transition note is the Rosetta stone for reading both companies when restatements land.

## 5. Valuation (Layer 4) — DRAFT
- At Q1-end price **70.00**: trailing P/E 12.2, **earnings yield 8.2% — FAILS the 12.6% long-bond hurdle**. (AAIC at 81.40: P/E 5.2, e/y 19.1% — passes.) On stated earnings, UAL is priced like the safety is free.
- Dividend anchor: DPS 5.00 held flat → 7.1% yield at 70. Real, covered (payout ~87% of EPS — high; sustained by smooth surplus transfers), but a 7.1% dividend competes with a 12.6% government bond.
- NAV/share 34.09 → P/B 2.05 at 70. Book is the wrong anchor for insurers, but the smoothed 14% ROE doesn't obviously justify 2× book when the risk-free pays 12.6%.
- **Floor 55 (draft):** e/y ~10.4%, dividend yield 9.1% — the price where UAL's smoothness plausibly compensates the residual gap to the hurdle. **Second tranche 50.** **Ceiling 78–80** (Q1 high was 83.20; above ~78 the e/y drops toward 7% and the position is donating the hurdle spread).
- Last known price 70.00 (31 Mar) — **between levels: watchlist, no order.**
