# LIOC — Lanka IOC PLC — Research File

> **Sources:** Annual Report 2025-26 (244pp, primary, year ended 31 March 2026) and Financial Statements for Q1 FY26/27 (three months ended 30 June 2026, primary), both read 04 Aug 2026.
> **Position:** none. **This is the deliberately-last file in the ALUM→SINS→SERV→LIOC sequence, and it exists to answer one specific question, not to be researched on its own merits alone:** every Hayleys-linked name completed so far — HAYL, DIPD, HAYC, ALUM, SINS (industrial) and now SERV (hospitality) — has traded 23% to over 200% above its hurdle-rate ceiling. LIOC is genuinely unrelated: **75.12% owned by Indian Oil Corporation Limited, India** — no Hayleys, no Carbotels, no shared board or shareholder cluster with anything else in this vault. If LIOC shows the same extreme premium, the "Hayleys-group premium" hypothesis weakens toward "the whole market is priced this way." If it doesn't, that's real evidence the premium is specifically tied to Hayleys-linked ownership. **The result below is a clean, interpretable answer — see §8.**

## 1. What LIOC actually is

Public limited company, incorporated 2002, commenced commercial operations 14 February 2003, listed on the CSE since 2004-05. **Single reportable segment** — the Annual Report states plainly: *"The Company is dealing only in Petroleum products. There are no separate activities other than the petroleum segment in the Company."* Product lines span retail fuel, lubricants (including an export-oriented manufacturing operation at Trincomalee), bitumen, bunker/marine fuel, greases, and a newer petrochemicals line (PROPEL brand, polymer products).

**Ownership: Indian Oil Corporation Limited (India) holds 75.12%** (400,000,005 of 532,465,705 shares) — the single largest, most concentrated controlling stake of any name researched in this vault, and structurally different in kind from every Hayleys-linked file: this is a foreign multinational parent, not a local conglomerate with cross-holdings across a CSE-listed group. **Public holding 24.88%, 17,215 shareholders.** Directors hold nil shares except the Managing Director and one other director, both nil per the disclosed table (confirmed clean).

**A structurally important minority holding, not fully controlled despite its size:** LIOC owns **33⅓% of Ceylon Petroleum Storage Terminal Limited (CPSTL)**, the "Common User Facility," carried at fair value through OCI (Rs. 7,483,000k, ~16% of total non-current assets). The Ceylon Petroleum Corporation (government) owns the remaining 66⅔% and controls six of nine board seats; LIOC nominates the other three. The Annual Report states directly that **LIOC's nominated directors "do not have significant influence over decisions of CPSTL"** due to government influence and industry sensitivity — a real governance limitation over a materially-sized asset, flagged explicitly rather than assumed away.

Separately, LIOC holds a **49% stake in Trinco Petroleum Terminal (Private) Limited** (TPTL), a joint venture with CPC (51%) formed under a 50-year lease (signed 2021-22) for 61 tanks at the Upper Tank Farm, Trincomalee — infrastructure-access economics, not a trading operation in its own right.

**Read this carefully — it's the central methodological question this file has to resolve.** FY2022-23 is an extreme outlier: nearly identical revenue to FY2025-26 (281.5bn vs 280.7bn) but EPS **3.6× higher** (70.79 vs 19.84) and GP margin more than double (19% vs 8%). This was the global oil-price-spike/Sri Lanka economic-crisis year — an exceptional trading-margin windfall, not repeatable operating performance. **Using it as a base year would badly overstate sustainable earnings power**, the same trap the Layer 4 method's own guidance warns against for commodity/cyclical businesses ("use mid-cycle earnings, never peak"). FY2021-22, symmetrically, is a genuine crisis trough (import-restriction-era Sri Lanka) and shouldn't anchor a floor either.

**The three "normal" years (2023-24 through 2025-26) show a real, declining trend**, not just noise: EPS 26.19 → 20.94 → 19.84, a genuine −24.3% decline over two years, on GP margin compressing from 9% to 8%. This is the named risk carried into §7 — not explained away, priced into the multiple.

**No file completed in this sequence comes close to this leverage profile.** Debt/equity of 2% (vs ALUM's 161%, SINS's 3.4× net-debt-to-equity, even HAYL's 1.70×) and interest cover of 26× are not "the best in the Hayleys queue" — they're an entirely different risk category, reflecting an asset-light distribution business with minimal capex needs (PP&E only Rs. 5.3bn against total assets of Rs. 127.9bn) sitting on a large accumulated investment book instead of debt-funded growth.

**That investment book is itself a major, real finding:** Long-term + short-term investments totalled **Rs. 59.16bn at FYE** (up from Rs. 44.92bn the year before) — larger than the company's entire PP&E base by more than 11×. Composition per the notes: portfolio-management-service holdings, unit trusts (Rs. 10.7bn), and bank deposits, with a clear FY26 shift from short-term into long-term buckets (long-term investments roughly doubled, 15.4bn→31.9bn, while short-term fell 29.5bn→27.3bn) — **the same "locking in the ~12.6% long-bond yield window" behaviour already flagged as a macro-dashboard theme and independently confirmed in DIPD's own treasury notes.** Finance income of Rs. 5.33bn in FY26 (dominated by "income from investments and deposits," Rs. 5.18bn) is a real, structurally recurring contributor to PBT — not a one-off — funded by decades of retained earnings never fully paid out (dividend payout ratio only 19-20% across the last three years).

## 4. Tax — a genuine, structural concessionary regime, not a one-off

The tax reconciliation note applies a **statutory rate of 15%**, not Sri Lanka's standard ~30% corporate rate — a BOI-linked concession tied to LIOC's original agreement and stated 10-year tax exemption structure. After add-backs (non-deductible expenses, income taxed at different rates), the **actual effective P&L tax rate came to 22.1% (FY26) and 19.6% (FY25)** — recurring in both years, so treated as a genuine structural feature of the business, not stripped as a one-off. Worth flagging as a policy-dependency risk (§7) rather than a current earnings-quality problem: if the concessionary regime narrows or expires, the tax line moves against the company, but nothing in the primary sources suggests that's imminent.

**Contingencies — resolved cleanly, not live:** an NBT dispute spanning several assessment periods was found in LIOC's favour by the Tax Appeals Commission; the Inland Revenue Department's appeal to the Supreme Court was refused leave in July 2025, and the case is now concluded in LIOC's favour (contingent liability of Rs. 549.15mn already recognised as a provision, not an open exposure). A separate Social Security Contribution Levy exemption on petrol/diesel sales (effective 1 July 2025) is a genuine, real tailwind, not yet fully reflected in a full fiscal year of results.

## 5. Q1 FY26/27 interim — a genuinely mixed quarter, margin pressure offset by cost control and tax

| Rs.'000 | Q1 FY26/27 (Apr-Jun 2026) | Q1 FY25/26 (Apr-Jun 2025) | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 92,107,073 | 62,538,420 | **+47.3%** |
| Gross profit | 3,790,257 | 4,796,481 | **−21.0%** |
| **GP margin** | **4.1%** | **7.7%** | **−3.6 pts** |
| Selling & distribution expenses | (1,903,577) | (2,320,908) | −18.0% |
| Operating profit | 1,867,205 | 1,093,947 | **+70.7%** |
| Finance income | 1,329,112 | 1,147,603 | +15.8% |
| PBT | 2,905,277 | 2,069,111 | +40.4% |
| Tax | (541,815) | (582,252) | −7.0% |
| **PAT** | **2,323,025** | 1,527,296 | **+52.1%** |
| EPS (Rs.) | 4.36 | 2.87 | +52.0% |

**Genuinely two different stories pulling in opposite directions.** Revenue surged 47% — most plausibly driven by rising fuel unit prices during a quarter that sat squarely inside the Brent price spike to ~$88-100/bbl already tracked on the macro dashboard (Israel-Iran/Middle East conflict period), not confirmed volume growth (no volume data disclosed in the interim). **But gross margin compressed sharply (7.7%→4.1%)** — consistent with import costs rising faster than the domestic retail pricing formula could pass through, the structural risk named in §2. Despite that, **PAT rose 52%**, because (a) selling & distribution costs fell 18%, (b) finance income kept growing on the large investment book, and (c) the effective tax rate fell (18.7% vs 28.1% Q1 prior year, plausibly reflecting the SSCL exemption or income-mix effects).

**Read plainly: the headline PAT growth this quarter is real, but it's not a margin story — it's opex discipline and treasury income compensating for a genuinely weaker core trading margin.** This is the single most important thing to watch in Q2: does gross margin recover as pricing catches up, or does this compression persist as a new normal.

**A data-quality note, in the same spirit as prior files' price-source flags:** the interim's own Statement of Changes in Equity shows figures that don't reconcile cleanly against the Income Statement's own period labels (a stray "1,649,000" figure appears where Stated Capital, confirmed elsewhere at a constant Rs. 7,576,574k, should be). The Income Statement and Balance Sheet cross-check consistently with each other and with the EPS/NAV figures independently disclosed elsewhere in the same document, so this file relies on those, and flags the equity-statement inconsistency as a source-document quality issue rather than a real financial event.

## 7. Valuation — hurdle method

**Hurdle:** long T-bond ≈12.6% → neutral P/E ≈ **7.9×**.

**Sustainable EPS: ≈ Rs. 22.30** — the 3-year average of FY2023-24 through FY2025-26 (26.19, 20.94, 19.84), explicitly excluding FY2022-23's windfall peak and FY2021-22's crisis trough as unrepresentative per §2. **The declining trend within this window (−24% over 2 years) is real and is priced into the multiple below, not smoothed out of the EPS estimate.**

**Floor multiple — five named risks, offset by exceptional balance-sheet quality:**
1. **Three-year declining EPS trend**, GP margin structurally thin (8-9%) and further compressed in the latest quarter (down to 4.1%) — a real, ongoing margin risk, not a one-off.
2. **Direct exposure to oil-price volatility as a core input cost**, not just a macro/inflation pass-through the way it is for most other names in this vault — the Middle East conflict and Brent price swings already tracked on the macro dashboard hit this company's margins directly and immediately.
3. **CPSTL governance limitation** — ~16% of non-current assets (Rs. 7.48bn) sits in a 33⅓%-owned entity LIOC's own Annual Report states it does not have significant influence over.
4. **Concessionary tax-regime dependency** — a real, structural 15% statutory base rate; any policy change narrows this benefit.
5. **Extreme historical earnings volatility** — a 5-year EPS range spanning 9.05 to 70.79 (7.8×) is wider than anything else researched in this vault, and the 3-year "normal" window used here still hasn't been tested through a genuine severe down-cycle.

**Offsetting, and unusually strong:** debt/equity 2%, interest cover 26×, current ratio 2.3×, a Rs. 59bn liquid investment book, a resolved (not live) legal contingency, and a genuine regulatory tailwind (SSCL exemption). This balance-sheet quality is materially better than any other file in this sequence and earns a real offset in the multiple, even against five named risks.

**Floor multiple: 5.8×** — same tier as HAYC (single dominant risk, offset by scale/quality), reflecting five named risks that are individually real but collectively offset by the strongest balance sheet in this vault.

**Floor** = 22.30 × 5.8 ≈ Rs. 129.3, rounded down → **Rs. 129.00.**
**Second tranche** = Floor × 0.90 ≈ **Rs. 116.00.**
**Ceiling** = 22.30 × 7.9 (hurdle-neutral) ≈ Rs. 176.2, rounded down → **Rs. 176.00.**

**Sensitivity check, given the EPS estimate involves a judgment call:** using just the latest single year (EPS 19.84) gives floor ≈115.00 / ceiling ≈156.50; using a 2-year average (EPS 20.39) gives floor ≈118.00 / ceiling ≈161.00. **Across every reasonable EPS assumption, the conclusion in §8 holds** — current price sits inside the band, not dramatically above it — so this file's headline finding isn't an artifact of one specific averaging choice.

## 8. Market price and verdict — the control case answers cleanly

**Last traded: Rs. 141.50** (Q1 FY26/27 interim). **At 141.50: trailing P/E ≈ 7.1× on the 3-year sustainable EPS estimate, earnings yield ≈ 15.8%** — genuinely close to, and above, the 12.6% hurdle rate, before any risk adjustment.

**Rs. 141.50 sits between the floor (129.00) and the ceiling (176.00) — inside the band.** This is the **first name in the entire ALUM→SINS→SERV→LIOC sequence to land inside its own valuation range** rather than trading well above the ceiling. Every Hayleys-linked name researched — HAYL (~50-65% over ceiling), DIPD (~23%), HAYC (~83%), ALUM (~200%), SINS (~50%), SERV (~150%) — has traded at a substantial, sometimes extreme, premium. LIOC, wholly unrelated to that ownership cluster, does not.

**This is a clean, interpretable result for the question this file was built to answer.** It doesn't prove the Hayleys-group premium is *caused* by Hayleys-linked ownership specifically (one control case is suggestive evidence, not proof, and LIOC differs from every other name in sector, ownership structure, and business model simultaneously — plenty of alternative explanations remain possible). But it is real evidence against the alternative hypothesis that the whole CSE industrial/energy space is uniformly priced 50-200% above hurdle-rate fair value right now. **The premium looks tied to something specific about the Hayleys-linked names, not the market as a whole.**

**Verdict: WATCHLIST. No position — price sits inside the band, closer to the floor (129.00, ~9.7% below current) than any other name in this sequence has been to its own floor**, but not yet at it. No mechanical buy signal per the system's own rules (floor must be touched, not approached).

**What would raise the ceiling:** Q2 FY26/27 confirming gross margin recovers toward FY24-26's 8-9% norm rather than settling near Q1's 4.1%; greater clarity on the remaining tax-concession runway; a genuine down-cycle test showing the 3-year "normal" EPS window holds up.
**What would lower the floor:** gross margin compression persisting into a second consecutive quarter; any adverse change to the concessionary tax regime; a material CPSTL-related governance or dividend-flow disruption.
