## 1. What LIOC actually is

Public limited company, incorporated 2002, commenced commercial operations 14 February 2003, listed on the CSE since 2004-05. **Single reportable segment** — the Annual Report states plainly: *"The Company is dealing only in Petroleum products. There are no separate activities other than the petroleum segment in the Company."* Product lines span retail fuel, lubricants (including an export-oriented manufacturing operation at Trincomalee), bitumen, bunker/marine fuel, greases, and a newer petrochemicals line (PROPEL brand, polymer products).

**Ownership: Indian Oil Corporation Limited (India) holds 75.12%** (400,000,005 of 532,465,705 shares) — the single largest, most concentrated controlling stake of any name researched in this vault, and structurally different in kind from every Hayleys-linked file: this is a foreign multinational parent, not a local conglomerate with cross-holdings across a CSE-listed group. **Public holding 24.88%, 17,215 shareholders.** Directors hold nil shares except the Managing Director and one other director, both nil per the disclosed table (confirmed clean).

**A structurally important minority holding, not fully controlled despite its size:** LIOC owns **33⅓% of Ceylon Petroleum Storage Terminal Limited (CPSTL)**, the "Common User Facility," carried at fair value through OCI (Rs. 7,483,000k, ~16% of total non-current assets). The Ceylon Petroleum Corporation (government) owns the remaining 66⅔% and controls six of nine board seats; LIOC nominates the other three. The Annual Report states directly that **LIOC's nominated directors "do not have significant influence over decisions of CPSTL"** due to government influence and industry sensitivity — a real governance limitation over a materially-sized asset, flagged explicitly rather than assumed away.

Separately, LIOC holds a **49% stake in Trinco Petroleum Terminal (Private) Limited** (TPTL), a joint venture with CPC (51%) formed under a 50-year lease (signed 2021-22) for 61 tanks at the Upper Tank Farm, Trincomalee — infrastructure-access economics, not a trading operation in its own right.

## 7. Valuation — re-derived under the new hurdle rate

**The hurdle moved.** Per the macro dashboard's 11-Sep-2026 update, the long T-bond (2037A) **secondary-market yield now reads 11.96%**, down from the 12.6% last confirmed at the 16-Jul-2026 primary auction. This is a CBSL secondary-market quote, **not yet confirmed by a fresh primary auction** — treat this re-derivation as current-best-estimate, to be re-checked when the next long-bond auction result lands (see next-trigger).

**New neutral P/E = 1 ÷ 0.1196 ≈ 8.36×** (was 7.9×).

**Sustainable EPS: held at Rs. 22.30** — unchanged, since no new financial data entered this file this update (§§1–6 are the same facts as 04-Aug-2026). The 3-year average (26.19, 20.94, 19.84, ex-windfall, ex-crisis) stands as before.

**Floor multiple — re-derived, not mechanically scaled.** The original 5.8× floor multiple represented a **73.1% retention of the old 7.9× neutral** (i.e., a ~26.9% aggregate haircut for the five named risks — declining EPS trend, direct oil-price exposure, the CPSTL governance limitation, tax-concession dependency, extreme historical volatility — offset by exceptional balance-sheet quality). **None of those five risks, nor the offsetting balance-sheet strength, changed this update** — only the risk-free comparator moved. Holding the same 73.1% retention against the new 8.36× neutral gives a like-for-like floor multiple of **6.11×**, materially more defensible than a flat ×1.058 rescale of the old dollar figures, because it re-applies the same risk *judgment* to the new anchor rather than just inflating the old output.

**Floor** = 22.30 × 6.11 ≈ Rs. 136.3, rounded down → **Rs. 136.00.**
**Second tranche** = Floor × 0.90 ≈ **Rs. 122.50** (rounded to the nearest 0.50, consistent with this file's existing precision).
**Ceiling** = 22.30 × 8.36 (new hurdle-neutral) ≈ Rs. 186.5, rounded down → **Rs. 186.00.**

**This remains the cleanest result in the ALUM→SINS→SERV→LIOC sequence** — LIOC is still the only name that ever traded inside its own band rather than well above it, and it's now the only name in that sequence with an actual position taken, at a genuine discount to the floor rather than merely close to it.

**Verdict: HOLD.** First tranche taken. Per the standing buy rules (max two tranches: floor, floor −10%), **the second tranche (122.50) remains available** if price falls further and the thesis stays intact — not a target to chase, just the pre-written next level. **Sell rules stay the four-item list only:** thesis broken by a named trigger (gross-margin compression failing to recover past a second consecutive quarter, an adverse tax-regime change, a CPSTL disruption), ceiling reached (186.00), a demonstrably better opportunity, or a genuine cash need. Price falling further, on its own, is not one of them — it's what the second tranche exists for.

**What would raise the ceiling:** Q2 FY26/27 confirming gross margin recovers toward FY24-26's 8-9% norm; greater clarity on the remaining tax-concession runway; a genuine down-cycle test of the 3-year "normal" EPS window.
**What would lower the floor / break the thesis:** gross margin compression persisting into a second consecutive quarter; any adverse change to the concessionary tax regime; a material CPSTL-related governance or dividend-flow disruption.
**What would move this whole re-derivation:** the next long-bond primary auction confirming, contradicting, or further moving the 11.96% secondary-market read this position's floor rests on — if it prints materially above 11.96%, the floor this trade was measured against moves down, and the position needs re-checking against the revised number, not the one it was bought under.
