## 1. What LIOC actually is

Public limited company, incorporated 2002, commenced commercial operations 14 February 2003, listed on the CSE since 2004-05. **Single reportable segment** — the Annual Report states plainly: _"The Company is dealing only in Petroleum products. There are no separate activities other than the petroleum segment in the Company."_ Product lines span retail fuel, lubricants (including an export-oriented manufacturing operation at Trincomalee), bitumen, bunker/marine fuel, greases, and a newer petrochemicals line (PROPEL brand, polymer products).

**Ownership: Indian Oil Corporation Limited (India) holds 75.12%** (400,000,005 of 532,465,705 shares) — the single largest, most concentrated controlling stake of any name researched in this vault, and structurally different in kind from every Hayleys-linked file: this is a foreign multinational parent, not a local conglomerate with cross-holdings across a CSE-listed group. **Public holding 24.88%, 17,215 shareholders.** Directors hold nil shares except the Managing Director and one other director, both nil per the disclosed table (confirmed clean).

**A structurally important minority holding, not fully controlled despite its size:** LIOC owns **33⅓% of Ceylon Petroleum Storage Terminal Limited (CPSTL)**, the "Common User Facility," carried at fair value through OCI (Rs. 7,483,000k, ~16% of total non-current assets). The Ceylon Petroleum Corporation (government) owns the remaining 66⅔% and controls six of nine board seats; LIOC nominates the other three. The Annual Report states directly that **LIOC's nominated directors "do not have significant influence over decisions of CPSTL"** due to government influence and industry sensitivity — a real governance limitation over a materially-sized asset, flagged explicitly rather than assumed away.

Separately, LIOC holds a **49% stake in Trinco Petroleum Terminal (Private) Limited** (TPTL), a joint venture with CPC (51%) formed under a 50-year lease (signed 2021-22) for 61 tanks at the Upper Tank Farm, Trincomalee — infrastructure-access economics, not a trading operation in its own right.

## 7. Valuation — re-derived under the new hurdle rate

**The hurdle moved.** Per the macro dashboard's 11-Sep-2026 update, the long T-bond (2037A) **secondary-market yield now reads 11.96%**, down from the 12.6% last confirmed at the 16-Jul-2026 primary auction. This is a CBSL secondary-market quote, **not yet confirmed by a fresh primary auction** — treat this re-derivation as current-best-estimate, to be re-checked when the next long-bond auction result lands (see next-trigger).

**New neutral P/E = 1 ÷ 0.1196 ≈ 8.36×** (was 7.9×).

**Sustainable EPS: held at Rs. 22.30** — unchanged, since no new financial data entered this file this update (§§1–6 are the same facts as 04-Aug-2026). The 3-year average (26.19, 20.94, 19.84, ex-windfall, ex-crisis) stands as before.

**Floor multiple — re-derived, not mechanically scaled.** The original 5.8× floor multiple represented a **73.1% retention of the old 7.9× neutral** (i.e., a ~26.9% aggregate haircut for the five named risks — declining EPS trend, direct oil-price exposure, the CPSTL governance limitation, tax-concession dependency, extreme historical volatility — offset by exceptional balance-sheet quality). **None of those five risks, nor the offsetting balance-sheet strength, changed this update** — only the risk-free comparator moved. Holding the same 73.1% retention against the new 8.36× neutral gives a like-for-like floor multiple of **6.11×**, materially more defensible than a flat ×1.058 rescale of the old dollar figures, because it re-applies the same risk _judgment_ to the new anchor rather than just inflating the old output.

**Floor** = 22.30 × 6.11 ≈ Rs. 136.3, rounded down → **Rs. 136.00.** **Second tranche** = Floor × 0.90 ≈ **Rs. 122.50** (rounded to the nearest 0.50, consistent with this file's existing precision). **Ceiling** = 22.30 × 8.36 (new hurdle-neutral) ≈ Rs. 186.5, rounded down → **Rs. 186.00.**

## 8. Market price and verdict — the control-case finding is unchanged, the margin to the floor is not

**Last confirmed price: Rs. 141.50 (30-Jun-2026, per the Q1 FY26/27 interim's own investor-information page).** This file has no more recent primary-sourced quote — flagged plainly as homework below, not papered over.

**At 141.50 against the re-derived band: ~3.8% above the new floor (136.00), ~24.1% below the new ceiling (186.00).** This is a materially different picture from the 04-Aug read (then: ~9.7% above the old floor of 129.00, ~19.6% below the old ceiling of 176.00) — **not because anything about LIOC changed, but because the risk-free comparator LIOC is being measured against got cheaper.** Every stock in the vault got a little more attractive on this basis; LIOC is simply the one name close enough to its own floor for the shift to matter in practice, exactly as flagged when this re-anchor was first logged.

**Trailing metrics at 141.50 on the (unchanged) 22.30 sustainable EPS:** P/E ≈ 6.35×, earnings yield ≈ 15.76% — versus the new 11.96% hurdle, a margin of ~3.8 points, _wider_ than the ~3.2-point margin over the old 12.6% hurdle. The falling hurdle helps the earnings-yield read and the floor/ceiling band simultaneously, which is the correct, consistent behaviour for a mechanical rate-driven re-derivation.

**This remains the cleanest result in the ALUM→SINS→SERV→LIOC sequence** — LIOC is still the only name trading inside its own band rather than well above it, and the hurdle move doesn't touch that finding. What it changes is urgency: **LIOC is now the closest any name in this vault has come to a written floor**, close enough that a genuinely current price check is the single most value-dense piece of homework in the whole vault right now.

**Verdict: WATCHLIST. No position — no order without a current price check first.** Per this file's own knife-check discipline, if the current quote is confirmed at or below Rs. 136.00, verify no adverse disclosure before doing anything, then treat per the standing buy rules — this is not yet a mechanical signal off a 43-day-stale price.

**What would raise the ceiling:** Q2 FY26/27 confirming gross margin recovers toward FY24-26's 8-9% norm; greater clarity on the remaining tax-concession runway; a genuine down-cycle test of the 3-year "normal" EPS window. **What would lower the floor:** gross margin compression persisting into a second consecutive quarter; any adverse change to the concessionary tax regime; a material CPSTL-related governance or dividend-flow disruption. **What would move this whole re-derivation:** the next long-bond primary auction confirming, contradicting, or further moving the 11.96% secondary-market read this file is currently built on.
