## 1. What the company actually is

JXG is a **pure holding company**. The valuation report says it plainly: it "derives value from its subsidiaries" and has "no other material sources of income or business operations." Three legs:

|Leg|Listed?|Look-through stake*|What it earns from|
|---|---|---|---|
|Janashakthi Insurance PLC (JINS.N0000)|Yes|74.23%|Life insurance — long-duration, embedded-value driven|
|First Capital Holdings PLC (CFVF.N0000)|Yes|58.02%|Investment banking / **government securities trading** — profits are largely bond gains|
|Janashakthi Finance PLC (BFN.N0000, ex-Orient Finance)|Yes — but free float only ~6.5%|93.44%|NBFI — leasing, gold loans, deposits|

*Look-through stakes per the valuation report's simultaneous-equations method (cross-holdings exist); accounting effective holdings are higher (77.58% / 76.57% / 93.44%).

**Consequence:** owning JXG = owning a leveraged basket of two listed stocks plus an NBFI, minus holdco debt. Its fair value is _arithmetic_, not mystery. That cuts both ways — easy to value, and no reason for the market to ever pay a premium over the sum.

**The holdco carries real debt of its own:** company-level borrowings ~LKR 14.0 Bn + debentures ~2.2 Bn at Dec-25, costing ~1.4 Bn in finance charges per 9 months. Standalone, the holdco is loss-making before subsidiary dividends (9M FY26 standalone PBT: −1,656 Mn before equity-accounted profits). Subsidiary dividends up-streamed in the period: 3,967 Mn — roughly 2.8× holdco finance cost. The holdco is a leveraged conduit; that leverage is why a discount to SOTP is _deserved_, not an anomaly.

## 2. The anchor audit — where "16.00" came from, and what it actually says

**Found it.** The Independent Valuation Report (Annexure 7) values 100% of JXG equity via SOTP + adjusted net assets:

|Component|Absolute value (LKR Mn)|Stake|Attributable (LKR Mn)|
|---|---|---|---|
|Janashakthi Insurance|29,749.5 – 32,866.1|74.23%|22,083 – 24,397|
|First Capital Holdings|22,279.9 – 24,565.4|58.02%|12,927 – 14,253|
|Janashakthi Finance|5,636.3 – 6,229.6|93.44%|5,267 – 5,821|
|Investment property + financial assets + misc|||+5,236|
|Borrowings + debentures + misc liabilities|||−18,396|
|**Equity value**|||**27,116 – 31,310 (mid 29,202)**|
|**Per share (1.8 Bn shares)**|||**15.06 – 17.39 (mid 16.22)**|

So the friend's "16" = the banker-commissioned valuation midpoint. Not invented — but three things make 16.00 and 17.50 unusable as targets:

**(a) The per-share number is pre-dilution.** It was computed on 1.8 Bn shares. The IPO then issued 500 Mn _new_ shares at 10.00 — below the claimed fair value. Rerun the arithmetic post-IPO: (29,202 + 5,000 raised) ÷ 2,300 Mn shares = **14.87 mid; range 13.96 – 15.79**. The 16.22 died on listing day by construction. Nobody updated the friend.

**(b) 17.50 exceeds the top of even the undiluted range (17.39).** The target required JXG to trade above the most optimistic number its own paid valuer would sign. Post-dilution, 17.50 is ~11% above the top of the corrected range.

**(c) Holdcos trade at discounts to SOTP, not at par.** The valuer applied **zero holding-company discount**. On the CSE (and everywhere), leveraged holdcos routinely trade 20–40% below SOTP. A price of 17.50 would require a _negative_ discount.

**Verdict on 17.50: falsified.** Not by opinion — by the source document of the original anchor, plus share-count arithmetic.

## 3. Live SOTP — the two big legs are now market-priced

All three legs are listed, so the SOTP can be marked to market at any time. As of 13 Jul 2026 (JINS ~30.5 Bn, CFVF ~21.7 Bn, BFN ~9.0 Bn):

```
JINS   30.5 × 74.23%            = 22.6
CFVF   21.7 × 58.02%            = 12.6
BFN     9.0 × 93.44%            =  8.4
Non-core assets                  = +5.2
Holdco debt & other liabilities  = −18.4
IPO cash raised                  = +5.0
                                  ------
Live SOTP (all-market)            ≈ 35.5 Bn  →  ~15.40 / share
Live SOTP (BFN at valuer mid 5.9) ≈ 32.6 Bn  →  ~14.20 / share
```

**Why two variants:** BFN's market cap (~9.0 Bn) is ~50% above the valuer's range (5.6–6.2 Bn) — but JXG owns 93.44% of it, so the price is set by a ~6.5% free float, after a +104% 52-week run, at ~25x trailing earnings for an NBFI with 8.85% ROE. A thin-float price is information, not a fact. The conservative variant treats the BFN mark as unreliable; the all-market variant takes it at face value. Truth is likely between.

Cross-checks on the other legs: the valuer's insurance number (29.7–32.9 Bn) brackets JINS's actual market cap; the FCH number (22.3–24.6 Bn) sits just above CFVF's. **The SOTP components are honest.** The per-share packaging (pre-dilution share count, zero holdco discount) was the misleading part.

At ~11.70, JXG trades at a **~17–24% discount to live SOTP** depending on the BFN treatment — thin-to-normal by holdco standards. Not obviously cheap, not obviously expensive.

> **Maintenance rule:** live SOTP ≈ (0.742 × JINS + 0.580 × CFVF + 0.934 × BFN − 13.2) ÷ 2.3 Bn shares (mcaps in Bn). Recompute in the 15-minute daily check whenever any leg moves >5%. Treat BFN's contribution with suspicion until its float or fundamentals justify the mark.

## 5. Earnings quality — the macro linkage (this is the thesis, in both directions)

First Capital's investment-banking segment generated 11.2 Bn of the group's 21.8 Bn 9M revenue — the largest single leg. Its profits are substantially **bond trading gains**, which exist when yields _fall_ and evaporate (or invert) when yields rise or flatten. The macro file already logged it: T-bill yields rose ~200bps from the mid-2025 bottom and stabilised. Q3's group loss and CFVF's own Q3 loss (−173 Mn) are the same event viewed from two tickers.

**Translation into the five-layer system:** JXG is a _rates-cycle instrument wearing an insurance costume._

- Yields falling → FCH trading gains + JINS EV discount-rate relief → JXG earnings surge.
- Yields flat/rising → holdco earnings compress toward the insurance leg alone, while 16+ Bn of holdco debt keeps costing ~15–16%.

Secondary flags: expense inflation assumption in the JINS actuarial report revised up to 5.4% citing the IMF's post-Cyclone Ditwah outlook; JIPLC's appraisal value (25.7 Bn) is _below_ what the market currently pays for JINS (~30.5 Bn) — the market is at ~1.5× embedded value, so the insurance leg is not cheap either. No dividend declared in 9M FY26; the pre-IPO dividends (FY24–25) went to the family holding structure and prove nothing about future payout policy.

**Conflict note, for the record:** the manager to the issue, First Capital Advisory Services, is a JXG subsidiary. The group managed its own IPO. The valuation is separately authored, and its components survived the market cross-check — but this is the kind of fact the file exists to remember.
